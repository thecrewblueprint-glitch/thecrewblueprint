Before your first call

What “ready” should mean.

You know how to receive an assignment.

You identify who is directing the task, what the expected result is, where the handoff goes, and what to do if something is unclear.

You know what you do not own.

You do not treat unfamiliar equipment, restricted areas, powered machinery, electrical systems, rigging, or other controlled work as something to “figure out” on your own.

You can leave the work cleaner than you found it.

Good crews protect access paths, equipment, labels, cases, documentation, and the next department’s ability to work.

Important: these courses are educational preparation. The employer and worksite determine required PPE, training, practical evaluation, authorization, supervision, and task-specific rules. A course does not replace those responsibilities.
