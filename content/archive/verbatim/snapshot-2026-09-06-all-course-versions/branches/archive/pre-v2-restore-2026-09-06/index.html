Learn the job before the call gets loud.

The Crew Blueprint is a growing training library for people entering live-event production. It gives you practical context before the jobsite: the language crews use, the rhythm of a call, common work expectations, department boundaries, and the habits that help you arrive ready to listen and learn.

A practical starting path

This is not a promise that everyone follows one career ladder. It is a simple way to decide what to learn first.

01 · Foundation

Learn the call

Start with the vocabulary, work phases, crew behavior, basic safety awareness, and expectations shared across many entry-level calls.

02 · Field Skills

Add practical context

Build awareness around common early-career tasks, handoffs, tools, movement, organization, and when the responsible lead needs to take over.

03 · Department

Choose a direction

Explore a starter course for a department that interests you. The goal is to understand the support role—not to skip directly to technician authority.

04 · Real Call

Learn from the crew

Take that context to the jobsite. Employer rules, site conditions, equipment, crew leads, supervised practice, and real experience still govern the work.

Current Project Status

This is a growing project, not a finished catalog.

There is more research, draft curriculum, and deeper course material behind the public site. Some of it may become useful public training after it is checked against stronger evidence and the people who actually hold those responsibilities. Some of it may be reorganized or never become a standalone course at all.

The public site should describe what is useful now and be clear about what still needs review. That is more valuable than filling the homepage with release promises.

Built from the perspective of someone doing the work.

The Crew Blueprint is being built by Aaron Bowman, a live-event production worker and owner of Deadhang Labor LLC. The goal is practical preparation with clear limits—not to pretend one person or one website can replace the crews, employers, specialists, and field experience that make this industry work.
