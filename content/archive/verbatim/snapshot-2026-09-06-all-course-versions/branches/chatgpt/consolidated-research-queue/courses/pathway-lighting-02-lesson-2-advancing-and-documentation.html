Advancing, Documentation, and Shop Preparation

Advancing is the pre-production process of turning assumptions into confirmed information. Documentation turns the plan into instructions the crew can build, test, and revise. Shop preparation makes the load-in faster and safer by checking equipment before the venue clock starts.

What You'll Learn On a Real Call Beginner Mistakes Habit & Safety

What You'll Learn

  • Identify what gets confirmed during advance work.
  • Recognize the core documents used by a lighting department.
  • Explain why pre-addressing, labeling, testing, and console preparation reduce jobsite risk.
  • Understand why one controlled record of changes matters.

On a Real Call

Most of the risk in a fast load-in gets removed before anyone touches the truck.

Before a stadium build, the team may confirm truck routes, dock access, floor loading, roof capacity, approved point loads, generator needs, FOH location, cable paths, lift access, fire lanes, curfews, and inspection requirements. In the shop, fixtures may be addressed, labeled, mode-checked, firmware-checked, tested, cased by zone, and matched to paperwork — all before the venue clock starts.

The patch list is the connective tissue

It maps console identities, fixture addresses, universes, and outputs together. When it's out of date, everything downstream — troubleshooting, checkout, even the show itself — gets harder.

Beginner Mistakes

  • Waiting until site to discover missing information. Unconfirmed roof, power, door size, or cable route details can stop the build.
  • Using old paperwork after field changes. A stale patch sheet can waste hours during troubleshooting.
  • Labeling only one end of a cable. Both ends need to be traceable when the rig is built, flown, or packed.
  • Assuming RDM or network discovery replaces physical labels and inspection. Digital tools help, but they do not replace controlled paperwork.

Professional Habit & Safety Notes

Update paperwork during the build, not after memory fades

When a fixture, address, universe, cable route, or substitution changes, make sure the department lead knows and the controlled record is updated — while it's fresh, not at strike.

Safety notes: Documentation must not be treated as proof that a system is safe by itself. Rigging plans, power schedules, and point plans may require engineering, qualified inspection, venue approval, licensed personnel, or local authority review. Follow the site process before acting on technical documents.

Quick Knowledge Check

Click a question to check your answer.

Why should both ends of important cables be labeled?

It helps tracing, troubleshooting, flying, striking, and packing — though two-end labeling improves traceability, it doesn't replace the patch sheet.

Which document maps console identities, fixture addresses, universes, and outputs together?

The patch list — it connects console and physical/network control information.

A client requests a lighting change after the rig is already built. What should happen?

Escalate through the lead, assess the labor/equipment/programming/safety impact, and update the records — late revisions affect more than just the look.

Key Takeaway

Good paperwork is a safety tool, a troubleshooting tool, and a speed tool.

Think About It

  • What information would you want confirmed before loading a truck for a lighting rig?
  • Why can one wrong fixture mode cause confusion during checkout?
  • Who should be told when a field substitution changes the plot?
