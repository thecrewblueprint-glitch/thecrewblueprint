Structural and Rigging Authority Awareness

A video wall can look electrically and visually correct while still requiring separate structural, rigging, venue, engineering, or site acceptance. Course 2 teaches that boundary; it does not teach the calculations or physical methods behind those decisions.

Video Quality Does Not Prove Mechanical Acceptance

A clean image tells you about the video system. It does not establish that the support method, attachment, structure, overhead system, base, environment, or venue condition is acceptable.

Those questions belong to the people and processes responsible for structure, rigging, venue approval, engineering, equipment documentation, and site conditions. The exact authority changes with the system and production.

Recognize the Handoff

Video-side observationRequired handoff
The display or support arrangement differs from the current planStop the affected work and bring the discrepancy to the responsible lead.
A structural, overhead, support, attachment, or stability question appearsGet the qualified structural/rigging/site authority responsible for that decision.
Weather, venue, surface, or surrounding conditions changeFollow the production's current site and safety decision process; do not infer a threshold from this course.
A video technician can see the problem but does not own the structural decisionKeep the issue visible and escalate across the department boundary.

What This Course Intentionally Does Not Teach

  • Load calculations or capacity decisions.
  • Attachment, hoisting, ballast, anchoring, bracing, or support procedures.
  • Rigging-point selection or overhead connection methods.
  • Wind, weather, surface, floor, or structural thresholds.
  • Inspection signoff or final mechanical acceptance.

Those are not gaps to fill with a generic online procedure. They require the exact system, current documentation, workplace authority, and qualified review for the production in front of the crew.

Knowledge Check

Does a correct image prove the wall is mechanically accepted?

No. Video performance and mechanical acceptance are separate responsibilities.

Should Course 2 provide a universal load or weather threshold?

No. Those decisions depend on the exact system, site, documentation, and responsible qualified authority.

What should a video worker do when a structural condition conflicts with the plan?

Stop the affected work, keep the discrepancy visible, and escalate to the person responsible for the structural or rigging decision.

Publication Boundary

This owner-review lesson is structural/rigging interface awareness only. It does not authorize calculations, attachment, hoisting, anchoring, ballast, bracing, inspection, signoff, or other structural/rigging work.
