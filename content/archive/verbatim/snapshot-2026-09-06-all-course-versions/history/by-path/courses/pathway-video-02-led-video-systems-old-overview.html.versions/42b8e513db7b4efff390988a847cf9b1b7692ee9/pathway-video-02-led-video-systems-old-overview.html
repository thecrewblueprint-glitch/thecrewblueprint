Large-Scale LED Video Systems

Course 1 taught you how to support an LED video wall build as a ground hand. This course goes deeper: how large-scale direct-view LED systems are actually designed, built, powered, rigged, and operated — across arenas, stadiums, corporate events, and outdoor festivals.

Mental model: "What system am I working inside, how does it work, and where does my work fit into it?"

Start Lesson 1 Back to Course 1

What this course helps you understand

This is systems-understanding training for stagehands and early-career live event workers who want to move beyond task execution into comprehending how video systems work and how their work fits into the larger production. Understanding how a system works is valuable. It does not provide independent operation, advanced troubleshooting, lead qualification, or authorization of any kind.

What this course does NOT do: It does not authorize or qualify you to independently operate systems, troubleshoot problems, configure equipment, modify procedures, or perform restricted rigging, electrical, weather-protection, or engineered work. Those stay with your employer, the venue, and qualified, authorized personnel. This course does not replace employer training, manufacturer instructions, union apprenticeships, equipment certifications, or OSHA/ANSI/ESTA/NFPA/NEC requirements.

Glossary

Terms used throughout this course. Come back to this list any time.

LED cabinet / tile / panelModular enclosure containing LED modules, power supplies, receiving electronics, connectors, and locks.
LED moduleSmaller serviceable pixel assembly within a cabinet, may be replaced from front or rear.
Pixel pitchCenter-to-center spacing between pixels, usually in millimeters — helps determine pixel density and viewing distance.
Native resolutionTotal physical pixel count of the assembled wall; defines the raster the processor must map.
NitUnit of luminance equal to one candela per square meter, used to discuss display brightness.
Refresh rateHow often LED driver electronics refresh the image; affects flicker and camera behavior.
Scan rateMultiplexing method used to drive LEDs; can affect brightness, efficiency, and camera artifacts.
LED processor / sending deviceDevice that scales, maps, synchronizes, processes, and distributes LED data — the bridge between sources and receiving cards.
Receiving cardElectronics in the cabinet that receive mapped LED data and drive outputs; must match configuration and hardware.
MappingAssignment of processor outputs and data paths to physical cabinets; bad mapping causes missing, reversed, or displaced imagery.
EDIDDisplay-identification data communicated to a video source; can affect resolution, refresh rate, and compatibility.
GenlockCommon timing reference for synchronized video devices, helping avoid tearing or timing mismatch.
Frame synchronizationBuffering/timing conversion to align asynchronous sources; can stabilize switching but may add latency.
IP ratingIngress-protection rating against solids and water — not a full weather approval or wind rating.
RedundancyBackup signal, processor, data, or power path — useful only when configured, current, and tested.
Ground supportStructure supported from the ground with towers, frames, bases, ballast, or outriggers.
Flown wallLED array suspended from overhead rigging points or temporary roof systems, requiring qualified rigging personnel.
Dead loadPermanent gravitational weight of the system and support hardware — includes more than panel weight.
Dynamic loadLoad created by motion, stopping, wind, impact, or oscillation — can exceed the static weight.
Fiber hygieneCleaning, inspecting, protecting, and handling fiber correctly — dirty or damaged fiber can cause signal failures.

Sources & Standards

This course was built from a structured research packet that names the standards bodies and regulations its safety-sensitive claims are grounded in. Standards, codes, and manufacturer documentation change — verify the current adopted edition and local applicability before relying on any of these for an actual job. None of this replaces engineered plans, manufacturer instructions, or direction from qualified, authorized personnel.

ANSI E1.21Minimum design and performance requirements for temporary ground-supported outdoor entertainment structures. ESTA Technical Standards Program
ANSI E1.56Permanent rigging support points attached to facility structures. ESTA Technical Standards Program
ANSI E1.6 familyPowered-hoist systems — selection, inspection, maintenance, controls, and use. ESTA Technical Standards Program
ANSI ES1.7Weather-preparedness strategies for live events and temporary event structures. ESTA Technical Standards Program
ANSI E1.58Temporary outdoor use of portable stage and studio electrical equipment. ESTA Technical Standards Program
OSHA 29 CFR 1926.404Wiring design, grounding, overcurrent protection, generators, and GFCI in covered construction settings. osha.gov
NFPA 70 / NECProvisions for temporary wiring, cords, overcurrent protection, grounding, generators, and stage/studio equipment. nfpa.org
AVIXA StandardsAudiovisual system performance, documentation, measurement, verification, and commissioning. avixa.org

This course does not cite specific standard clauses inline — it teaches the underlying concepts (integrated-systems thinking, load paths, power planning, weather awareness) at an awareness level, and points here to where the enforceable detail actually lives.
