Module 1.3

Crew, Direction & Value

Understand who leads on a jobsite, what makes direction clear, and what value means to the people paying for the work.

What you'll learn

By the end of this lesson, you'll be able to:

  • Identify the chain of command on a production call
  • Recognize how direction gets communicated and why clarity matters
  • Understand what "value" means in production context
  • Spot situations where you should ask rather than guess
On a live event: The person giving you direction is usually not the person who hired the labor company. They're the production manager or department lead for that specific event. Your job is to be clear on who is leading you and what they're asking for.

The Structure of Leadership

Every production call has a clear hierarchy, even if it's not written down. Understanding who makes decisions at each level helps you know who to report to, who to ask questions of, and who makes final calls.

Event Organizer / Venue Manager

Controls the space itself. Makes decisions about when work starts/stops, what goes where, and what can move through which doors.

Production Manager

Coordinates all departments and the overall flow. Usually the person who communicates timing and priorities to the crew leaders.

Department Lead (Lighting, Audio, Video, Staging, etc.)

Makes decisions specific to that department. You'll take direction from them about how equipment moves, where it goes, and what you can or cannot touch.

Why this matters: When you know who's leading you and what their role is, you can ask the right person when you need clarification. This prevents miscommunication, rework, and accidents. It also makes you valuable because you're clear and you can follow direction.
Watch for: Situations where two people give you conflicting direction. When that happens, ask: "Who is leading this task?" That's your signal to clarify the chain of command before you move.
Key Takeaway

Know who is leading you. Follow their direction. Ask before you guess. This simple habit makes you reliable and keeps the work safe.

Check Your Understanding

Scenario: Two people are giving you different instructions

You're moving a lighting truss. The department lead says to move it stage left. A stagehand next to you says to move it stage right. What do you do?

Answer:

Stop. Ask: "Who is leading this task?" Wait for clarification before moving. The department lead has authority over department work. You need clear direction.

  • OSHA 1910.176 - handling materials (command structure)
  • Production management best practices from 50+ arena and venue productions
