Audio Pathway · Course 1

Supporting an Audio Load-In

The Audio pathway starts here: move it, stage it, carry it, run it, and connect it only when directed. Reconfiguring, tuning, or troubleshooting stays with the audio department — this course is about everything you genuinely can do.

What You Can Help With What Stays Off-Limits Mistakes & Communication

What You Can Help With, Under Direction

Real ground-hand support includes some of the most physical work on an audio call.

You can move and stage speaker cabinets, subs, and monitor wedges using their actual handles — not grilles, rigging pins, or connectors — and use a team carry when the size or weight calls for it. You can help build a line array by steadying a cabinet or passing hardware while the audio technician makes the actual connection; locking hardware and rigging angles are manufacturer-specific, so don't assume one system works like another. Running and dressing cable along the path you're given is normal ground-hand work too.

What Stays Off-Limits Without Direction

The clearest rule in this course: never strike a cable because it looks done.

A cable that looks unused might not be

It might still be patched, powered, or needed for testing. Wait for the audio lead to clear it before you disconnect, coil, or move it — and if a connector doesn't seat normally, stop and ask instead of forcing it.

Console and mixer settings, system tuning/EQ, RF and wireless coordination, and amplifier settings all stay with the audio department. Treat a microphone that's already been placed as deliberate — move it only if you're told to.

Common Mistakes and Talking to the Audio Lead

A few habits matter more than knowing anything technical about how the system works.

Common problems: pulling on the cable instead of the connector, forcing a connector that doesn't align, and mishandling speaker cabinets by grabbing rigging pins or connectors instead of the designed handling points. When in doubt, short confirmation beats assumed terminology — "Which case?", "Is this cable cleared to strike?", "Do you want this connected or just placed?"

Under a suspended line array, stay outside the fall zone and follow the audio/rigging lead's exclusion zone. Hearing protection during testing and soundcheck isn't just for the audio crew — follow whatever the site requires.

Quick Knowledge Check

Click a question to check your answer.

A cable looks unused during a PA build. Can you strike it?

Not until the audio lead clears it — it may still be patched, powered, or needed for testing.

You're carrying a speaker cabinet. What should you grab?

The cabinet's actual handle — not grilles, rigging pins, or connectors.

A microphone is already placed on stage. Can you move it if it looks like it's in your way?

No — treat a placed microphone as deliberate and move it only if you're told to.

A connector doesn't seat normally. What do you do?

Stop and ask — don't force it. Forcing a connector can damage contacts, shells, or locking mechanisms.

Who decides system EQ, RF coordination, and amplifier settings?

The audio department — these stay off-limits for a ground hand regardless of how confident you feel.

Key Takeaways

  • Move it, stage it, carry it, run it — connect it only when directed. Reconfiguring, tuning, or troubleshooting stays with audio.
  • Never strike a cable that hasn't been cleared, and treat a placed microphone as deliberate.
  • Console settings, system tuning, RF coordination, and amplifier settings are off-limits without direction.
  • Use short confirmation phrases rather than assumed terminology — ask, don't guess.
