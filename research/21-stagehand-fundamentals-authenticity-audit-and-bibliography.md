## Claims Still Requiring Qualification

The following course content should include explicit qualifications or disclaimers when taught:

### 1. Communication Phrases
**Current claim:** Uses phrases like "Copy," "Standing by," "Heard," etc.  
**Qualified teaching:** These are common examples used on some sets and by some departments. Always ask your lead or crew what phrases they use, as each venue and department may have different conventions.

### 2. First 30 Days Framework
**Current claim:** Module 10 presents Week 1-4 development sequence  
**Qualified teaching:** This is a Crew Blueprint suggested professional-development framework, NOT a universal industry standard or certification program. Onboarding timelines vary by employer, venue, and union.

### 3. Load-Out Risk Comparison
**Current claim:** "Load-out often carries higher risk than load-in"  
**Qualified teaching:** Fatigue, low visibility, time pressure, and dense movement do create real hazards in load-out. However, load-in can be equally hazardous if it involves heavy overhead work, time pressure, or complex rigging. Hazard varies by call, not phase.

### 4. Show Blacks
**Current claim:** Module 3 covers "Show Blacks" as workwear  
**Qualified teaching:** Show blacks are low-visibility work clothing worn during performances when crew may be visible during blackout/darkness and need to remain visually unobtrusive. They are NOT PPE and are NOT necessarily required for load-in/load-out. Exact requirements depend on the performance and venue.

### 5. Phone Use Rules
**Current claim:** Generic expectation of availability  
**Qualified teaching:** Do not use your phone in ways that distract you from safety, instructions, or radio awareness. Exact phone-use policy varies by employer/venue — ask about it at check-in.

## Verified Gaps (Unsupported by Industry Standards)

The following claims in Stagehand Fundamentals should NOT be presented as universal industry standards:

### 1. One Universal Set of Hand Signals
**Status:** Unsupported  
**Course reference:** Any section implying crane/rigging hand signals are universal to all stagecraft  
**Reality:** Formal crane/derrick signaling is OSHA-regulated for rigging operations, not generalized to all stagehand roles.  
**Recommendation:** Remove or replace with: "Learn the hand signals and terminology your department uses; they may differ from other venues."

### 2. Exact Tool List as Universal Requirement
**Status:** Partially sourced (examples exist)  
**Course reference:** Module 8 / Module 10 tool-list expectations  
**Reality:** IATSE Locals publish tool lists, but they vary by local and job. Individual employers set requirements.  
**Recommendation:** Teach tools as "common examples" not "required for all general stagehands."

### 3. Show-Call Assignment Guarantee for New Hands
**Status:** Contradicted by union rules  
**Course reference:** If present, any statement that new hands typically get show calls  
**Reality:** IATSE Locals 101, 193 explicitly state that show-call assignments follow production need and worker ability.  
**Recommendation:** Already corrected in Module 1 — verify current wording.
