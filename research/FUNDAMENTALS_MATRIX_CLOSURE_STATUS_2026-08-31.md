## Important audit outcomes

The first-pass matrix supports keeping most of the current Fundamentals course, but it identifies specific learner-facing wording that should be revised during the later enhancement phase.

### Revision priority — safety / liability

1. **Module 3.3 — phase transitions / PPE**
   - Current coaching implies that a production clothing change means the hazard assessment changed.
   - Correct model: production appearance requirements and workplace PPE/hazard requirements are separate inputs and can change independently.

2. **Module 6.1 — trailer unload sequence**
   - Current text turns one possible unload sequence into a universal physics rule and contains contradictory front/rear wording.
   - Keep only the transferable boundary: follow the responsible loader/cargo process and do not bypass restraints or load-control decisions independently.

3. **Module 6.3 — ramp/body-position/team handling**
   - Current lesson contains prescriptive physical movement/body-position language that is too universal for a general online Foundation course.
   - Later revision should retain hazard recognition, coordination, stop/escalate and employer-assigned-method concepts while moving specific techniques behind appropriate practitioner/equipment review.

4. **Module 7.2 — generic equipment inspection**
   - Keep obvious-defect recognition/reporting.
   - Remove the absolute statement that a replacement always exists and avoid generic functional-test instructions for unfamiliar equipment.

5. **Module 7.3 — team handling commands**
   - Keep the principle of one clearly understood current-call method and stopping on conflicting direction.
   - Treat exact command vocabulary, caller placement and countdown sequences as examples/site-specific practice, not universal procedure.

### Revision priority — career accuracy

6. **Module 10.2 — specialization after “5–10 calls”**
   - Reliability/professionalism as recurring callback factors is supportable.
   - A fixed number of calls before specialization/better assignments is not established as a transferable industry threshold.

7. **Module 10.3 — specialization begins when “invited”**
   - Keep the boundary against self-authorizing specialist work.
   - Legitimate progression can also occur through hiring, training, apprenticeship/referral, supervised assignment and external credential pathways.

## Qualifications that should remain

- Show blacks are not PPE by default.
- PPE is task/site/hazard controlled, not one universal stagehand kit.
- High-visibility clothing is not sourced to OSHA 1910.135; that section is head protection.
- Exact radio/confirmation phrases are examples, not universal commands.
- Crew Blueprint's NCCE model is internal instructional architecture.
- Crew Blueprint's early-call development sequence is internal instructional architecture, not a universal probation system.
- Load-out should not be described as universally more dangerous than load-in.
- Task completion, department release, call release and timekeeping/sign-out are separate states.
- Physical support, conceptual knowledge, observed practice, employer authorization, specialist qualification and licensure/certification remain separate states.

## Architectural implication

This pass supports the evidence-first strategy: the current page structure does **not** need to dictate the eventual curriculum architecture. The normalized claims, competencies, assessments, sources and review states can be reused whether Fundamentals remains one course, becomes part of a larger Stagehand Core system, or is reorganized after full-site synthesis.
