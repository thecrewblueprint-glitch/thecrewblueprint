# MDQ-110–116 — Sector and Context Transfer Evidence

**Prepared:** 2026-08-31  
**Status:** Cross-domain sector synthesis; suitable for competency-transfer mapping and initial context overlays  
**Depends on:** Stagehand evidence, department packets, production/coordination evidence, IATSE touring/local-condition evidence, ESA event-safety sources  
**Purpose:** Separate transferable production competencies from sector-specific workflow, titles, authority, environment and client/show constraints.

---

## 1. Core finding

A worker does not enter a completely new industry when moving from arena concerts to theatre, corporate AV, venue work, worship, broadcast or outdoor festivals. Many competencies transfer, but **workflow, titles, ownership, show rhythm, permanent-vs-temporary systems and authority rules can change substantially**.

The curriculum should therefore model:

**transferable competency + sector overlay + employer/venue/local authority**

rather than duplicate every course by sector or pretend one sector's conventions are universal.

---

# MDQ-110 — Touring and festival production workflow

## Defining characteristics

- advance/specification before arrival;
- traveling package plus local/house labor;
- compressed load-in/show/strike cycle;
- trucking/case/logistics dependence;
- tour/house/local authority handoffs;
- guest packages and substitutions;
- changeovers / festival shared systems;
- curfew/schedule pressure;
- weather/outdoor exposure where applicable;
- repeated venues with changing local conditions.

## Current IATSE evidence

IATSE touring guidance documents department labor through Yellow/White Cards and explicitly states that local venue conditions can change responsibilities such as unloading, house-head roles, rigging staffing and other work practices.

## Transferable competencies

- check-in/chain of command;
- material/case/cable workflow;
- department ownership;
- advance/document literacy;
- rapid handoff/changeover;
- report-back/reassignment;
- show/strike discipline.

## Sector-specific learning

- tour vs house vs local crew;
- festival patch/package ownership;
- compressed changeovers;
- touring paperwork;
- trucking/overnight transitions;
- local-condition variation.

**State:** `draftable` as a sector overlay.

- volunteer + professional hybrid teams;
- audio/lighting/video/broadcast integration;
- streaming/recording;
- training/onboarding repeated internally;

- cable/patch/support workflow;
- console/show-file concepts;

- cameras/streaming;

## Sector-specific learning

- recurring weekly workflow;
- installed broadcast/streaming integration;

# MDQ-115 — Film / TV / broadcast production interface

## Transfer limits matter

Live-event production skills transfer, but role jurisdiction and terminology can change substantially.

## Shared competencies

- lighting fixtures/control/network concepts;
- video/camera/signal/sync;
- audio/RF/comms;
- temporary-production logistics;
- cases/cables/material movement;
- power-system awareness;
- documentation/change control.

## Important differences

- grip vs electric department distinctions;
- camera/DIT/video-assist/broadcast-engineering roles;
- production sound vs reinforcement sound;
- take-based vs continuous-live workflow;
- camera exposure/color/flicker priorities;
- union/local jurisdiction differences;
- virtual-production/LED-volume workflows;
- production communication and chain of command.

## Curriculum rule

Teach transferability **and non-transferability**. A live-event job title does not automatically map to an equivalent film/broadcast authority.

**State:** `researched` orientation; deeper film/broadcast specialty research remains.

# MDQ-116 — Outdoor / festival / weather operations

## Cross-domain hazards

Outdoor work can change nearly every department through:

- wind;
- lightning/severe weather;
- heat/cold;
- rain/water;
- mud/uneven surfaces;
- public/vehicle routes;
- temporary structures;
- outdoor electrical systems;
- cable protection;
- crowd/egress needs;
- changing site conditions.

## Current event-safety evidence

Event Safety Alliance continues to treat weather preparedness as a major live-event safety system; its 2026 Safer Events programming focused on weather decision making, structures/displays and the ANSI ES1.7 weather-planning standard.

## Transferable learner outcome

- recognize changing conditions;
- know who owns weather/site decisions;
- maintain communication;
- understand that wind/lightning/structure/power thresholds are not invented by individual crew;
- follow hold/evacuation/stop-work directions;
- report new conditions;
- protect public routes/work areas within assignment;
- understand that outdoor-rated/qualified systems and responsible personnel control technical decisions.

Do not teach a general worker to independently decide structural wind limits, electrical weather configurations or severe-weather clearance criteria.

**State:** `draftable` for general awareness/context; technical thresholds remain specialist/site-specific.

# 10. Architecture implication

Do not create seven duplicate full curriculums.

The evidence favors:

1. **canonical transferable competencies**;
2. **sector-context nodes/overlays**;
3. exact sector-specific sources when the workflow materially changes;
4. role/title aliases per sector;
5. authority differences recorded separately;
6. learner ability to see “this skill transfers here, but this responsibility does not.”

This model will later support the everything-map/career-transit visualization much better than a siloed course catalog.
