# 30 — Electrics Lead: Power Authority & Code Compliance

**Research Queue ID:** CB-007  
**Status:** Complete research packet  
**Research date:** 2026-08-17  
**Depends on:** CB-001 / Package 24  
**Purpose:** Define the lead-tier responsibility model for entertainment electrics while preserving the distinction between educational content, employer qualification, ETCP credentials, code compliance, and licensed electrical work.

---

## Decision question

**What should an electrics lead be responsible for, and where does that authority stop?**

## Bottom-line finding

ETCP’s current certification structure provides a unusually clear answer.

The **Entertainment Electrician (EE)** credential covers broader entertainment-electrical work and is intended for managers, supervisors, and technicians. The **Portable Power Distribution Technician (PPDT)** credential covers a narrower subset focused on portable distribution, often under the supervision of an Entertainment Electrician.

Source: ETCP — Scope of Work for Entertainment Electrician and Portable Power Distribution Technician  
https://etcp.esta.org/certify/scopeofwork.html

That supports a Crew Blueprint model in which the electrics lead is accountable for:

**power planning within qualified scope → equipment/system suitability → crew assignment → installation oversight → verification/testing → code/standard awareness → fault escalation → accurate readiness handoff**.

But title alone does not create legal or technical authority. Local law, venue rules, employer policy, licensure requirements, ETCP credentials, equipment listings, and the actual task determine who may perform or approve electrical work.

---

# 1. External competency benchmark

ETCP’s Entertainment Electrician examination is based on a formal job analysis. The published scope includes electrical theory, measurement, equipment installation, portable power distribution, control/network systems, total power requirements, drawings, equipment selection, safety procedures, code/standards compliance, maintenance, and troubleshooting.

Source: ETCP — Entertainment Electrician Examination Content  
https://etcp.esta.org/certify/examination_electrical.html

The PPDT scope is narrower but still includes substantial responsibility for portable distribution design, assembly, power requirements, source suitability, cable-management systems, plans, electrical measurements, and compliance with relevant safety requirements.

Sources:

https://etcp.esta.org/certify/scopeofwork.html  
https://etcp.esta.org/certify/examination_ppdt.html

This establishes that “electrics lead” cannot responsibly mean only “person who knows where the feeder goes.” The role requires systems-level knowledge and supervisory judgment.

---

# 2. What the lead should own

## 2.1 Scope and power-plan comprehension

The lead should understand the approved power plan sufficiently to coordinate execution and detect missing/conflicting information.

That includes conceptual awareness of:

- source and distribution hierarchy;
- intended loads and destinations;
- portable distribution equipment;
- required cable management;
- control/data interfaces where applicable;
- environmental conditions;
- venue/source limitations;
- which decisions require an EE, PPDT, licensed electrician, venue electrician, engineer, or authority having jurisdiction.

## 2.2 Crew assignment by qualification

Electrical work is not an appropriate area for casual delegation.

A lead should differentiate:

- general stagehand support;
- trained electrics technicians;
- portable-power specialists;
- ETCP-certified personnel;
- licensed/venue electrical authority where required.

The key supervisory skill is knowing **who is actually authorized and competent for the task**.

## 2.3 Verification and testing responsibility

ETCP’s scope includes electrical measurements, system setup, compliance, and troubleshooting. Therefore a lead-tier curriculum should require learners to understand the verification process at a management level:

- system matches the approved plan;
- equipment is appropriate for its assigned use;
- required inspections/tests are completed by qualified personnel;
- discrepancies are resolved before energization/use;
- unsafe or uncertain conditions are isolated and escalated;
- the system’s readiness status is communicated accurately.

Crew Blueprint should not provide novice step-by-step live-power procedures.

## 2.4 Code and standards literacy

The lead needs to understand that entertainment electrical work exists inside a formal code environment.

ETCP states that EE and PPDT work covers portable equipment within NEC Articles 518, 520, 525, and 530 and corresponding Canadian Electrical Code sections. ETCP also expects understanding of NFPA 70E PPE requirements and compliance with applicable codes, standards, and laws.

The course should teach **how to identify the applicable authority and verify compliance**, not attempt to make a short online module a substitute for the NEC, NFPA 70E training, local licensing, or employer qualification.

---

# 3. Authority boundaries

An electrics lead should not be taught to assume authority to:

- perform permanent building wiring merely because they lead show electrics;
- work energized equipment outside applicable qualification/procedure;
- bypass protective devices;
- defeat grounding/bonding requirements;
- substitute unlisted or inappropriate distribution components;
- exceed equipment ratings;
- modify venue electrical infrastructure without venue/qualified authority;
- override local code or AHJ requirements;
- treat ETCP certification as an electrical contractor license;
- treat Crew Blueprint completion as ETCP certification or legal qualification.

ETCP explicitly distinguishes portable entertainment distribution scope from permanent wiring and broader tasks outside its certifications.

# 4. Recommended CB-007 curriculum architecture

## Module A — Authority and electrical responsibility map

- lead versus technician;
- EE versus PPDT scope;
- venue electrician/licensed electrician/AHJ interfaces;
- employer authorization;
- stop/escalation responsibility.

## Module B — Power-plan and system literacy

- reading system intent at a conceptual level;
- identifying sources, distribution layers, destinations, and constraints;
- recognizing missing/conflicting information;
- understanding that load planning requires qualified calculation and documentation.

## Module C — Crew and installation oversight

- qualification matching;
- work-zone coordination;
- cable-management and access interfaces;
- interdepartmental dependencies;
- preventing unauthorized changes.

## Module D — Verification, compliance, and handoff

- required checks completed;
- equipment/system documentation available;
- discrepancies resolved;
- code/venue requirements confirmed by responsible authority;
- readiness communicated accurately.

## Module E — Fault/change/escalation scenarios

- unexpected source limitation;
- damaged equipment;
- conflicting power request;
- weather/water exposure concern;
- last-minute load addition;
- production pressure to energize before verification.

---

# 5. Assessment model

Use decision scenarios, not hazardous practical procedures.

The learner should be able to determine:

- whether the request is within their authority;
- which qualified role must be involved;
- what documentation or verification is missing;
- whether the system can remain in service;
- when work must stop;
- how to communicate a technically accurate status.

---

# 6. Credential relationship

ETCP states that its certifications target experienced technicians and use work-experience-based eligibility systems. As of 2026, Entertainment Electrician applicants need 30 eligibility points; PPDT applicants need 20.

Sources:

https://etcp.esta.org/certify/certify_electrical.html  
https://etcp.esta.org/certify/certify_ppdt.html

This reinforces a critical Crew Blueprint boundary:

> **Lead-tier online education can organize knowledge and judgment, but cannot create the practical experience or legal/employer qualification required for electrical authority.**

## Curriculum decision

**Teach the electrics lead as the accountable coordinator and verifier of entertainment power work within demonstrated qualification—not as a title that automatically grants electrical authority.**
