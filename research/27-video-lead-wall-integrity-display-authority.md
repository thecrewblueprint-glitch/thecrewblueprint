# 27 — Video Lead: Wall Integrity & Display Authority

**Research Queue ID:** CB-004  
**Status:** Complete research packet  
**Research date:** 2026-08-17  
**Depends on:** Package 24 / CB-001  
**Purpose:** Define the leadership transition from LED/video technician to video lead/crew chief, including mechanical wall integrity, signal/control integrity, display quality, troubleshooting, crew management, and specialist escalation.

---

## Bottom-line finding

PRG’s current Video Technician–LED progression gives unusually direct evidence of the transition. Early technicians work under instruction and understand rig/de-rig, flown/ground-stack solutions, video signal, system control, and equipment limitations. At higher experience levels PRG adds **fault finding, understanding LED plots and product capabilities, small-show crew chief responsibility, and management of 2–6 crew**; more senior levels require broader crew-chief experience and larger-team management.

The correct Crew Blueprint model is therefore:

**product/system knowledge + wall build integrity + signal/control understanding + fault finding + crew direction + visual/readiness verification + escalation**.

The Video Lead is accountable for the video system being mechanically correct within the assigned installation procedure, logically configured, visually acceptable to the production standard, and ready for show handoff. That does not make the lead the structural engineer, rigger, power authority, content designer, broadcast director, or every specialist in the video chain.

---

# 1. The video system has multiple integrity layers

Teach the lead to reason across at least five layers:

## 1.1 Mechanical/display surface

- correct product/panel type;
- correct orientation;
- correct locks/connections according to the product procedure;
- alignment/seams;
- ground-stack or flown configuration interfaces;
- serviceable modules/components;
- physical damage.

## 1.2 Power

- approved power distribution to panels/processors;
- connector/branch integrity;
- power status;
- escalation of electrical faults to the appropriate authority.

## 1.3 Data/control

- data path between controller/processor and receiving hardware;
- cabinet/module mapping;
- network/control connectivity;
- processor configuration.

## 1.4 Video signal

- source;
- switcher/media server/playback;
- processor;
- scaling/routing;
- display destination.

## 1.5 Image state

- correct content;
- correct mapping;
- brightness/color consistency;
- dead/incorrect modules or panels;
- calibration state;
- acceptable visual output.

A lead-level diagnosis should first identify **which integrity layer failed**.

---

# 2. Product-specific mechanical authority

ROE Visual’s current documentation demonstrates why LED-wall leadership cannot be taught as one universal locking/repair procedure.

ROE states that its panel locks are factory adjusted and should not be field-adjusted without consultation. Its product families use different installation/service features, and its FAQ repeatedly directs users to product/control-system-specific instructions where behavior differs.

Therefore teach:

> **The lead owns compliance with the current product’s approved procedure, not the invention of a generic LED-wall method.**

The lead should verify that the crew knows the actual panel family, orientation, locking method, hanging/stacking components, service boundaries, and stop conditions before work begins.

---

# 3. Crew deployment

Following Package 24, the Video Lead should divide work according to competence:

- unload/cart staging;
- panel handling;
- hanging/stacking under the applicable system procedure;
- locks/connections;
- power/data cabling;
- processor/control setup;
- mapping/configuration;
- module/service work;
- visual QC;
- strike/repack.

A worker capable of carrying and locking panels under direction is not automatically qualified to alter receiving-card configuration, processor settings, firmware, calibration, or structural support.

PRG’s progression directly supports this staged model: early LED technicians work under instruction; more advanced technicians add system-control understanding, signal knowledge, fault finding, plot knowledge, and eventually crew-chief responsibility.

---

# 4. Wall-integrity verification

The lead should coordinate a structured build verification before treating the wall as complete.

A Crew Blueprint framework:

1. **Product/configuration** — correct panel and accessories for the plan.
2. **Mechanical** — connections/locks/alignment follow product procedure.
3. **Support interface** — flown/ground-stack interface matches the approved plan; structural/rigging questions go to the responsible authority.
4. **Power/data** — routing and connections complete.
5. **Control/mapping** — processor/controller sees the intended system.
6. **Image test** — known test content verifies mapping, continuity, color/brightness anomalies, and obvious failures.
7. **Service discrepancies** — damaged/failed components isolated and handled through approved procedure.
8. **Final handoff** — unresolved limitations communicated before show readiness is declared.

This is a curriculum framework, not a manufacturer checklist.

---

# 5. Calibration authority

Calibration is a strong example of where lead awareness and specialist competence must be separated.

ROE Visual explains that calibration data for many of its panels is stored in the LED modules and can be re-read by the receiver card after a module change/power cycle, while also warning that behavior varies by panel/control system.

NovaStar’s current NCE calibration certification goes much further: its professional calibration credential requires prior Level 1 certification and relevant experience, and covers camera-based brightness/color calibration, multiple calibration workflows, and troubleshooting. Higher calibration certification expects still more experience and advanced calibration capability.

Therefore:

### Video Lead should know

- what calibration is;
- how calibration mismatch presents visually;
- that module/card replacement can affect image state;
- the approved recovery procedure for the actual system;
- when to call the calibration/control specialist.

### Do not assume every Video Lead can

- independently perform camera-based screen calibration;
- alter advanced calibration parameters;
- recalibrate irregular/complex screens;
- update firmware or receiver-card configuration without system-specific authority.

---

# 6. Firmware and configuration change control

ROE explicitly cautions against firmware updates without prior support consultation for applicable systems. That supports a broader lead rule:

**Do not use a live show system as an experimentation platform.**

Teach:

- preserve known-good configuration;
- record/backup configuration where the system supports it;
- avoid firmware updates during production unless approved and necessary;
- make one controlled change at a time during troubleshooting;
- document temporary workarounds;
- restore test changes;
- escalate when the fix requires manufacturer-level or advanced control expertise.

---

# 7. Troubleshooting model

A lead should classify symptoms before assigning technicians.

### Mechanical symptom

Examples: seam/alignment problem, lock problem, damaged frame/module.

### Power symptom

Examples: one cabinet dark with no power indication, branch/group outage.

### Data/control symptom

Examples: cabinets powered but not receiving expected control/data.

### Mapping/configuration symptom

Examples: image present in wrong location/order.

### Source/signal symptom

Examples: wall healthy but expected source not reaching processor.

### Image/calibration symptom

Examples: color/brightness mismatch after service.

The lead should route the problem to the correct owner rather than replacing random components.

---

# 8. Structural and rigging boundary

PRG’s LED technician model includes understanding flown rigging attachments and ground-stack solutions, but that does not erase rigging/structural authority boundaries.

ROE’s own FAQ notes that ballast requirements depend on screen configuration and provides system-specific calculation tools. It also addresses wind-loading and hanging-bar questions as configuration-dependent engineering/product issues.

Crew Blueprint should teach Video Leads to understand the approved support solution and verify that video work matches it, while escalating:

- ballast changes;
- support geometry changes;
- omitted hanging/support components;
- unapproved wall-size/height changes;
- rigging-point changes;
- wind/load concerns.

The lead should not independently improvise structural fixes because the video schedule is late.

---

# 9. Display-quality authority

The Video Lead should be capable of organizing objective QC:

- test pattern/content;
- correct mapping;
- full-canvas continuity;
- panel/module anomalies;
- seam/alignment issues;
- brightness/color inconsistencies;
- processor/source path verification;
- documented acceptance or escalation.

Creative content approval remains with the production’s content/video/design authority. The Video Lead owns **technical display integrity**, not necessarily creative approval of what the screen should show.

---

# 10. Recommended terminal capability

A learner completing Video Lead training should be able to:

> Plan and direct an LED/video crew; interpret the relevant wall/system plan; protect product-specific installation procedures; reason across mechanical, power, data/control, video-signal, mapping, and image-quality layers; assign fault finding intelligently; verify wall/display readiness; manage controlled configuration changes; and escalate structural, electrical, advanced calibration, firmware, or creative decisions to the correct authority.

Course completion does not confer rigging qualification, engineering authority, electrical qualification, NovaStar certification, manufacturer service authorization, or employer appointment as Video Crew Chief.

## Curriculum decision

**Make “integrity layers” the central Video Lead mental model.** A wall can be mechanically correct and electronically wrong, powered and unmapped, mapped but visually uncalibrated, or visually correct while structurally outside the approved support plan. The lead’s job is to see those layers as one production system, direct the right people, verify readiness, and escalate outside-scope decisions.
