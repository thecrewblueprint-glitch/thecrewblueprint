# MDQ-061–066 — Rigging Body of Knowledge, Roles, Qualification and Authority Boundaries

**Prepared:** 2026-08-31  
**Status:** Source-grounded role/body-of-knowledge packet; awareness and career architecture only for general learners  
**Domain:** `D-RIG` Rigging  
**Depends on:** MDQ-000–003, MDQ-060 role architecture, existing Lead Rigging evidence, ETCP/ESTA/OSHA source families  
**Purpose:** Map what belongs in the rigging knowledge universe without creating a self-study path to overhead-rigging, climbing, hoist-operation, load-calculation, rescue, or other controlled work.

---

## 1. Controlling boundary

Rigging is a safety-critical specialist field. Crew Blueprint should explain:

- what rigging roles exist;
- what non-riggers need to recognize;
- how qualified riggers communicate with other departments;
- the categories of systems/hardware/documents they work with;
- why ratings, inspection, load paths, venue structure, fall protection and rescue matter;
- where external employer/union/venue/credential authority begins;
- how a worker can responsibly pursue the career path.

Crew Blueprint should **not** provide a general learner with operational overhead-rigging steps, climbing methods, hoist-operation procedures, rigging calculations, rescue procedures, or a claim of qualification.

ETCP itself targets experienced technicians. Current eligibility requires **30 points** for either Arena or Theatre rigging certification, and the certification examinations are intended to validate experienced working riggers rather than train beginners.

# MDQ-061 — Ground-rigging awareness for non-riggers

## Appropriate general-stagehand knowledge

A non-rigger may need to recognize, without independently configuring:

- truss and suspended-system components;
- chain-hoist/motor presence;
- lifting/rigging hardware categories;
- temporary and permanent rigging points;
- floor-marking / point-layout activity;
- exclusion/drop zones;
- controlled overhead movement;
- damaged/unidentified gear as a stop/report condition;
- the responsible rigger/house/tour chain of command.

## Transferable behavior

General curriculum can teach:

- do not improvise overhead attachments;
- do not assume hardware is interchangeable because it looks similar;
- do not enter controlled overhead/work-at-height areas without direction;
- preserve labels/identification and report missing or damaged markings;
- stop and ask when ownership, rating, destination or authority is unclear;
- follow the rigging lead/house/tour process for assigned ground support.

This is awareness and assigned support, not practical rigging qualification.

**State:** `draftable` for awareness-only content.

# MDQ-062 — Rigging hardware and system knowledge map

## Category map

The curriculum may identify broad categories such as:

- shackles and rated connectors;
- slings and lifting media;
- wire-rope assemblies;
- chain-hoist / entertainment-hoist systems;
- truss and structural-support interfaces;
- venue rigging/support points;
- load-monitoring / load-cell systems;
- theatre counterweight or machinery-system families;
- motor-control interfaces;
- documentation and inspection records.

## What learners should understand conceptually

- rated hardware has identification and capacity information;
- hardware/system compatibility is not determined by appearance alone;
- inspection status and manufacturer information matter;
- damaged, modified, unidentified or questionable components require escalation;
- a complete rigging system is more than individual hardware pieces;
- the overhead structure/support point is part of the load path and has its own design/inspection authority.

## Current official evidence

OSHA 1926.251 requires rigging equipment used for material handling to be inspected and marked with manufacturer-recommended working-load information; defective equipment is removed from service. OSHA 1910.184 separately addresses slings in general industry.

ESTA's current rigging standards ecosystem includes multiple permanent/temporary entertainment rigging and support-system standards. A notable current update is **ANSI E1.56-2026 — Rigging Support Points**, covering design, fabrication, installation, inspection and documentation of permanent stationary rigging support points.

## Curriculum boundary

Do not teach hitch selection, attachment methods, hardware assembly, or overhead connection procedure as a general online practical.

**State:** `researched` for categories/authority; operational application remains external.

# MDQ-063 — Load, force and structural-concept map

## Scope

Rigging professionals need quantitative understanding of load and force, but a general Crew Blueprint course should teach **why these concepts matter and who owns the calculation**, not how to calculate a live overhead system.

## Concept families

- load path;
- static vs changing/dynamic effects;
- point loads vs distributed loads;
- center of gravity;
- vector/angle effects;
- multi-point systems;
- truss/structure capacity as engineered/manufacturer-defined information;
- working-load/design/safety-factor terminology distinctions;
- documentation and revision control;
- environmental effects where relevant.

## Learner outcome

A non-rigger should understand that apparently small changes in geometry, hardware, support point, system configuration or load can change the engineering/rigging problem and therefore require the responsible qualified person to reassess it.

No learner-facing general module should provide a formula tutorial that implies competence to design or approve an overhead rig.

**State:** `researched` for conceptual literacy; calculation competency is external specialist education/experience.

# MDQ-064 — Arena, theatre and touring rigging workflow map

## ETCP distinction

ETCP maintains **separate Arena and Theatre rigging certifications**.

- Arena certification covers work associated with temporary overhead suspension using chain hoists/truss systems across entertainment environments.
- Theatre certification covers a distinct body of knowledge associated with traditional theatre rigging systems.

This supports treating the rigging field as multiple related specializations rather than one generic “rigger” ladder.

## Workflow phases at role/handoff level

Curriculum may map:

- advance / plot / venue information exchange;
- house/tour responsibility split;
- point/layout planning;
- equipment/prep phase;
- controlled load-in/rigging phase;
- verification/acceptance;
- show-change/maintenance state;
- strike;
- inspection/reporting/closeout.

The learner-facing objective is to understand **who owns what and when handoffs occur**, not to reproduce the technical procedure.

## Sector variation

- arena/concert/touring;
- theatre/performing arts;
- festival/outdoor temporary structures;
- corporate/convention;
- venue/house systems;
- broadcast/film stages where applicable.

**State:** `draftable` as workflow/career orientation.

# MDQ-065 — Work at height, fall protection and rescue interface

## Scope

This is an external qualification/employer-control boundary.

General curriculum may explain:

- why elevated work requires specific authorization/training/equipment;
- falling-person and falling-object hazards;
- exclusion zones;
- communication and coordination;
- rescue planning as an employer/site responsibility;
- weather/site-condition effects;
- interaction with MEWPs/ladders/scaffolds only at recognition and authority-boundary level.

It should not teach climbing technique, fall-arrest setup, rescue technique, MEWP operation, or other operational methods.

OSHA walking-working-surface/fall-protection standards provide the regulator baseline; exact applicability depends on work context and employer/site conditions.

**State:** `external_boundary` for operational practice; `draftable` for recognition/role/authority content.

# MDQ-066 — Inspection, documentation and rigging leadership

## Documentation families

Rigging professionals may work with:

- rigging plots;
- venue point information;
- equipment identification/inspection records;
- rejected/quarantined-gear records;
- hoist/motor records;
- support-point documentation;
- revisions/change notes;
- load/system records;
- incident/near-miss reports;
- handoff/signoff records according to employer/venue practice.

## Leadership responsibilities

At a high level, head/lead/house/tour rigging roles can include:

- interpreting venue/tour information;
- defining work ownership;
- assigning qualified personnel;
- coordinating with lighting/video/audio/staging departments;
- protecting exclusion zones;
- managing change control;
- determining when work stops/escalates;
- verifying required inspections/documentation;
- communicating unresolved conditions;
- incident/reporting follow-through.

Crew Blueprint may teach the leadership architecture without representing course completion as appointment to a head-rigger role.

**State:** `draftable` for documentation/leadership architecture; qualified practitioner review required.

# 8. Career / qualification architecture

A responsible career map should show:

**General Stagehand / Rigging Awareness**  
→ **Assigned Ground Support / Local Training Exposure**  
→ **Employer/Union/Venue Training and Documented Experience**  
→ **Specialist Ground/Up/Theatre/Arena Roles according to local system**  
→ **External credential pathways such as ETCP where eligibility is met**  
→ **Head/House/Tour Rigging Leadership or other specialist directions**

This path is not automatic and does not imply that early stages grant overhead or climbing authority.

## Current ETCP baseline

- Rigging certifications: Arena and Theatre.
- Target audience: experienced riggers age 21+.
- Eligibility: 30 points for either rigging examination.
- ETCP describes the examinations as validating experienced riggers rather than serving as entry-level training.

This makes ETCP a useful **career/qualification reference**, not a Crew Blueprint completion badge.
