# PROJECT-E01 — Learner, Customer, and Business Requirements Baseline

**Prepared:** 2026-08-31  
**Status:** Baseline evidence map; hypotheses separated from validated requirements  
**Purpose:** Define what is currently supported about learners, workers, employers/labor providers, and potential buyers before choosing product packaging, pricing, organization features, or final curriculum architecture.

---

# 1. Controlling rule

The Crew Blueprint has enough content evidence to move beyond MVP-style guessing, but **content maturity does not automatically prove customer demand, buyer willingness, pricing, procurement requirements, or preferred product format**.

This file therefore separates:

- **known / source-supported industry facts**;
- **strong product hypotheses**;
- **questions that require interviews, pilots, usage data, or sales tests**.

No business-model decision becomes canonical merely because it appears plausible here.

---

# 2. Learner-side problem supported by the existing corpus

Existing Stagehand research consistently shows that early-career workers are expected to enter environments containing:

- multiple departments and chains of command;
- unfamiliar venue/stage terminology;
- road cases, carts, cable, staging/scenic components, soft goods and other physical equipment;
- changing load-in/show/load-out phases;
- work around controlled systems such as rigging, temporary power and powered equipment;
- strong expectations around punctuality, check-in, communication, safety, professionalism and following assignment ownership.

Existing Crew Blueprint Packages 15, 18, 20/20A, 21, 46–53 and the SFS-U/O evidence series substantiate this as an education problem: there is a large amount of **job-context knowledge and bounded practical preparation** that can be taught before or between calls without pretending to replace employer authorization or specialist qualification.

## Supported learner value proposition

The strongest defensible learner-facing value is:

> **Help new and developing live-event workers understand what they are looking at, how work flows, how to support the crew correctly, what ordinary tasks are likely to involve, where their boundaries are, and how to prepare for deeper department/career growth.**

Do not promise:

- employment;
- callbacks;
- certification where none exists;
- employer authorization;
- union membership;
- guaranteed promotion;
- replacement for hands-on/site/manufacturer/qualified-person training.

# 3. Industry evidence that training and progression matter

## ETCP — specialist credential ecosystem

The Entertainment Technician Certification Program is a mature industry credential program supported by organizations including ESTA, IATSE, IAVM, USITT, AVIXA and major employers/businesses. ETCP certifications target experienced technicians in higher-liability disciplines such as arena/theatre rigging, entertainment electrical work and portable power distribution.

Sources:

https://etcp.esta.org/  
https://etcp.esta.org/etcp/about.html  
https://etcp.esta.org/certify/certify.html

### Product implication

This strongly reinforces Crew Blueprint's authority separation:

- general education and early-career practical preparation are one layer;
- experienced specialist credentialing is another layer;
- Crew Blueprint should help learners understand and progress toward legitimate external pathways rather than imitate them.

ETCP explicitly uses substantial work experience/eligibility requirements. That is evidence against presenting an introductory online course as equivalent to experienced technician validation.

## ETCP recognized employers/labor providers

ETCP operates recognition programs for employers, contractors and labor providers that commit to using certified technicians in relevant specialist contexts.

Sources:

https://etcp.esta.org/findrecognized/search.php  
https://etcp.esta.org/findrecognized/becomerecognized.html

### Customer implication

The industry has evidence of organizational willingness to use external competency/credential signals when the signal is credible, bounded and relevant to safety-critical work.

That does **not** prove companies will pay for Crew Blueprint. It does support researching organizational workflows such as:

- training assignment;
- completion visibility;
- practice observation;
- supervisor/reviewer records;
- external credential visibility;
- role/pathway recommendations.

## AVIXA workforce-development model

AVIXA currently provides career pathways, guided content, structured training, workforce-development resources, apprenticeship frameworks and employer/talent resources.

Source:  
https://www.avixa.org/community/av-careers

### Product implication

The adjacent professional-AV market treats career navigation, introductory learning, employer talent needs and structured progression as connected problems rather than unrelated products.

Crew Blueprint should therefore research both:

- **worker value** — “help me become more useful and advance”; and
- **organization value** — “help me prepare/develop people and understand what evidence I can trust.”

# 6. Business-model questions that must be researched before pricing

## Individual product

Research:

- free entry layer vs paid advanced material;
- one-time purchase vs subscription;
- whether workers value progress history, practice records, career maps, source transparency, job resources, or community enough to pay;
- whether irregular employment makes monthly subscription unattractive;
- whether employer-paid access dominates individual willingness to pay.

## Organization product

Research:

- seat-based vs active-user vs cohort pricing;
- seasonal workforce requirements;
- manager/reviewer seats;
- whether organizations need custom content/local policies;
- whether they require SSO, roster imports, exports, APIs, LMS interoperability, or audit reports;
- whether they need separation between educational completion and employer authorization;
- retention/deletion requirements for former workers;
- procurement, insurance, privacy and accessibility requirements.

## Marketplace / credential-adjacent possibilities

Do **not** build credential issuance merely because it is technically easy.

Research first:

- what evidence employers actually trust;
- whether observed-practice records are useful when the observer is not the employer;
- whether a portable worker skills profile creates value or liability;
- whether external credentials such as ETCP/OSHA/employer records should be linkable/verified rather than recreated;
- whether employer endorsements or practice observations create worker-classification or reference/defamation risks.

# 7. Customer-facing trust requirements already visible

Even before interviews, the evidence program indicates that trust will depend on:

1. **Source transparency** — important claims trace to authoritative/current sources.
2. **Scope honesty** — the product says what it does not authorize.
3. **Versioning** — learners/organizations can tell which content version was completed.
4. **Clear evidence states** — knowledge, observed practice, employer authorization and external credentials are separate.
5. **Visual quality** — physical work is not reduced to vague text-only instruction.
6. **Practitioner review** — employer/local/manufacturer variation is not flattened into fake standards.
7. **Accessible design** — learner access should not depend on unnecessary barriers.
8. **Privacy/security** — progress and worker records cannot be treated casually if organization accounts are introduced.

# 8. Primary-research program required

## Learner interviews / usability sessions

Recruit across:

- 0 calls;
- 1–10 calls;
- 10–50 calls;
- department-transition workers;
- experienced crew leads.

Investigate:

- what confused them on first calls;
- what they learned only through correction/mistakes;
- what they wish they had seen visually beforehand;
- which online material they would actually complete;
- whether task cards, call-flow simulations, search/reference mode, guided course mode, or a hybrid is more useful;
- how they interpret completion/practice/authorization labels;
- career-navigation needs.

## Buyer discovery

Interview labor providers, venues, production companies, locals, rental/AV companies and educational programs.

Do not pitch features first. Ask:

- how they onboard workers now;
- what failures consume supervisor time;
- what they will and will not trust from external education;
- what records they already keep;
- what proof they need before assigning work;
- what data they would refuse to store in an external system;
- what integrations/procurement/security/accessibility requirements exist;
- whether training is a cost center, recruiting advantage, risk-control tool, worker-retention tool, or all of the above.

# 9. Metrics for business/customer evidence

Do not use signups alone.

Potential evidence categories:

- learner activation;
- lesson/skill completion;
- return usage on later calls;
- search/reference usage;
- knowledge retention;
- observed-practice progression;
- learner confidence calibrated against actual performance;
- crew-lead/practitioner ratings;
- onboarding time avoided;
- repeated correction/error categories;
- organization assignment/completion rates;
- buyer renewal/expansion intent;
- willingness to pay;
- support burden;
- content freshness/maintenance cost.

---

# 10. Decision gates

Do **not** finalize business packaging until:

- at least the major curriculum domain research is mature enough to show product scope;
- learner research demonstrates how workers actually want to use the material;
- buyer discovery distinguishes individual vs organizational value;
- liability/privacy/security requirements for worker records are understood;
- testing establishes what evidence the platform can legitimately claim.

## Current conclusion

There is sufficient evidence to justify continued investment in a **deep live-event workforce education and progression system**.

There is **not yet sufficient primary customer evidence** to lock pricing, packaging, organization features, credentials, or procurement strategy.

**Next state:** `research_active` — customer discovery and learner testing should run in parallel with content research, not after the entire platform is built.
