# 32 — Event Operations / Supervisor Tier: Production Coordination

**Research Queue ID:** CB-009  
**Status:** Complete research packet / decision gate resolved  
**Research date:** 2026-08-17  
**Depends on:** CB-001 through CB-008  
**Purpose:** Resolve the governance question about whether production-wide supervision is simply a higher department lead role or a distinct cross-functional tier.

---

## Decision question

**What is the relationship between a department lead and a production/event supervisor?**

## Bottom-line finding

The evidence supports treating **event operations / production supervision as a distinct cross-functional tier**, not simply the next promotion above every department lead.

A department lead owns a bounded technical system or crew. A production/event supervisor coordinates **multiple departments, venue functions, contractors, schedules, clients, safety interfaces, and event-wide readiness**.

Current IAVM job evidence consistently describes event/production management roles as integrators across technical production, venue operations, guest services, security/public safety, vendors, staffing, client communication, timelines, logistics, and post-event reporting.

Sources:

- IAVM — Associate Event Production Manager, Jerome Schottenstein Center  
  https://member.iavm.org/cv5/cgi-bin/JobBoard.dll/Info?CONTRACTNUM=59021&WRP=JobEdit.htm
- IAVM — Director of Events, PSQ Productions  
  https://member.iavm.org/cv5/cgi-bin/JobBoard.dll/Info?CONTRACTNUM=59295&WRP=JobEdit.htm
- IAVM — Director of Operations, Mississippi Coast Coliseum & Convention Center  
  https://member.iavm.org/cv5/cgi-bin/JobBoard.dll/Info?CONTRACTNUM=59217&WRP=JobEdit.htm

Therefore:

> **Lead = depth/accountability inside one department or defined work package. Supervisor/event operations = breadth/accountability across interfaces.**

This resolves OD-11 in favor of a separate production-coordination branch.

# 1. Evidence from current venue/event roles

## Associate Event Production Manager

The Jerome Schottenstein Center role coordinates planning and execution across events, advances technical requirements, coordinates equipment/services, manages client/internal communication, supervises stagehands/technicians, works with Guest Services, Security, Parking, Public Safety, and Building Operations, and produces event-day/post-event documentation.

This is direct evidence that production supervision crosses technical and operational boundaries.

## Director of Events

PSQ Productions’ current Director of Events role is responsible for planning, execution, staffing, safety, production coordination, venue operations, vendor management, public safety coordination, run-of-show planning, advancing, technical-rider fulfillment, weather planning, and pre-event readiness inspections.

The role is explicitly the operational integrator across FOH, BOH, production, security, guest services, facilities, artists, clients, vendors, and public agencies.

## Director of Operations

The Mississippi Coast Coliseum & Convention Center’s current Director of Operations combines facility operations, event execution, setup/conversions, production coordination, labor supervision, operational planning, and cross-department execution.

These roles differ by organization, but the common competency is **integration across systems**.

# 2. Department lead versus supervisor

## Department lead

Primary accountability:

- one technical department, crew, or work package;
- department-specific planning;
- technical verification;
- internal crew assignments;
- department safety controls;
- department readiness status.

Examples:

- lighting lead;
- audio lead;
- video lead;
- staging/carpentry lead;
- rigging lead;
- electrics lead.

## Production/event supervisor

Primary accountability:

- event-wide or multi-department coordination;
- dependencies between technical and venue functions;
- integrated schedule/timeline;
- staffing/resource coordination;
- client/tour/venue communication;
- change management;
- event-wide readiness;
- operational safety escalation;
- documentation and handoffs;
- resolution of conflicts between departmental priorities.

The supervisor does **not** become the technical expert who overrides every department lead. The role depends on knowing when to rely on department authority.

---

# 3. Correct authority model

Crew Blueprint should teach production supervision as a **federated authority model**.

The supervisor may own:

- event schedule;
- cross-department priorities;
- coordination meetings/briefings;
- resource conflicts;
- client/venue status communication;
- overall readiness tracking;
- escalation of operational risks;
- stop/hold decisions within assigned organizational authority.

The supervisor should not automatically override:

- rigging calculations or qualified rigging judgment;
- electrical qualification/code decisions;
- engineering approvals;
- manufacturer limitations;
- creative/design authority;
- venue/AHJ restrictions;
- specialist safety decisions outside the supervisor’s competence.

A strong supervisor coordinates experts rather than pretending to replace them.

# 4. Core supervisor competencies

## 4.1 Advance and requirements integration

The supervisor should be able to combine information from:

- production riders;
- venue constraints;
- schedules;
- staffing plans;
- CAD/layout information;
- equipment/service orders;
- contractor/vendor requirements;
- security/public-safety needs;
- guest/accessibility requirements;
- emergency/weather plans.

The objective is to find conflicts before show day.

## 4.2 Dependency mapping

Production-wide supervisors need to see relationships such as:

- staging readiness before dependent technical placement;
- rigging/electrics interfaces with lighting/audio/video;
- dock/load-in sequence versus venue access;
- FOH/BOH transitions;
- security/public access versus ongoing production work;
- artist/client schedule versus technical readiness.

## 4.3 Resource and labor coordination

Current event-management roles routinely include scheduling staff, contractors, vendors, stagehands, technicians, security, parking, EMT/public-safety support, and other operational personnel.

Crew Blueprint should teach supervisors to identify **resource conflicts and competence gaps**, not simply headcount shortages.

## 4.4 Status communication

The supervisor needs a common operational picture.

Recommended status vocabulary:

- ready;
- ready with constraint;
- blocked;
- waiting on dependency;
- unsafe / work held;
- changed requirement;
- escalation required.

This vocabulary is a Crew Blueprint design recommendation, not an industry standard.

## 4.5 Change control

Supervisors should evaluate changes for effects on:

- timeline;
- staffing;
- equipment;
- safety;
- technical departments;
- venue operations;
- public/guest interfaces;
- cost/authorization;
- required approvals.

The critical behavior is **do not allow one department’s “small change” to propagate invisibly through the event.**

## 4.6 Event-wide safety coordination

Package 31 establishes the predictive hazard model. At supervisor tier, this expands across departments and public/venue interfaces.

The supervisor should ensure hazards that cross boundaries have a clear owner and that unresolved risk is visible in the event status.

# 6. Assessment model

Use an integrated event scenario with several department leads reporting to the learner.

The learner should have to:

1. identify dependencies;
2. resolve a resource conflict;
3. recognize when a technical decision belongs to a department specialist;
4. communicate a delay to client/venue leadership;
5. react to a new safety or operational constraint;
6. decide whether the event/workstream is ready, blocked, or requires escalation;
7. document the decision path.

This is substantially different from a department-lead assessment because the learner is graded on **integration**, not craft technique.

---

# 7. Career-path implication

Production/event supervision should be shown as a **branch**, not a mandatory rung above every technical lead.

Possible pathways include:

- experienced stagehand → crew chief → event operations supervisor;
- lighting/audio/video/staging lead → production coordinator/manager;
- venue operations worker → event manager;
- stage-management background → production coordination;
- technical specialist → department head without ever moving into event-wide supervision.

This preserves Package 05’s finding that stage management is a distinct discipline and Package 24’s finding that leadership titles do not form one universal national ladder.

# 9. Hard boundaries

Completion of CB-009 must not imply:

- appointment as production manager;
- authority over every department’s technical decisions;
- venue-management certification;
- emergency-management certification;
- security/crowd-control qualification;
- engineering, rigging, or electrical qualification;
- legal authority to override venue, AHJ, employer, union, or public-safety requirements.

## Governance decision — OD-11

**Resolved: treat Event Operations / Production Supervisor as a distinct cross-functional branch above or alongside department-lead experience, not as the automatic universal next rung.**

The defining competency is the ability to integrate multiple expert departments and venue functions into one coherent, safe, executable event plan while respecting the technical authority of the specialists inside those departments.
