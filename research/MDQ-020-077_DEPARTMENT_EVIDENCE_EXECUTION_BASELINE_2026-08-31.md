# MDQ-020–077 — Department Evidence Execution Baseline

**Prepared:** 2026-08-31  
**Status:** Cross-department evidence consolidation and active execution map  
**Domains:** Lighting, Audio, Video/LED, Staging/Scenic, Rigging, Electrics/Production Power  
**Purpose:** Put the six major technical department research waves under one evidence framework before completing the detailed MDQ packets.

> This file consolidates current evidence, identifies missing bodies of knowledge, and sets source/authority rules. It does not replace MDQ-020–077 detailed packets.

---

# 1. Cross-department decision

The existing Crew Blueprint department material is broad enough that the next phase should not be “make more courses.” It should be:

1. inventory the real body of knowledge for each department;
2. distinguish general support, systems understanding, operator/specialist work, leadership and design;
3. tie each claim to exact current sources;
4. preserve manufacturer/model/venue/local/regulatory variation;
5. identify what can be taught visually online versus what requires supervised field practice or external qualification;
6. only then decide the final department course structures.

---

# 2. Shared evidence rules

For all six departments:

- industry standards describe technical requirements but may be copyrighted; use exact citations/metadata and paraphrase rather than reproducing protected standards text;
- manufacturer documentation controls product/model-specific procedures;
- one manufacturer's workflow does not become a universal industry workflow;
- employer/local/venue rules may be stricter than transferable educational content;
- online course completion never creates employer authorization;
- specialist qualification/licensure/certification remains external where applicable;
- visual instruction must identify whether it is conceptual, generic, or exact-product/model specific;
- all safety-critical claims require strong source lineage and review.

# 4. Audio — MDQ-030–039

## Existing Crew Blueprint evidence

Current Audio C1/C2 and Package 43 already establish a pathway, but the domain requires a larger independent body of knowledge.

## Standards/current external source families

The Audio Engineering Society publishes standards across analog/digital interconnection and networked audio. Current AES material identifies AES67 as an audio-over-IP interoperability standard and AES70 as a control/monitoring architecture for professional media networks.

Sources:

https://aes.org/standards/  
https://aes.org/publications/standards-store/  
https://aes.org/audio-topics/audio-networking-2/  
https://aes.org/standards-blog/aes70-2023-aes-standard-for-audio-applications-of-networks-open-control-architecture-has-been-published/

AES standards are copyrighted; Crew Blueprint should cite/interpret within allowed limits, not reproduce standards.

## Audio research priorities

### MDQ-030 — Audio role architecture

Map:

- general audio support;
- patch/cable/stage support;
- system technician;
- FOH;
- monitors;
- RF coordination;
- network audio;
- systems engineering/optimization;
- comms/intercom interfaces;
- shop/prep;
- lead/system designer.

FOH, Monitor, RF and Systems should not remain hidden inside one generic “advanced audio” title.

### MDQ-031 — Signal-flow fundamentals

Sources → input stage → processing/mixing → routing/distribution → amplification/processing → transducers, with analog/digital/network variants.

### MDQ-032 — Connectors/cabling/stage patch

Separate physical support from patch authority and energized/system decisions.

### MDQ-033 — Console/mixing concepts

Transferable channel/bus/routing/gain-processing concepts before manufacturer-specific operation.

### MDQ-034 — Loudspeaker/system deployment concepts

General system architecture and safe support; structural rigging and final system engineering remain specialist/department responsibilities.

### MDQ-035 — Audio networking

AES67, Dante and other ecosystem concepts; clocking, addressing, discovery, redundancy and interoperability should be carefully separated.

### MDQ-036 — RF coordination

RF/wireless is a specialist branch. Manufacturer tools such as Shure Wireless Workbench provide useful product/workflow evidence, but generic curriculum must avoid turning one tool into a universal method.

Sources:

https://www.shure.com/en-US/insights/rf-coordination-what-you-need-to-know  
https://www.shure.com/en-US/docs/quickstart/WIRELESS-WORKBENCH

### MDQ-037 — Intercom/comms

Coordinate with Stagehand communication research and show-control/production communication standards.

### MDQ-038 — Shop/QC/troubleshooting

Cable/test workflows, documentation, spares, fault isolation and escalation.

### MDQ-039 — Audio leadership/design/sector transfer

FOH/monitor/system roles vary by concert, theatre, corporate, broadcast and installed environments.

**Current Audio state:** `drafted pathway + meaningful research; expanded body-of-knowledge research required`.

# 7. Rigging — MDQ-060–066

## Existing Crew Blueprint evidence

Rigging is the clearest asymmetry in the current curriculum: there is lead/awareness material, but no safe reason to invent a novice physical rigging tier for symmetry.

## External credential evidence

ETCP's Arena and Theatre Rigging certifications are explicitly designed for experienced riggers and cover safety-critical work. Eligibility emphasizes significant experience/education rather than completion of a short introductory course.

Sources:

https://etcp.esta.org/certify/certify_rigger.html  
https://etcp.esta.org/certify/examination_rigger_arena.html

## Rigging research priorities

### MDQ-060 — Rigging role architecture

Map:

- general-hand interface/ground support;
- ground rigger;
- arena up-rigger;
- theatre rigger/fly roles;
- rigging lead/head;
- engineering/qualified-person/venue structural authority;
- hoist inspection/maintenance interfaces;
- ETCP/external qualification pathways.

### MDQ-061 — General-stagehand rigging awareness

Safe topic families:

- recognize rigging zones and ownership;
- do not interfere with ropes/chains/hoists/hardware;
- understand overhead-work boundaries;
- communicate/escalate through the responsible rigger/lead.

### MDQ-062 — Ground-rigging body of knowledge map

Research competencies, terminology, equipment families and authority structure **without publishing step-by-step physical rigging tutorials for unqualified learners**.

### MDQ-063 — Arena rigging body of knowledge map

Use ETCP/ESTA/manufacturer/venue sources to map the professional domain, not to simulate qualification.

### MDQ-064 — Theatre rigging/fly body of knowledge map

Separate installed counterweight/mechanical/hydraulic systems from arena temporary rigging.

### MDQ-065 — Inspection/documentation/engineering interfaces

Who owns inspection, load information, venue approval, qualified-person decisions and records.

### MDQ-066 — Career/credential/leadership path

ETCP and employer/local pathways; do not imply Crew Blueprint certification.

# 8. Electrics / Temporary Production Power — MDQ-070–077

## Existing Crew Blueprint evidence

Crew Blueprint already contains Production Power Awareness and advanced/lead research. The missing question is not whether power matters; it is how to teach useful system understanding without turning self-study into permission to perform energized or qualified-person work.

## External credential/regulatory evidence

ETCP offers experienced-technician certifications for Entertainment Electrician and Portable Power Distribution Technician roles.

Source:  
https://etcp.esta.org/certify/certify_electrical.html

OSHA Subpart S governs electrical safety in general industry and includes requirements affecting temporary wiring and qualified-person access.

Source:  
https://www.osha.gov/etools/subpart-s

## Electrics research priorities

### MDQ-070 — Production-power role architecture

Map:

- general stagehand interface;
- lighting/audio/video power support boundaries;
- entertainment electrician;
- portable-power distribution technician;
- venue electrician;
- generator/utility/vendor interfaces;
- lead/head electrician;
- licensed/AHJ/qualified-person boundaries.

### MDQ-071 — General electrical hazard / recognition

Recognition, damaged-equipment reporting, route/protection, energized boundaries and stop/escalate rules.

### MDQ-072 — Portable-power system mental model

Source → distribution → protection → branch/load conceptually, without giving unqualified learners live connection/testing procedures.

### MDQ-073 — Connectors/cable/equipment identification

Identification and handling context only where safe; exact energized use/testing remains qualified/employer-controlled.

### MDQ-074 — Grounding/bonding/protection concepts

Educational conceptual understanding tied to authoritative sources; no improvised field procedure.

### MDQ-075 — Load planning/documentation interfaces

What information exists and who owns calculations/acceptance.

### MDQ-076 — Inspection/testing/troubleshooting boundaries

Map professional responsibilities and tools without turning the course into a live-fault troubleshooting guide.

### MDQ-077 — Career/credential/sector transfer

ETCP, licensure/AHJ variation, theatre/concert/corporate/festival/film/broadcast differences.

**Current Electrics state:** `awareness + advanced conceptual research; live/qualified work remains external`.

# 12. Definition of department-research completion

A department is not “research complete” because a Course 1/2/3 exists.

Completion requires:

- role architecture;
- competency map;
- workflow map;
- tools/equipment/system map;
- failure/hazard map;
- sector variation;
- exact source ledger;
- curriculum recommendation;
- unresolved gaps;
- authority/credential map;
- matrix-ready claim families;
- freshness/review plan.

Only then should final course structure be optimized.
