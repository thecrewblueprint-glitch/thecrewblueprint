# Research Package 02 — Department Skill Progressions in U.S. Live Events

**Research date:** 2026-08-15  
**Scope:** U.S. live-events production departments — audio, lighting, video, rigging, electrics, carpentry, and staging — with touring, arena, festival, theatre, and corporate AV differences where the evidence supports them.

## Executive finding

There is **no single U.S. live-events career ladder shared by every employer, venue, union local, tour, and production company**. Real advancement structures exist, but they are department-specific and context-specific.

Three broad patterns recur:

1. **Assist / technician level** — works under direction, performs defined setup/strike/operation tasks, and is not yet the person accountable for the whole system or department.
2. **Independent operator / system owner level** — can prep, install, troubleshoot, operate, and maintain a department system with limited supervision. Titles vary heavily: A1, lighting technician, board operator/programmer, video technician, rigger, electrician, carpenter, etc.
3. **Lead / department-head level** — plans work, assigns crew, coordinates other departments, owns safety/quality for the department, advances shows, solves exceptions, and often trains or evaluates less-experienced workers. Common titles include Crew Chief, Head Audio, Head Electrician, Head Rigger, Head Carpenter, Lead Technician, Technical Lead, and department-specific equivalents.

The critical point for curriculum design is that **many familiar titles are parallel specialties rather than sequential promotions**. FOH Engineer and Monitor Engineer are not necessarily “above” A1 in all markets; Lighting Programmer and Lighting Designer are different functions; Systems Engineer is often a highly advanced audio specialty rather than simply the next title after A1; Video roles fragment into LED, projection, switching, media servers, cameras, and network/data specialties.

The most standardized national personnel credentials are concentrated in **rigging and entertainment electrical work** through ETCP. Audio/video have strong AVIXA and manufacturer credentials, but those are not universal job-rank systems. Carpentry and staging do not have a comparable national certification ladder.

# 1. Audio

## Real titles found

Across touring/live production and corporate AV, documented titles include:

- Audio Technician
- A2 / Audio Assist / Audio Technician (common industry shorthand; not nationally regulated)
- Audio Crew Chief
- Audio FOH Engineer
- Audio Monitor Engineer
- Audio Wireless Technician / RF Technician
- Audio Network Technician
- System & PA Calibration Technician / Systems Technician / Systems Engineer
- Intercom Technician
- Audio Designer
- Technical Lead — Audio

PRG’s current crew portal is unusually useful because it lists separate real-world skill categories for **Audio Crew Chief, Audio Technician, Audio Wireless Technician, Audio Network Technician, Audio FOH Engineer, Audio Monitor Engineer, System & PA Calibration Technician, Intercom Technician, Backline Technician, and Audio Designer**. It also explicitly distinguishes experience bands within several specialties. Source: [PRG Crew Portal](https://crew.prg.com/en/).

Encore currently uses **Technical Lead (Audio)** for an intermediate-to-advanced live-event role responsible for FOH console setup/operation, RF systems, and PA build/tuning, and describes the position as capable of handling small to large-scale AV systems. Source: [Encore Technical Lead (Audio)](https://jobs.encoreglobal.com/en/job/-/-/6228/93456645984).

IATSE Local 13’s 2026 public job-posting list separately includes **Head Audio**, **Lead Sound Engineer**, and **Assistant Sound Engineer**, demonstrating that union/venue structures may use a more traditional assistant → lead/head vocabulary rather than A2/A1. Source: [IATSE Local 13](https://www.iatse13.org/).

## Practical progression

A defensible training progression is:

### Assist level — A2 / Audio Technician / Assistant Sound

Typical scope:

- patching and cable work under direction;
- microphone, stand, DI, intercom, and stage-box deployment;
- RF pack preparation and basic checks where authorized;
- speaker/PA deployment under system lead direction;
- line checks and troubleshooting assigned subsystems;
- supporting FOH, monitors, systems, or wireless personnel.

The defining boundary is not a universal number of years. It is whether the worker can reliably execute assigned audio tasks without redesigning the system or making system-wide decisions.

### Independent level — A1 / FOH / Monitor / Audio Technician / specialist technician

At this level, a worker can independently operate the assigned system or subsystem. Depending on production type that may mean:

- mixing FOH;
- mixing monitors;
- independently operating a corporate general session room;
- independently coordinating RF;
- independently operating an intercom system;
- managing an audio network;
- deploying and troubleshooting a PA.

**Important:** A1 is a common live/corporate shorthand, but employer titles may instead say Audio Engineer, Lead Audio, Technical Lead Audio, or simply Audio Technician.

### Lead / advanced level — Audio Crew Chief / Head Audio / Systems Engineer / Audio Designer

PRG defines Audio Crew Chief by leadership and system accountability: experience leading technicians and stagehands, planning/executing/monitoring audio requirements, and responsibility for the audio systems. PRG’s higher crew-chief bands explicitly require repeated leadership of larger audio teams. Source: [PRG Crew Portal](https://crew.prg.com/en/).

At the advanced end, **Systems Engineer / PA Calibration Technician** is a specialist branch involving prediction, deployment, optimization, measurement, tuning, and system-wide troubleshooting. It should not be presented as merely “A1 plus one promotion.” Likewise, Audio Designer is a design function rather than simply a senior operator title.

## Touring / arena / festival vs corporate AV

**Touring / arena / festival:** role names tend to fragment by function: FOH, monitors, systems, RF/wireless, PA tech, stage/patch, intercom. A touring audio crew may contain multiple senior specialists at the same time.

**Corporate AV:** employers often use broader ladders such as AV Technician → Technical Lead → Senior Technical Lead / Technical Supervisor → Technical Manager or Director. Encore’s current career listings show Event Technician, Event Technical Lead, Senior Technical Lead, Technical Supervisor, Operations Manager, and Director of Event Technology titles. Source: [Encore job search](https://jobs.encoreglobal.com/en/search_jobs).

## Formal apprenticeship / journeyman structure

There is **no nationwide audio-specific apprentice → journeyman licensing structure for live sound**. Some IATSE stagecraft locals do have formal apprenticeships that expose apprentices to sound along with lighting, rigging, carpentry, and other crafts.

Examples:

- IATSE Local One’s apprenticeship is a 2–3 year program across theatres, television studios, lighting/sound shops, and scenic facilities, with instruction across lighting, sound, rigging, carpentry, and emerging technologies. Completion leads to full membership. Source: [IATSE Local One Apprenticeship](https://www.iatselocaloneapprenticeship.com/).
- IATSE Local 470 explicitly describes apprentice members studying to become **Journeymen Stagehands**. Source: [IATSE Local 470 Education](https://www.ia470.com/education.html).
- IATSE Local 38 offers both direct Journeyperson membership and an Apprenticeship Program. Source: [IATSE Local 38 membership](https://www.iatse38.org/mobile/index.cfm?page=How20To20Join&zone=%2Funionactive%2Fview_page.cfm).

These are stagecraft/local structures, not a nationally standardized “Journeyman Audio Engineer” credential.

## Credentials

There is **no ETCP audio certification**. ETCP’s national certifications are limited to Arena Rigger, Theatre Rigger, Entertainment Electrician, and Portable Power Distribution Technician. Source: [ETCP](https://etcp.esta.org/).

Relevant audio/AV credentials include:

- **AVIXA CTS** — general audiovisual credential; no prerequisite, with AVIXA recommending roughly six months of experience. Source: [AVIXA CTS](https://www.avixa.org/training-certification/certification/cts-certification).
- **AVIXA CTS-I** — installation specialization; requires CTS and AVIXA recommends 3–4 years of experience. Source: [AVIXA CTS-I](https://www.avixa.org/training-certification/certification/cts-i-certification).
- **AVIXA CTS-D** — AV design specialization; requires CTS. Source: [AVIXA certification overview](https://www.avixa.org/training-certification/certification).
- **Dante Certification Levels 1, 2, and 3** — genuine progressive Audinate networking credentials. The 2025 third-edition program covers foundations through advanced networking and troubleshooting. Source: [Audinate Dante Training](https://www.audinate.com/press/audinate-launches-completely-revamped-dante-training-program/).
- Shure currently publishes Pro Audio learning paths at **Level 1 Fundamentals, Level 2 Intermediate, and Level 3 Advanced**, targeted respectively at aspiring/entry-level personnel, engineers/live sound technicians, and experienced AV/RF/system personnel. This is useful as a training model but is not an industry job-rank standard. Source: [Shure Academy](https://www.shure.com/en-US/shure-academy/certification).

### Bottom line — Audio

**Teach:** Assist → independent operator/system specialist → crew chief/head/systems/design branch.  
**Do not teach:** A2 → A1 → FOH → systems engineer as a universal mandatory ladder. That sequence is too simplistic.

# 2. Lighting

## Real titles found

Current employer and union evidence supports:

- Lighting Technician
- Electrician / Stage Electrician
- Follow Spot Operator
- Lighting Desk Operator / Board Operator
- Lighting Programmer
- Moving Light Technician / Moving Light Service Technician
- Lighting Network Technician
- Assistant Electrician
- Head Electrician
- Lighting Crew Chief
- Head of Lighting
- Lighting Designer (LD)
- Gaffer / Chief Lighting Technician in film/TV contexts

PRG currently lists Lighting Crew Chief, Lighting Technician, Moving Light Service Technician, Lighting Network Technician, Lighting Desk Operator, Lighting Follow Spot Operator, and Lighting Designer as separate specialties. Source: [PRG Crew Portal](https://crew.prg.com/en/).

IATSE Local 13’s 2026 postings include **Head Electrician** and **Head of Lighting**, confirming head-level titles in venue/live-event employment. Source: [IATSE Local 13](https://www.iatse13.org/).

A 2026 national-tour Head Electrician posting describes the Head Electrician as leader of the electrics department, responsible for assigning the Assistant Electrician and local crew, advancing venues, focusing the show, maintaining systems, and running the production to the Lighting Designer’s standards. Source: [Operation Mincemeat Head Electrician posting](https://playbill.com/job/head-electrician-operation-mincemeat-tour/c93251e5-f664-4f10-ab35-ab97fe1bed99).

## Practical progression

### Assist level — Lighting Hand / Electrician / Lighting Technician / Followspot

Typical scope:

- fixture handling and hanging under direction;
- cable, data, safety, and accessory deployment;
- addressing/configuration under supervision;
- focus assistance;
- followspot operation after show-specific training;
- basic troubleshooting and maintenance.

### Independent level — Lighting Technician / Board Operator / Programmer / department electrician

The independent threshold is the ability to take responsibility for an assigned lighting subsystem or show-operating position: console operation, fixture/system troubleshooting, data/networking, power/control distribution within authorization, or a repeatable show track.

PRG’s Lighting Technician categories progress from training/comparable experience to 3+ years and then 5+ years plus experience leading other lighting technicians. This is an employer-specific example of a genuine competency ladder. Source: [PRG Crew Portal](https://crew.prg.com/en/).

### Lead level — Lighting Crew Chief / Head Electrician / Head of Lighting

PRG’s Lighting Crew Chief requires the ability to lead technicians and stagehands, plan and monitor lighting requirements, and possess strong electrical and product knowledge. Source: [PRG Crew Portal](https://crew.prg.com/en/).

The Head Electrician on a tour typically becomes the operational department head: local labor assignment, maintenance, focus, load-in/load-out execution, and maintaining the designer’s show.

### Lighting Designer is a branch, not simply the next technician rank

A Lighting Designer owns the creative lighting design. A programmer may work for the designer. A technically excellent Head Electrician may never become an LD, and an LD’s career path can develop through design/assistant-design work rather than through venue electrician hierarchy.

## Credentials

There is no national “Lighting Technician I/II/III” certification equivalent to ETCP rigging.

Relevant credentials/training:

- **ETCP Entertainment Electrician** is highly relevant when lighting work includes entertainment electrical systems. ETCP says the exam is intended to validate the competency of the **upper third of entertainment electricians**. Source: [ETCP Entertainment Electrician](https://etcp.esta.org/certify/certify_electrical.html).
- ETC provides Eos console training and explicitly offers beginning/intermediate/advanced training pathways, but these are manufacturer training levels rather than a cross-industry personnel rank. Source: [ETC Training FAQ](https://www.etcconnect.com/Support/ETC-Training-FAQ.aspx).
- IATSE Local 728’s current training catalog includes electrical theory, 480V power systems, console training, and GroundControl followspot training, illustrating how union training builds specialized competence without creating a national lighting rank system. Source: [IATSE Local 728 Training](https://www.iatse728.org/members/lms).

### Bottom line — Lighting

A strong teaching model is:

**Assist/Lighting Technician → independent electrician/operator/programmer/network/moving-light specialty → Head Electrician or Lighting Crew Chief**, with **Lighting Designer** treated as a creative/design branch rather than the automatic final promotion.

# 3. Video

## Real titles found

PRG currently lists an extensive video taxonomy:

- Video Crew Chief
- Video Technician — LED
- Video Technician — Projection
- Video Technician — Camera Engineering
- Video Technician — Media Servers
- Video Technician — AV Tech
- Video Technician — GFX Switching
- Video/Data Operator
- Media Server Operator
- Camera Operator
- Video/Camera Direction
- Vision Mix / Switcher
- Media/Content Design

Source: [PRG Crew Portal](https://crew.prg.com/en/).

IATSE Local 13’s 2026 job postings include **Head Video**, providing union/venue confirmation that “Head Video” is a real department-head title. Source: [IATSE Local 13](https://www.iatse13.org/).

## Practical progression

### Assist level — Video Technician / LED Tech / AV Tech

PRG’s published LED and AV technician matrices are especially explicit:

- **6–12 months**: rig/de-rig basics, cable management, working under instruction;
- **1–2 years**: basic system control, signal knowledge, monitors/cabling/computer interfacing;
- **2–5 years**: stronger fault-finding, show prep, small-show crew-chief capability, ability to manage roughly 2–6 crew;
- **5+ years**: comprehensive show experience, system design/brief fulfillment/client management in AV contexts, and ability to manage 6+ crew.

Source: [PRG Crew Portal](https://crew.prg.com/en/).

This is one of the clearest real-world examples found of an employer explicitly drawing the line between assist, independent operation, and leadership.

### Independent level — specialist video operator/engineer

At this point the technician can independently own a subsystem such as:

- LED wall processing;
- projection;
- switcher/vision mix;
- playback/media servers;
- camera engineering;
- video/data distribution;
- corporate room AV.

### Lead level — Video Crew Chief / Head Video / Technical Lead

PRG requires Video Crew Chiefs to independently plan, execute, and monitor video technical requirements and documents higher experience bands based on both years and team size. Source: [PRG Crew Portal](https://crew.prg.com/en/).

## Corporate AV difference

Corporate AV often collapses audio, lighting, and video into broad technical ranks. Encore’s current ladder includes Event Technician, Event Technical Lead, Senior Technical Lead, Technical Supervisor, Operations Manager, and Director of Event Technology. Source: [Encore jobs](https://jobs.encoreglobal.com/en/search_jobs).

Therefore, a corporate technician may advance in organizational responsibility without adopting a touring-style “V1/V2” title.

## Credentials

There is no ETCP video certification.

AVIXA provides the strongest cross-vendor credential structure:

- CTS — general AV competency;
- CTS-I — installation;
- CTS-D — design.

AVIXA explicitly says CTS must be earned before CTS-I or CTS-D. Source: [AVIXA certification overview](https://www.avixa.org/training-certification/certification).

These are meaningful professional credentials but **not equivalent to V2 → V1 → Video Crew Chief titles**.

Dante’s current Levels 1–3 increasingly span AV-over-IP concepts beyond audio alone, but remain technology/network credentials rather than job classifications. Source: [Audinate](https://www.audinate.com/press/audinate-launches-completely-revamped-dante-training-program/).

### Bottom line — Video

**Teach:** assistant/technician → independent subsystem specialist → small-show lead → crew chief/head video.  
**Also teach:** the field branches heavily into LED, projection, switching, media servers, camera, and networks.

# 4. Rigging

Rigging has the clearest combination of recognized job language, safety thresholds, and national personnel certification.

## Real titles found

- Ground Rigger
- High Rigger / Up-Rigger
- Rigger
- Arena Rigger
- Theatre Rigger
- Senior Rigger
- Lead Rigger
- Head Rigger
- Rigging Coordinator / Rigging Manager
- Automation Specialist / Automation & Performance Rigger

The terms **ground rigger, high/up rigger, and head rigger are real**.

Nasco describes Ground Riggers as assisting high riggers from the ground under plans laid out by the Head Rigger, while the Head Rigger is responsible for installation and hoisting and directs both high and ground riggers. Source: [Nasco Special Rigging](https://www.nasco.ca/service-type/special-rigging-projects/).

Universal Orlando’s 2026 rigging postings explicitly distinguish a **Tech III acting as ground rigger** and a **Tech II functioning as the up rigger**. Source: [Universal Rigging Opportunities](https://careers.pageuppeople.com/851/cw/en-us/job/655912/rigging-opportunities-universal-studios-and-islands-of-adventure).

Encore’s 2026 postings distinguish Event Rigger and Event Senior Rigger. The Event Rigger requires 500+ hours of onsite rigging experience; the Senior Rigger requires 2,000+ hours and may lead assigned rigging calls, with preference for ETCP certification. Sources: [Encore Event Rigger](https://jobs.encoreglobal.com/fr/emploi/mississauga/event-rigger-toronto/6228/95423040032) and [Encore Senior Rigger](https://jobs.encoreglobal.com/en/job/brampton/event-senior-rigger-toronto/6228/95423039232).

PRG lists **Rigger, Head Rigger, and Automation Specialist** as separate crew skills. Source: [PRG Crew Portal](https://crew.prg.com/en/).

## Practical progression

### Assist/basic — ground rigging under competent leadership

Ground-rigging competence includes recognizing/handling hardware, motors, ropes, truss, points, communication, staging chain and equipment, spotting lifts, and understanding exclusion/fall zones. It does **not** automatically establish competence for high work or independent point selection.

### Independent rigging — qualified rigger / high or up-rigger

This requires significantly greater competence: fall protection, climbing/lift work, point establishment, hardware selection/inspection, load awareness, rope skills, truss/motor systems, plans/plots, and the ability to identify and escalate unsafe conditions.

### Lead — Senior/Lead/Head Rigger

The lead boundary is accountability for the rigging plan and crew: interpreting plots, coordinating points, assigning ground/up teams, resolving obstructions, controlling safety, checking completed work, and coordinating with production/venue engineering requirements.

## Credentials

ETCP offers two distinct rigging personnel certifications:

- **Rigger — Arena**
- **Rigger — Theatre**

ETCP explicitly treats them as separate certification examinations and requires 30 eligibility points, primarily from work experience, with formal education/apprenticeship/internship able to contribute. Sources: [ETCP general testing](https://etcp.esta.org/certify/certify.html) and [ETCP Arena exam content](https://etcp.esta.org/certify/examination_rigger_arena.html).

ETCP is **not an entry-level rigging card**. Its eligibility model is aimed at experienced technicians.

Employer-specific basic qualifications also exist. Encore uses a “Qualified-Basic Rigging certification” prerequisite in current postings, but this should not be confused with ETCP’s nationally recognized experienced-technician credential. Source: [Encore Event Rigger](https://jobs.encoreglobal.com/fr/emploi/mississauga/event-rigger-toronto/6228/95423040032).

### Bottom line — Rigging

The most defensible progression is:

**Ground Rigger → qualified independent Rigger / High or Up-Rigger → Senior/Lead Rigger → Head Rigger**, with ETCP Arena or Theatre certification as an advanced professional credential rather than a job-title ladder.

# 5. Electrics / Entertainment Power

Lighting and electrics overlap, but they should not be treated as identical. “Lighting” can mean fixture/control/show operation; “electrics” can mean temporary entertainment power distribution, feeder, distro, branch circuits, grounding/bonding, load calculations, and code/safety responsibilities.

## Real titles found

- Electrician / Stage Electrician
- Assistant Electrician
- Production Electrician
- Head Electrician
- Chief Lighting Technician / Gaffer in film/TV contexts
- Entertainment Electrician
- Portable Power Distribution Technician
- Power Technician / Generator or Distro technician in employer-specific contexts

Local 13’s 2026 postings include **Head Electrician**. Source: [IATSE Local 13](https://www.iatse13.org/).

The Operation Mincemeat tour posting defines Head Electrician as the department leader who assigns Assistant Electrician and local labor and maintains the show’s electrical/lighting systems. Source: [Head Electrician posting](https://playbill.com/job/head-electrician-operation-mincemeat-tour/c93251e5-f664-4f10-ab35-ab97fe1bed99).

## Practical progression

### Assist level

A worker can safely perform assigned cable, fixture, distro, and equipment tasks under direct supervision but is not yet independently responsible for energization, system design, load planning, code compliance, or troubleshooting hazardous conditions.

### Independent level — Electrician / Production Electrician / PPDT-level portable-power specialist

Independent work involves system understanding, load calculations, inspection, appropriate PPE and procedures, distribution configuration, troubleshooting, and compliance with applicable rules and venue/AHJ requirements.

### Lead level — Head Electrician / Production Electrician / electrical department head

Lead-level responsibility includes planning the system, coordinating local power and tie-in requirements, assigning crew, verifying safe operation, managing changes, and serving as the department’s accountable technical authority within the production structure.

## ETCP credentials

ETCP currently has **two electrical certifications**:

1. **Entertainment Electrician** — ETCP says this exam is designed to validate the competency of the upper third of electricians working in entertainment. It spans theatre, film/TV, arenas/stadiums, festivals, corporate events, touring, and other public-assembly environments. Source: [ETCP Entertainment Electrician](https://etcp.esta.org/certify/certify_electrical.html).
2. **Portable Power Distribution Technician (PPDT)** — targeted specifically at portable entertainment power distribution; ETCP describes it as targeting the top two-thirds of people working with that technology in corporate, trade-show, outdoor-event, theatre, and film/TV environments. Source: [ETCP PPDT](https://etcp.esta.org/certify/certify_ppdt.html).

ETCP requires 30 eligibility points for Entertainment Electrician and 20 for PPDT. Source: [ETCP certification requirements](https://etcp.esta.org/certify/certify.html).

IATSE Local 728 actively promotes both ETCP electrical certifications and provides exam preparation, electrical theory, power, console, and other technical training. Sources: [Local 728 ETCP](https://www.iatse728.org/members/lms/classes/etcp-entertainment-electrician-and-portable-power-technician-certifications) and [Local 728 Training](https://www.iatse728.org/members/lms).

## Apprenticeship nuance

Some IATSE stagecraft apprenticeships teach electrical/lighting craft, but there is no single national entertainment-electrical apprentice license comparable to construction electrician licensing. Local electrical licensing requirements can also interact with entertainment work and must be treated separately from ETCP.

### Bottom line — Electrics

**Assist under supervision → independently competent entertainment electrician / power technician → Head/Production Electrician**, with ETCP Entertainment Electrician and PPDT providing the strongest national personnel credentials in this department.

# 6. Carpentry / Scenic / Deck

Carpentry is one of the areas where job language changes sharply by theatre, touring, arena show, fabrication shop, and concert/festival staging environment.

## Real titles found

- Stage Carpenter
- Carpenter / Scenic Carpenter
- Carpenter-Welder
- Deck Carpenter
- Flyperson / Flyman
- Automation Carpenter / Assistant Carpenter–Automation
- Assistant Carpenter
- Production Carpenter
- Head Carpenter
- Technical Director / Assistant Technical Director in theatre/scenic-production organizations
- Stagehand Carpenter on arena tours

Current evidence:

- A 2026 IATSE Local 15-represented **Assistant Carpenter** at Seattle’s 5th Avenue Theatre reports to the Head Carpenter, supervises carpentry crews during load-in/out, leads/delegates tasks, covers the Head Carpenter when absent, and works across carpentry/rigging/automation/rail interfaces. Source: [5th Avenue Assistant Carpenter posting](https://playbill.com/job/assistant-carpenter/795f224a-7b3d-4213-a386-3157d5ad19d1).
- The 2026 Operation Mincemeat tour advertises both **Head Carpenter and Assistant Carpenter**, with Head at $2,000/week and Assistant at $1,800/week under an IATSE Pink contract. The Assistant leads local crew while the Head is handling other portions of the load-in; the Head advances venues, schedules road labor/trucking, owns truck packs, and coordinates all technical elements. Sources: [Assistant Carpenter](https://playbill.com/job/assistant-carpenter-operation-mincemeat-tour/bf27a195-b543-497b-b9fe-1035169e27f0) and [Head Carpenter](https://playbill.com/job/head-carpenter-operation-mincemeat-tour/6152ac8b-c49c-476a-9e4b-b5ddeebff68c).
- Touring postings also separately use **Deck Carpenter** and **Flyman/Flyperson**, demonstrating branch roles within the carpentry/stage department. Sources: [Deck Carpenter](https://playbill.com/job/deck-carpenter/3d4b1d5a-36b4-4ea5-b06f-31fb8d0d5a62) and [Flyman](https://playbill.com/job/flyman/5b217721-26f6-4961-8fc7-9a45d133491f).
- Feld Entertainment uses **Stagehand Carpenter** on large arena touring productions; the role assembles/disassembles the stage set, directs local crews, performs maintenance, and runs performance cues. Source: [Feld Stagehand Carpenter](https://www.feldentertainment.com/careers/job/b0205ee4-d785-42df-a41a-2c10d203db8f/).

## Practical progression

### Assist/basic — carpentry hand / scenic shop helper / stagehand carpenter under direction

Expected skills can include tool safety, material handling, reading simple marks/layouts, hardware, basic scenic assembly, soft goods, deck/platform installation, and working safely around automation/rigging interfaces.

### Independent — Carpenter / Deck Carpenter / Flyperson / Automation Carpenter / Scenic Carpenter

The worker can execute assigned builds/installations/maintenance and often supervise a local crew on their portion of the show.

### Lead — Assistant Carpenter → Head Carpenter / Production Carpenter

The evidence shows that **Assistant Carpenter is already a leadership role on many professional tours and theatres**, not merely a novice helper. This is important for course design.

Head Carpenter is clearly a top operational touring/theatre title. The 2026 Head Carpenter posting requires five years of touring experience as a carpenter or department head and three years as a department head, while giving the role responsibility for advance work, labor schedules, trucking, truck packs, installation, maintenance, and interdepartmental coordination. Source: [Head Carpenter posting](https://playbill.com/job/head-carpenter-operation-mincemeat-tour/6152ac8b-c49c-476a-9e4b-b5ddeebff68c).

**Production Carpenter** can sit above or alongside Head Carpenter during initial production/build/tech depending on producing structure; it should not be assumed to mean the same thing at every employer.

## Credentials

There is **no national ETCP-equivalent personnel certification for entertainment carpenters or stage carpenters**.

Useful credentials may include welding qualifications, lift/forklift/MEWP training, fall protection, OSHA training, manufacturer/automation training, or local union skill certifications, but none functions as a universal “Certified Stage Carpenter Level I/II/III.”

## Apprenticeship

Carpentry does appear within formal stagecraft apprenticeships. Local One’s apprenticeship explicitly includes carpentry, and Local 470 describes apprentices progressing toward Journeymen Stagehand status. Sources: [Local One](https://www.iatselocaloneapprenticeship.com/) and [Local 470](https://www.ia470.com/education.html).

### Bottom line — Carpentry

A useful professional model is:

**Carpentry assist/basic stagehand → Carpenter / Deck / Fly / Automation specialty → Assistant Carpenter → Head Carpenter**, with Production Carpenter/Technical Director treated as production-structure branches rather than universal next ranks.

# 7. Staging / Stage Building

“Staging” is especially inconsistent. It can mean portable deck/stage construction at concerts and festivals, stage mechanics and venue systems, scenic/stage-set work, or a combined rigging-and-staging technical department.

## Real titles found

- Stagehand / Stage Builder
- Staging Technician
- Tech III / Tech II / Tech I — Staging (employer-specific)
- Stage Carpenter
- Stage Builder / Decoration-Stage Builder
- Scenic Specialist
- Lead Staging Technician / Lead Tech (employer-specific)
- Staging Supervisor / Technical Supervisor
- Stage/Scenic Crew Chief in some companies

PRG’s crew portal uses **Stage Builder** and publishes explicit experience bands:

- relevant formal training or ~2 years comparable experience;
- 3+ years plus responsibility for sections of event builds;
- 5+ years plus responsibility as team leader for complex set/stage builds.

Source: [PRG Crew Portal](https://crew.prg.com/en/).

Universal Orlando currently uses numbered staging titles. Its 2026 **Tech II Staging** role is the point of contact for an assigned venue/project/event, is accountable for installation/setup/storage/strike/transport, troubleshoots completion of team objectives, and assists Tech I with inventory/maintenance. Source: [Universal Tech II Staging](https://careers.pageuppeople.com/851/cw/en-us/job/663533/tech-ii-staging-technical-entertainment-events).

This illustrates why numerical ranks must not be assumed to have a universal direction: Universal’s technical numbering does not necessarily map to the “I = entry, III = senior” assumption used by other employers.

Encore’s current **Scenic Specialist II** posting describes the role as a **senior-level position** based on proven experience and qualifications, acting as a leader in the warehouse and on show site and supervising assigned personnel. Source: [Encore Scenic Specialist II](https://jobs.encoreglobal.com/fr/emploi/mississauga/scenic-specialist-ii/6228/96833107072).

## Practical progression

### Assist/basic — Stagehand / Stage Builder assistant

- moves and sorts decks/legs/rails/ramps/ground support;
- follows build sequence and layout;
- uses hand tools safely;
- recognizes components and hardware;
- works under a lead or competent installer.

### Independent — Stage Builder / Staging Technician / Stage Carpenter

- reads layouts;
- independently assembles common systems within training/authorization;
- recognizes structural/connection problems;
- uses lifts or powered equipment only when separately trained/authorized;
- can own a defined section of build/strike.

### Lead — lead stage builder / staging lead / crew chief / supervisor

- interprets plans;
- divides work among crew;
- controls build sequence;
- coordinates rigging, scenic, barricade, flooring, and production interfaces;
- checks completed sections;
- manages time, safety, and corrections.

## Credentials

There is **no national staging personnel certification equivalent to ETCP rigging**.

Relevant credentials are task-specific: lift/MEWP/forklift, fall protection, OSHA, manufacturer stage-system training, welding where applicable, and rigging credentials when the person crosses into rigging scope.

### Bottom line — Staging

**Assist → independent stage builder/staging technician → section lead → staging crew chief/supervisor**, but names are highly employer-specific. This department should be explicitly labeled **non-standardized** in Crew Blueprint.

# 8. Formal apprentice / journeyman structures — what actually exists

The industry does contain real apprentice/journeyman structures, but they are primarily **local-union stagecraft structures**, not one national departmental ladder.

## Strong examples

### IATSE Local One — New York

Local One operates a 2–3 year apprenticeship with hands-on work in theatres, TV studios, lighting/sound shops, and scenic fabrication facilities. It explicitly develops skills across lighting, sound, rigging, carpentry, and emerging technologies and leads to full membership. Source: [Local One Apprenticeship](https://www.iatselocaloneapprenticeship.com/).

### IATSE Local 470 — Wisconsin

Local 470 states that it has an active apprentice-member training program and that its Stagehand Primer is an aid to apprentices studying to become **Journeymen Stagehands**. Source: [Local 470 Education](https://www.ia470.com/education.html).

### IATSE Local 38

Local 38 offers both direct application for experienced workers seeking **Journeyperson membership** and an Apprenticeship Program intended to develop workers toward journeyperson membership. Source: [Local 38 membership](https://www.iatse38.org/mobile/index.cfm?page=How20To20Join&zone=%2Funionactive%2Fview_page.cfm).

### IATSE Local 28

Local 28 uses **Junior member** and **Journeyman** membership levels in its Stagehand Craft category. Source: [Local 28 membership](https://www.iatse28.org/?page=Becoming20a20member20of20Local2028&zone=%2Funionactive%2Fview_page.cfm).

## What this means

Crew Blueprint should **not** say “the U.S. stagehand industry uses apprentice → journeyman → master.” That would be false nationally.

It can accurately say:

> Some IATSE locals maintain formal apprentice/journeyperson or junior/journeyman structures, but the structure, hours, tests, membership rules, and department treatment are local-specific.

# 10. Cross-department competency model for The Crew Blueprint

Because titles are inconsistent, the curriculum should not make title alone the competency measure. A stronger model is to classify learners by **scope of work and accountability**.

## Level A — Awareness / assist

Worker can:

- identify department equipment and hazards;
- follow department-specific instructions;
- safely perform defined handling/setup tasks;
- recognize when a task exceeds training or authorization;
- communicate completion/problems to the responsible technician.

This is the appropriate educational boundary for broad introductory stagehand training.

## Level B — Technician under limited supervision

Worker can:

- perform routine setup/strike and maintenance;
- read basic plots, labels, patch sheets, drawings, or system documentation relevant to the department;
- troubleshoot common problems;
- operate assigned equipment after training;
- work independently on a defined subsystem or task.

## Level C — Independent operator / system technician

Worker can:

- own an assigned system, operating position, or technical area;
- make routine technical decisions within scope;
- troubleshoot failures under show pressure;
- coordinate with adjacent departments;
- verify work before operation;
- supervise hands assisting the task.

Examples: A1/FOH or monitor engineer, lighting programmer/operator, video system specialist, qualified rigger, electrician, deck carpenter, independent stage builder.

## Level D — Lead / crew chief / department head

Worker can:

- plan and advance departmental work;
- allocate labor;
- set sequence and priorities;
- verify safety and quality;
- resolve non-routine technical problems;
- communicate with production management, venue management, touring department heads, designers, or clients;
- train/evaluate less-experienced workers;
- accept accountability for department delivery.

Examples: Audio Crew Chief, Head Audio, Lighting Crew Chief, Head Electrician, Video Crew Chief, Head Video, Head Rigger, Head Carpenter, staging supervisor/lead.

## Level E — design / systems / management branch

Not every worker proceeds here, and it is not always “higher” in a simple craft hierarchy.

Examples:

- Audio Designer / Systems Engineer
- Lighting Designer
- Video Designer / Media Designer / Video Director
- Production Rigger / Rigging Manager
- Production Electrician
- Production Carpenter / Technical Director
- Technical Manager / Production Manager / Director of Event Technology

This level should be shown as **branching career architecture**, not a single vertical ladder.

# 12. Context differences

## Touring

Touring titles are often highly specialized because the same technicians travel with the system from city to city. Department heads frequently **advance** venues, supervise local labor, maintain truck packs, and protect consistency across venues. Head Carpenter and Head Electrician postings demonstrate this clearly.

## Arena / festival

Large calls split local labor more aggressively into departmental crews, and rigging commonly distinguishes ground and high/up work. Touring specialists may direct large pools of local hands. System ownership and labor leadership become major markers of seniority.

## Corporate AV

Corporate/hotel AV employers often prefer broad organizational titles: Technician → Technical Lead → Senior Technical Lead → Supervisor/Manager/Director. A worker may operate audio, video, and basic lighting rather than belonging to one touring-style department. Encore’s current job architecture is a strong example.

## Theatre / resident venue

Theatre often preserves traditional department-head titles: Head Carpenter, Assistant Carpenter, Head Electrician, Head Audio/Sound, fly/rail/deck roles. Local union classifications and house practice matter heavily.

# 13. Important cautions for curriculum language

1. **Do not equate years with competence.** Employers use years as screening proxies, but actual advancement depends on system knowledge, troubleshooting, safety, judgment, leadership, and proven reliability.
2. **Do not present every specialty as a promotion.** FOH, monitors, RF, programming, systems, LED, media servers, fly, automation, etc. are often parallel branches.
3. **Do not teach “certified” casually.** ETCP is a real personnel-certification program. OSHA Outreach cards, lift cards, vendor training, and course completion certificates are different things.
4. **Do not imply AVIXA CTS equals live-show A1 qualification.** CTS demonstrates AV knowledge; employers still determine show-specific competence.
5. **Do not imply ETCP is entry-level.** ETCP eligibility is experience-based and is intended for experienced entertainment technicians.
6. **Do not imply apprentice/journeyman language is universal.** It exists in specific IATSE locals but is not a national stagecraft rank system.
7. **Treat authorization separately from knowledge.** A person may understand a system and still require employer/site authorization, local licensing, or equipment-specific qualification before performing the task.

# Final conclusion

The U.S. live-events industry has **real departmental progression**, but it is best understood as a competency graph rather than a universal title ladder.

The most defensible general rule is:

> **Assist safely → own a defined technical system independently → lead people and accept departmental accountability → branch into advanced systems, design, or management.**

Rigging and electrics have the clearest national certification structure through ETCP. Audio and video have credible AVIXA and technology/vendor credentials, but job titles remain employer- and production-dependent. Lighting has strong operational and electrical hierarchies but no universal national lighting-tech rank. Carpentry and staging have clearly documented professional leadership roles but no national personnel-certification ladder.

For The Crew Blueprint, the safest and most accurate architecture is therefore to teach **competency levels first and map multiple real-world titles onto each level**, rather than claiming that one title sequence applies everywhere in U.S. live events.
