**Purpose:** Preserve RQ-400's valid coverage findings while preventing a learner-path gap from being misread as permission to create unsafe or authority-expanding instruction.

---

# 1. What RQ-400 gets right

RQ-400 correctly establishes that:

- one universal live-events career ladder is inaccurate;
- department progression must be represented separately;
- Lighting, Audio, Video and Staging/Carpentry already have visible early/system/lead/advanced learner routes;
- Rigging has no dedicated learner-facing entry/support route;
- Electrics has no dedicated Department Support / Department Systems sequence;
- Backline/Props/Wardrobe historically rested on thinner role/progression evidence;
- Camera/broadcast, logistics, special systems and production-role branches were underrepresented in the learner-facing catalog.

These are valid **coverage observations**.

# 3. Rigging — coverage gap versus instructional boundary

RQ-400's useful finding is:

> The learner-facing catalog has no dedicated entry route for someone trying to understand the Rigging department before advanced/lead material.

That does **not** require Crew Blueprint to create a novice physical-rigging qualification course.

## Canonical internal learner route

Crew Blueprint may teach, at awareness/support level:

- Rigging roles and chain of command;
- rigging-zone and overhead-work recognition;
- equipment/hardware **category recognition** without selection instructions;
- why identification, ratings, inspection status, load paths and documentation matter;
- communication/handoff expectations;
- how touring/arena/theatre/house authority can differ;
- explicit stop/escalate boundaries;
- external training/experience/credential pathways.

## External gate

`GATE-RIG-TRAINING/AUTH`

Crew Blueprint does not internally authorize or qualify:

- climbing/work at height;
- overhead attachment decisions;
- point selection;
- hoist operation;
- rigging-hardware selection/assembly decisions;
- load calculations;
- inspection/signoff;
- rescue responsibilities;
- other employer/venue/qualified-rigger controlled work.

## Architecture disposition

**RQ-400 gap accepted; implementation reframed.**

The eventual entry learner path can be a **Rigging Awareness / Ground-Support Interface** path if the evidence/validation supports it. It must terminate at the external gate rather than mimic the normal C1→C2→operator ladder used by less controlled departments.

# 4. Electrics / Production Power — coverage gap versus instructional boundary

RQ-400 correctly identifies an asymmetry: learner-facing material jumps between general electrical awareness, Lead, and advanced load-planning literacy without a clear department-entry path.

## Canonical internal learner route

Crew Blueprint may teach:

- electrical/production-power role recognition;
- hazard recognition;
- distribution-system category/one-line literacy;
- cable-route/protection interfaces;
- terminology and documentation;
- codes/standards/AHJ/employer authority layers;
- power-quality symptom awareness;
- when to stop and escalate;
- external qualification/training routes.

## External gate

`GATE-ELC-TRAINING/AUTH`

Crew Blueprint does not internally authorize or qualify:

- connecting or disconnecting energized production distribution;
- testing/verification requiring a qualified electrical role;
- energization;
- generator configuration;
- live fault work;
- licensed/AHJ-controlled functions;
- other employer/jurisdiction-specific qualified-person work.

## Architecture disposition

**RQ-400 gap accepted; implementation bounded.**

A dedicated Electrics support/systems learner path may ultimately make sense, but it must be structured around **role/system/authority literacy**, not an online route to electrical authorization.

# 5. Backline / Props / Wardrobe update

RQ-400 correctly recorded that this combined pathway had thinner primary progression evidence than the seven Package-02 departments.

MDQ-080–083 now materially closes that research gap.

RQ-400's evidence warning therefore remains useful but is no longer the full research state.

# 7. Canonical interpretation of a 'gap'

From this point forward, every coverage gap must be classified as one of:

1. **Research gap** — insufficient evidence to model the subject.
2. **Course/build gap** — evidence exists, learner-facing content does not.
3. **Validation gap** — content exists, practitioner/learner/model/jurisdiction evidence is incomplete.
4. **External-boundary gap** — the real workforce has a role, but Crew Blueprint deliberately should not provide operational qualification for it.
5. **Out-of-scope segment** — real work exists but does not currently justify curriculum investment.

This prevents `no course exists` from automatically becoming `build a course exactly like the other departments`.

# 8. Final reconciliation decision

RQ-400 remains a valid canonical historical coverage analysis.

The **cross-domain competency graph + MDQ research + this reconciliation** control future interpretation of its recommendations.

In particular:

> **Rigging and Electrics need clearer learner entry routes if future structure synthesis confirms them, but those routes must respect their external qualification/authorization gates rather than manufacture internal novice operator pathways.**

No current live page is changed by this reconciliation.
