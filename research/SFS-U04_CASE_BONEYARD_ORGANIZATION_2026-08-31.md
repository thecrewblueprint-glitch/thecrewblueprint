# SFS-U04 — Case / Boneyard / Work-Area Organization

**Prepared:** 2026-08-31  
**Status:** Source packet complete enough for bounded course drafting; venue/department review remains required  
**Domain:** `D-FLD` Stagehand Field Skills  
**Recommended tier:** `T2` Field Skill  
**Authority:** assigned material staging/organization only; no independent truck-pack, structural stacking, fire/life-safety, or department-ownership decisions

## 1. Research question

What can The Crew Blueprint responsibly teach a new stagehand about parking, staging, labeling, sorting, and organizing road cases and other production materials so the crew remains efficient without blocking egress, aisles, doors, emergency equipment, department access, or active material flow?

## 2. Executive finding

This is a high-value universal Field Skill because good case/work-area organization improves both production flow and basic workplace safety.

The learner outcome is:

> **Place assigned cases/materials where the responsible lead directs, preserve labels and destinations, keep required paths and exits clear, avoid unstable or improvised storage, and report unclear ownership/damage rather than inventing a location or stack.**

The course should teach organization as an **assigned workflow skill**, not as permission to redesign the boneyard, decide the truck pack, block a route because it seems unused, or create an unsupported stack.

## 3. Evidence base

### OSHA 1910.176 — materials handling and storage

OSHA requires sufficient clearances for aisles, loading docks, doorways, turns and passageways where mechanical material handling is used. It also requires aisles/passageways to remain clear and stored materials to remain stable and secure against sliding or collapse.

Source: OSHA 1910.176  
https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.176

### OSHA 1910.22 — clean and orderly walking-working surfaces

OSHA requires workplaces, passageways, storerooms and walking-working surfaces to be maintained in clean and orderly condition and free of recognized surface hazards.

Source: OSHA 1910.22  
https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.22

### OSHA 1910.37 — exit routes

OSHA requires exit routes to remain free and unobstructed and prohibits placing equipment/materials in the exit route.

Source: OSHA 1910.37  
https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.37

## 4. Terminology boundary

The course may explain common industry terms such as:

- case parking/staging;
- workbox;
- boneyard/case storage area;
- department zone;
- dock/traffic lane;
- empty case versus show-critical case;
- label/ID/destination marking.

Do not present any one employer/local's exact vocabulary or boneyard layout as universal.

## 5. Transferable organization principles

The learner can be taught to:

1. Follow the assigned destination/zone.
2. Keep labels/IDs visible when practical and directed.
3. Preserve access to cases/materials the crew still needs.
4. Keep required aisles, passageways, doors and exit routes clear.
5. Avoid blocking fire/life-safety equipment, loading routes, department access or active work zones.
6. Keep staged materials stable and avoid creating improvised stacks whose stability/acceptance has not been approved.
7. Separate damaged/uncertain/mislabeled material for reporting rather than guessing.
8. Re-check the area after a move because a formerly clear path may become blocked as the call changes.

## 6. What the learner should not decide independently

- the final truck pack;
- load distribution or cargo-securement acceptance;
- structural load limits for a platform/floor/truck;
- whether cases may be stacked beyond the actual equipment/site rules;
- whether an exit/door/aisle may be temporarily blocked;
- department ownership when labeling is unclear;
- disposal of unidentified gear or materials;
- re-labeling technical equipment without direction;
- changes to fire/life-safety routes.

## 7. Stop-and-ask conditions

Stop and clarify when:

- the assigned area would obstruct an exit, aisle, doorway or emergency equipment;
- the destination conflicts with another department's active access;
- the case/material is damaged, unstable, leaking, unusually heavy, or missing identification;
- the only apparent option requires unstable stacking or an improvised support;
- the route/destination changes without a clear owner;
- a case appears show-critical or still in active use;
- a public or vehicle route is becoming constrained;
- the learner is asked to make a truck-pack/load-distribution decision outside the assignment.

## 8. What good observed practice looks like

A controlled practical can record whether the learner:

- identifies the assigned destination;
- reads/preserves the label or asks when unclear;
- parks/stages the material without uncontrolled movement;
- maintains required aisle/door/exit access;
- keeps the area orderly enough for the next work step;
- avoids unstable/improvised storage;
- reports conflicts/damage/unknown ownership;
- can explain who controls the final location/stack/pack decision.

## 9. Practice record

Record:

- `course_id`: `C-FLD-CASE-BONEYARD-ORG`
- venue/work-area context;
- case/material family;
- destination/zone;
- observer and role;
- any site-specific aisle/egress/parking rules;
- result: pass / rework / stop;
- comments;
- date.

Observed practice does not grant authority to redesign storage, stack beyond directed limits, or control truck/load plans.

## 10. Visual requirements

A strong visual course should include:

1. clear versus blocked aisle examples;
2. acceptable designated case zone versus doorway/exit obstruction;
3. visible label/destination examples;
4. orderly boneyard versus chaotic mixed-access area;
5. stable floor parking versus visibly unstable/improvised stacking;
6. department access and active-traffic conflicts;
7. decision diagram: `assigned location clear? → place → re-check / conflict? → stop and ask`.

## 11. Matrix handoff

Suggested claims:

- `CL-FLD-CASE-ORG-0001` — aisles/passageways required for work/material movement must remain clear.
- `CL-FLD-CASE-ORG-0002` — exit routes may not be obstructed by stored equipment/material.
- `CL-FLD-CASE-ORG-0003` — stored material should not create instability/sliding/collapse hazards.
- `CL-FLD-CASE-ORG-0004` — case/material destination remains an assigned production decision.
- `CL-FLD-CASE-ORG-0005` — unclear/damaged/unidentified material is reported rather than independently reclassified.

## 12. Curriculum recommendation

**Build as a Core Field Skill.**

It may later be partially integrated with Road Case Movement and Cart/Dolly Movement in the Stagehand Core dashboard, but it should retain its own observed-practice state because organization/handoff is a distinct behavior from simply moving an object.
