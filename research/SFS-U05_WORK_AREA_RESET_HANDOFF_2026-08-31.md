# SFS-U05 — Work-Area Reset and Handoff

**Prepared:** 2026-08-31  
**Status:** Source packet complete enough for bounded course drafting; venue/employer review remains required  
**Domain:** `D-FLD` Stagehand Field Skills  
**Recommended tier:** `T2` Field Skill  
**Authority:** housekeeping, path protection, tool/material return and directed handoff only; no fire/life-safety signoff or department closeout authority

## 1. Research question

What can The Crew Blueprint responsibly teach a new stagehand about resetting a work area during and after a task so the next crew operation can proceed safely and efficiently, while keeping final acceptance with the responsible lead/venue/employer?

## 2. Executive finding

This should be a concise universal Stagehand Core Field Skill.

The observable learner outcome is:

> **Leave the assigned work area in the condition the responsible lead expects: remove or stage loose materials as directed, keep walking/working and exit paths clear, return borrowed tools/materials to the correct owner, identify damage or unresolved conditions, and complete a clear handoff rather than assuming the area is finished.**

The course should not imply that a learner can declare a stage, venue, emergency route, technical system, or department “safe/closed/accepted” merely because cleanup is complete.

## 3. Evidence base

### OSHA 1910.22 — orderly walking-working surfaces

OSHA requires workplaces, passageways, storerooms and walking-working surfaces to be kept clean and orderly and free of recognized hazards.

Source: OSHA 1910.22  
https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.22

### OSHA 1910.37 — unobstructed exit routes

OSHA requires exit routes to remain free and unobstructed; materials or equipment may not be placed in the exit route.

Source: OSHA 1910.37  
https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.37

### OSHA 1910.176 — storage-area housekeeping and clear aisles

OSHA requires aisles/passageways used around material handling to remain clear and storage areas to remain free from accumulations that create hazards.

Source: OSHA 1910.176  
https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.176

### Existing Crew Blueprint evidence

Stagehand Fundamentals and Packages 15, 18, 20 and 20A already support cleanup/reset, organized work areas, final walkthrough concepts, clear paths, borrowed-item/tool accountability and damage reporting. This packet converts those behaviors into a repeatable observed skill instead of leaving them as soft-skill prose.

## 4. The work-area reset loop

A learner may be taught this transferable loop:

1. **Pause and scan** — identify loose material, packaging, tape/debris, tools, cable/gear, blocked paths, damage and items that do not belong to the current work package.
2. **Sort by owner/destination** — return or stage items according to the responsible crew/department's direction.
3. **Clear required paths** — preserve aisles, doors, exit routes, stairs/ramps, loading routes and designated work paths.
4. **Protect the next work state** — do not move show-critical, preset, technical or intentionally staged equipment merely because the area looks cluttered.
5. **Report unresolved conditions** — damage, missing items, unclear ownership, spills, blocked access or other conflicts go to the appropriate lead.
6. **Handoff** — tell the responsible lead what was completed, what remains and what was found.

## 5. Why “clean it up” is not enough

A production work area can look visually tidy while still being wrong for the next department or work phase.

Examples of mistakes the course should prevent:

- moving deliberately staged equipment out of position;
- throwing away unidentified hardware, labels or packaging needed for return/shipping;
- placing empty cases in an exit/door/traffic route;
- mixing another department's cable/tools into the wrong destination;
- hiding damaged equipment instead of reporting it;
- assuming borrowed tools have been returned without verifying the owner;
- declaring a task complete while another dependency remains unresolved.

The core habit is **reset + verify + handoff**, not simply “make it look clean.”

## 6. What the learner may act on directly

Under ordinary assignment, a stagehand can generally be taught to:

- collect ordinary debris/packaging as directed;
- organize loose non-show-critical materials in the assigned zone;
- return known borrowed tools/items to the designated owner/location;
- keep clear paths from becoming re-blocked;
- stage empty cases/materials where directed;
- report obvious damage, spills, missing items or access conflicts;
- communicate task status to the responsible lead.

## 7. What the learner should not decide independently

- move technical/show-critical equipment whose ownership/state is unclear;
- discard unknown parts, labels, paperwork or consumables;
- change cable routes, presets, barricades, public routes or emergency arrangements;
- declare fire/life-safety compliance;
- sign off a department/system as complete;
- repair damaged equipment;
- resolve a spill/contamination condition requiring a site-specific response they have not been assigned/trained for;
- override the next department's staging requirements.

## 8. Stop-and-ask conditions

Stop and clarify when:

- an item has unclear ownership/destination;
- cleanup would require moving a preset/show-critical/connected system;
- an exit, aisle, door, stair, ramp or emergency-equipment access is blocked and the learner cannot resolve it within the assignment;
- damaged/leaking/contaminated equipment or material is found;
- a tool/part appears missing or borrowed from another crew;
- the work area contains a condition outside ordinary housekeeping responsibility;
- another department is actively using the area or has conflicting staging needs;
- the learner is asked to provide final technical, safety or venue acceptance.

## 9. What good observed practice looks like

A controlled practical can record whether the learner:

- performs a deliberate work-area scan;
- distinguishes obvious debris from production materials;
- returns/stages items by known ownership/destination;
- maintains required paths and access;
- preserves intentionally staged/show-critical equipment;
- identifies and reports unresolved conditions;
- gives a concise handoff: completed / remaining / problem found.

## 10. Practice record

Record:

- `course_id`: `C-FLD-WORK-AREA-RESET`
- work-area context;
- task/work package;
- departments present;
- observer and role;
- site-specific housekeeping/egress constraints;
- result: pass / rework / stop;
- unresolved conditions reported;
- comments;
- date.

Observed practice demonstrates the reset/handoff behavior only. It does not prove department or venue closeout authority.

## 11. Visual requirements

A strong course should use before/after visuals showing:

1. blocked versus clear work/exit paths;
2. ordinary debris versus “do not move without asking” production items;
3. mixed tools/materials versus owner/destination sorting;
4. empty-case staging versus doorway/aisle blockage;
5. damage-report example;
6. three-part handoff card: `done / remains / issue`;
7. final decision graphic: `Do I know what this is and where it belongs?` → yes: follow assignment; no: ask.

## 12. Matrix handoff

Suggested claims:

- `CL-FLD-RESET-0001` — walking-working surfaces should remain orderly and free of recognized hazards.
- `CL-FLD-RESET-0002` — exit routes must remain unobstructed.
- `CL-FLD-RESET-0003` — storage/work areas should not accumulate material that creates trip/fire/other hazards.
- `CL-FLD-RESET-0004` — a stagehand should preserve intentionally staged/show-critical items whose ownership/state is not theirs to change.
- `CL-FLD-RESET-0005` — completion requires a handoff to the responsible lead when the learner does not own final acceptance.

## 13. Curriculum recommendation

**Build as a Core Field Skill.**

Keep it short and highly visual. It should appear near the end of the universal core sequence because it teaches the learner how to finish any assigned work package professionally and leave the next crew in a better state.
