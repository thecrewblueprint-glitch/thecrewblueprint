# Frontier Evidence — Production-Management Artifacts, Control Loops, and Closeout

**Prepared:** 2026-08-31  
**Status:** Draftable systems/management extension

## Purpose

The existing Production Management corpus establishes role architecture, advance, labor/vendor/venue coordination, documents, communication, change, show-day control, and closeout. The remaining instructional gap is artifact fluency: learners need to see how a production manager turns information into a controlled operating picture.

## Management control loop

A generic, transferable PM loop is:

**request / requirement → owner → current version → due time → dependency → status → decision/change → communication → verification → closeout**

This is a Crew Blueprint organizational model, not a claim that every production uses identical forms or titles.

## Draftable artifact families

### Advance packet / responsibility matrix
Learner should recognize:
- event/show identifiers and revision date;
- contacts and decision owners;
- venue/site constraints;
- departmental requirements and open questions;
- labor, transport, parking/dock, credential/access, schedule, and vendor dependencies;
- explicit unresolved items.

### Labor-call and schedule plan
Learner should distinguish:
- call time from task duration;
- crew/department assignment from individual authorization;
- dependencies between dock, staging, rigging, power, lighting/audio/video, rehearsal/show, and strike;
- contingency/slack from assumed free time;
- change communication and acknowledgement.

### Quote / budget / change log
Learner should understand:
- estimate vs approved scope vs actual cost;
- quantity/rate/unit assumptions;
- owner of approval;
- scope change, schedule change, labor change, vendor substitution, and cost impact;
- audit trail rather than silent edits.

### Show-day issue log
Record:
- timestamp;
- issue/changed condition;
- affected area;
- responsible owner;
- decision/status;
- next checkpoint;
- unresolved handoff.

### Closeout / postmortem
Separate:
- operational facts;
- financial reconciliation;
- equipment/vendor/labor unresolved items;
- safety/incident processes controlled elsewhere;
- improvement observations;
- assigned corrective actions and owners.

## Authority ceiling

Management education does not appoint a learner as PM, authorize specialist decisions, override venue/AHJ/employer authority, create contract-signing authority, approve engineering, or grant legal/financial authority. Exact artifacts and approval chains remain production/company specific.

## Recommended routes

- Production Advance Packet & Responsibility Matrix
- Labor Calls, Schedule Dependencies & Change Communication
- Budget Tracking, Quotes & Change Control
- Show-Day Issue Log, Closeout & Postmortem

## Recommended learner artifacts

Provide generic practice templates that contain no real personal/client data and no hidden legal promises. The purpose is document literacy and simulation, not a substitute for an employer's production paperwork.
