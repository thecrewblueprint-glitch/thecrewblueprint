# MDQ-080–083 — Backline, Props and Wardrobe Specialty Evidence + Split Architecture

**Prepared:** 2026-08-31  
**Status:** Source-grounded specialty map; early support can remain adjacent, deeper pathways should split  
**Domains:** `D-BKL`, `D-PRP`, `D-WRD`  
**Depends on:** MDQ-000–003, Package 11/11A, current combined Backline/Props/Wardrobe C1/C2 materials, IATSE touring/wardrobe evidence  
**Purpose:** Determine how far Backline, Props and Wardrobe can share early support education before their real job families diverge.

---

## 1. Executive decision

The evidence supports a **shared early-career interface layer** but **separate specialty pathways beyond that layer**.

These departments overlap at beginner level because a general hand needs similar behaviors:

- identify department ownership;
- preserve labels/presets/order;
- handle assigned items carefully;
- avoid unauthorized adjustments;
- report damage/missing items;
- support load-in/load-out and changeovers;
- maintain organization;
- respect performer/artist/show-critical boundaries.

They diverge quickly in systems knowledge, maintenance, show operation, performer interaction, documentation and leadership.

Recommended architecture:

- shared **Support / Department Interface** orientation is reasonable;
- Backline, Props and Wardrobe should become **separate competency graphs** at systems/specialist depth;
- advanced/lead material should not remain one combined course.

# MDQ-080 — Backline specialty map

## Role families

Depending on production/market, roles may include:

- backline support / stagehand interface;
- backline technician;
- instrument-specific technician paths;
- drum / guitar / keyboard / playback-support interfaces;
- festival/changeover backline crew;
- touring artist technician;
- backline rental/shop technician;
- backline lead/head.

## Competency families

### Early support

- instrument/amplifier/case/stand family recognition;
- artist/tech ownership awareness;
- stage-plot/input-list interface;
- assigned movement/staging;
- preserving settings/cabling/accessories unless directed;
- damage/missing-part reporting;
- changeover organization.

### Technician depth

Research should separately map:

- instrument-family setup/maintenance boundaries;
- amplification and support-system interfaces;
- tuning/intonation/maintenance as role-specific competencies;
- spares/consumables;
- stage plots / input-list coordination;
- artist preferences and repeatability;
- show/changeover support;
- shop/rental prep and return/QC.

Technical work becomes instrument- and artist-specific quickly; Crew Blueprint should not imply that general support completion makes someone an artist instrument technician.

## Sector variation

- festival/local backline provider;
- touring artist crew;
- venue/house backline;
- rehearsal/production facility;
- rental shop;
- corporate/show-band environments.

## Career map

**General Stagehand / Backline Support**  
→ **Backline Utility / Changeover Support**  
→ **Backline Technician**  
→ instrument/artist specialization, rental/shop depth, or touring path  
→ **Backline Lead / Artist Tech / Shop Lead** depending direction.

# MDQ-081 — Props specialty map

## Role families

Current touring-theatre evidence clearly treats Props as its own department, with traveling **Head Prop-Person** roles and dedicated local take-in/performance/put-out staffing on current national tours.

Roles may include:

- props load-in/run crew;
- prop technician/artisan;
- prop supervisor/master/head prop-person;
- set-dressing/furniture interface;
- props shop/build/repair roles;
- touring props roles;
- film/live-event continuity interfaces.

## Current show-operation evidence

A current 2026 props run-crew posting describes work including:

- pre-setting items before performance;
- maintaining correct placement;
- performer handoffs;
- receiving/tracking items during a show;
- storage after performance;
- inspection/reporting for repair;
- assigned paperwork.

This supports a strong **preset → handoff → tracking → reset/storage** competency loop.

## Competency families

### Early support

- identify prop/set-dressing/furniture ownership;
- preset/location labels and tracking awareness;
- careful assigned movement;
- performer handoff awareness;
- continuity/repeatability;
- storage/reset;
- report damage/missing items;
- do not independently modify show-critical props.

### Technician/specialist depth

- prop documentation/tracking;
- maintenance/repair boundaries;
- fabrication/material/artisan specialties;
- performer safety/user interface;
- consumables/food/liquid/breakaway or other special-item management at role-awareness level;
- touring/festival pack/changeover;
- rehearsal-to-performance continuity;
- shop organization/spares.

Any weapon-like, pyrotechnic, hazardous, breakaway, flame or special-effects prop belongs behind a separate controlled-work/safety boundary and is not a general Crew Blueprint practical.

## Leadership

Prop master/head responsibilities can include:

- sourcing/build coordination;
- rehearsal items;
- preset/tracking systems;
- performer/show safety coordination;
- maintenance/repair planning;
- staffing;
- storage/trucking;
- designer/stage-management/scenic handoffs;
- incident/damage reporting.

## Career map

**General Stagehand / Props Support**  
→ **Props Run Crew / Utility**  
→ **Props Technician / Artisan branch**  
→ **Props Lead / Prop Master / Head Prop-Person**.

**State:** `draftable` for ordinary prop workflow and career architecture; special/hazardous props remain external/controlled.

# MDQ-082 — Wardrobe / costume specialty map

## Current industry evidence

IATSE explicitly represents wardrobe workers as a major behind-the-scenes craft. Current national-tour Yellow Card data repeatedly identifies **Head Wardrobe** as a traveling department head and separates wardrobe staffing across take-in, performance and put-out.

Current IATSE Canada 2026 training offerings include dedicated live-entertainment workshops on:

- backstage laundry and garment/material handling;
- costume presets and quick changes;
- repairs/alterations and wardrobe emergencies.

This directly supports Wardrobe as a deep specialty rather than a small props-adjacent topic.

## Role families

- wardrobe support;
- dresser;
- stitcher;
- laundry/maintenance;
- wardrobe technician;
- wardrobe supervisor/head;
- touring wardrobe;
- costume-shop construction/alterations interfaces;
- wig/hair/makeup interfaces where separate departments exist.

## Competency families

### Early support

- costume/garment ownership and labeling;
- preset/order awareness;
- organized dressing/wardrobe areas;
- clean/dirty or ready/repair state recognition;
- careful assigned movement/storage;
- performer privacy/professional-boundary awareness;
- reporting damage or missing pieces.

### Show-operation / dresser path

Current role evidence supports:

- pre-show setup/check;
- assigned changes;
- show tracking;
- post-show reset;
- coordination with wardrobe supervisor/stage management;
- maintenance issue reporting.

### Maintenance / shop depth

- fabric/garment-care knowledge;
- laundry/care documentation;
- repairs/alterations;
- stitching skills;
- costume construction interfaces;
- inventory/tracking;
- touring packing/storage.

Exact chemical/product/process details should follow garment labels, employer procedures and qualified wardrobe practice; Crew Blueprint should emphasize material-care reasoning and documentation rather than unsafe improvisation.

## Performer-facing boundary

Wardrobe has a higher privacy/personal-boundary component than most technical departments. Curriculum should explicitly cover professionalism, consent/boundaries, changing-space etiquette and performer dignity in non-explicit workplace terms.

## Career map

**General Stagehand / Wardrobe Support**  
→ **Dresser / Laundry / Stitcher branches**  
→ **Wardrobe Technician / Touring Wardrobe**  
→ **Wardrobe Supervisor / Head Wardrobe / Costume-Shop Leadership**.

**State:** `draftable` for support/show-operation/career architecture; practitioner validation still needed.

# MDQ-083 — Combined-versus-split architecture decision

## Evidence finding

Current touring evidence strongly supports Props and Wardrobe as **independent departments** with separate heads, staffing and performance responsibilities.

Backline similarly has distinct artist/instrument/rental workflows not shared by Props or Wardrobe.

Therefore the current combined route should be treated as a temporary **early-support adjacency**, not the long-term technical architecture.

## Recommended competency architecture

### Shared interface layer

A compact shared orientation may cover:

- department ownership;
- labels/presets;
- careful handling;
- changeovers;
- performer/artist/show-critical boundaries;
- damage/missing-item reporting;
- storage/reset;
- general load-in/load-out interface.

### Split immediately after shared support

**Backline**
→ instrument/artist/rental/changeover systems.

**Props**
→ preset/tracking/run-crew/artisan/prop-master systems.

**Wardrobe**
→ dresser/laundry/stitching/costume-maintenance/supervisor systems.

## Learning-tier implication

- T3 Department Support: may share a common gateway with department-specific examples.
- T4 Department Systems: split into three pathways.
- T5 Lead and beyond: definitely separate.
- Specialist/hazardous props: separate external/controlled boundary.

## Site-structure implication

Do not decide the final learner-facing number of courses yet. The evidence decision is only that the **underlying competency/source model must represent three separate domains beyond the initial support interface**.
