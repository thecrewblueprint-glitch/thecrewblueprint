# Research Package 46 — Stagehand Field Skills: Over-Under Cable Coiling

**RQ item:** RQ-SFS-01  
**Research status:** Source packet complete; practitioner review required  
**Prepared:** 2026-08-30  
**Use:** Evidence package for curriculum architecture, original visuals, assessment, and field-practice design. Not course copy.

## Decision question

What should an entry-level stagehand understand and demonstrate when assigned to coil cable, without turning one common method into a universal rule for every cable, reel, fiber assembly, department, or manufacturer?

## Bottom-line finding

Over-under is a defensible default teaching method for many flexible production cables because it alternates the direction placed into successive loops. QSC demonstrates a repeatable over-loop/under-loop hand sequence and connects it to cable longevity and manageable deployment. Hosa explains the mechanical distinction: repeated over-over loops keep adding twist, while the under loop counteracts the preceding twist.

The training must immediately add an exception rule: **the responsible technician and the cable's manufacturer determine the method and coil diameter for the actual cable.** Neutrik specifically warns that opticalCON fiber must respect its minimum coiling diameter, and Belden documents cable-specific bend-radius and pulling limits. A learner who knows over-under is not authorized to ignore those requirements.

## Source findings

### 1. A teachable hand sequence exists

QSC's official training transcript demonstrates:

1. hold the cable end away from the body;
2. make an over loop by gathering a comfortable length and rotating the working hand inward so the cable falls naturally;
3. make the next loop with the thumb reversed and rotate the wrist outward;
4. continue alternating over and under;
5. secure the finished coil with a cable tie;
6. practice until the motion is controlled.

Source: https://training.qsc.com/mod/book/tool/print/index.php?id=1715

This is useful procedural evidence, but the exact left/right hand orientation is not the learning objective. A left-handed learner may mirror the movement. The observable objective is alternating the loop direction without forcing the cable.

### 2. Over-under manages accumulated twist

Hosa identifies over-over and tying the cable around itself as harmful practices. Its explanation is that over-over repeatedly twists the cable in the same direction; over-under adds twist with one loop and removes it with the next. Hosa connects the method to reduced stored tension, cleaner deployment, and longer cable life.

Source: https://hosatech.com/press-release/how-to-wrap-a-cable/

The course may accurately teach this as the reason for the method. It should avoid claiming that every internal failure is caused by improper coiling or that over-under guarantees a cable will remain undamaged.

### 3. Cable-specific limits override the generic technique

Neutrik states that opticalCON assemblies must respect the minimum coiling diameter and warns that using too small a diameter reduces fiber-cable life and performance.

Source: https://www.neutrik.com/en/neutrik/faq/opticalcon

Belden explains that kinks and tightened loops can damage conductors or insulation and violate minimum bend-radius requirements. Belden product specifications also demonstrate that stationary and installation bend radii vary by cable.

Sources:

- https://www.belden.com/blog/copper-cable-installation-best-practices-be-aware-of-the-why
- https://www.belden.com/products/cable/audio-cable/ethernet-snake-cable/1303e
- https://www.belden.com/products/Cable/Electronic-Wire-Cable/Multi-Conductor-Cable/9418

The curriculum implication is not to teach a universal numeric coil diameter. It is to teach the learner to identify the cable and follow the technician/manufacturer's limit.

## Appropriate learner outcome

After instruction and supervised practice, the learner should be able to:

- identify the department owner before handling an unfamiliar cable;
- inspect for visible jacket, connector, strain-relief, kink, contamination, or crush concerns and report them;
- confirm whether the cable is hand-coiled, figure-eighted, reeled, or handled by another specified method;
- make consistent, relaxed alternating loops at the diameter directed by the technician;
- keep connectors from striking the floor or being buried under the coil;
- secure the coil with the provided tie without knotting the cable around itself;
- stage or return the coil where directed;
- stop when the cable will not fall naturally, appears damaged, has model-specific requirements, or is still connected/energized/in service.

## Recommended task sequence

### 1. Receive the assignment

Ask or confirm:

- Which cable?
- Which end should be brought in?
- Is it disconnected and released for strike?
- Hand coil, figure eight, reel, or another method?
- What coil size and tie/label convention?
- Where does the completed cable go?

Do not disconnect, repatch, unplug, move, or de-energize a cable unless that action is explicitly assigned within the learner's authorization.

### 2. Inspect before coiling

Look for and report:

- cuts, crushed areas, flat spots, exposed material, severe kinks, or heat damage;
- bent, cracked, dirty, wet, or missing connector parts;
- damaged strain relief;
- an unreadable label when identification matters;
- a cable trapped under equipment or still routed through a live work area.

The learner reports the condition rather than attempting an electrical, connector, or fiber repair.

### 3. Prepare the route

Bring in slack only as directed. Keep the cable clear of feet, case wheels, lift traffic, doors, sharp edges, and other departments' work. Do not pull against a connector or use the cable to move equipment.

### 4. Form the coil

- Hold the receiving end and let the cable relax.
- Gather a length appropriate to the directed coil diameter.
- Form the first relaxed over loop.
- Reverse the working-hand orientation and form the under loop.
- Continue alternating without twisting the jacket into position.
- If a loop resists, reduce tension, let the cable rotate naturally, and try again.
- If the cable still resists or has a set that conflicts with the method, stop and ask.

### 5. Finish and stage

- Keep both ends visible or positioned according to department convention.
- Use the supplied reusable tie; do not use the cable itself as a knot.
- Do not overtighten the tie or crush connectors inside the coil.
- Carry and stage the coil where directed, protecting connector faces and caps.

## Common wrong setups and what they signal

| Observation | Likely issue | Learner response |
|---|---|---|
| Every loop is formed with the same wrist motion | Over-over is adding twist | Stop, lay out enough cable to remove the accumulated twist, restart under direction |
| Loop must be forced into shape | Natural lay or bend limit is being ignored | Relax the cable; ask if method or diameter should change |
| Connector drags or strikes the floor | Connector control is lost | Stop and protect the connector before continuing |
| Coil includes sharp kinks or very small loops | Bend radius may be violated | Stop and notify technician |
| Cable is tied around itself | Added stress and slow deployment | Use the approved tie if authorized |
| Coil snarls during a controlled deployment check | Alternation or cable lay is inconsistent | Re-practice before practical signoff |
| Fiber, multicore, feeder, or unfamiliar assembly is treated like ordinary mic cable | Model-specific risk is being ignored | Stop and obtain technician/manufacturer direction |

## Stop-and-ask conditions

Stop when:

- ownership or destination is unclear;
- the cable is connected, energized, carrying show-critical service, or not released for strike;
- the technician specifies a reel, figure eight, basket, drum, or other method;
- the minimum bend/coiling diameter is unknown for a sensitive assembly;
- visible damage, contamination, moisture, heat, crushing, or connector problems are found;
- the cable crosses active traffic or another crew's work;
- the learner would need to disconnect, repatch, clean fiber, repair, test, or alter the assembly.

## Visual and video plan

Create original, reviewed assets:

1. overhead still sequence: neutral cable, over loop, under loop, alternating pattern;
2. hand-orientation closeups with a mirrored left-hand option;
3. good coil versus over-over twist versus forced/kinked coil;
4. connector-control and reusable-tie closeup;
5. controlled floor-deployment comparison;
6. exception panel showing reel, fiber/minimum diameter, and “ask the technician.”

The public course links learners directly to QSC's official “How to properly roll a cable” training page, which supplies the visual demonstration and a time-coded transcript. The course does not embed the player or load third-party tracking before the learner chooses the external link. QSC's transcript remains the text fallback. Any future embedded third-party video needs a licensing/availability record, captions, and a text/diagram fallback. The preferred long-term demonstration is original footage using actual Crew Blueprint gear reviewed by the responsible technician.

## Assessment plan

### Knowledge check

The learner should choose the correct response when:

- a cable's natural lay conflicts with the loop;
- a fiber assembly arrives on a drum;
- a cable is still connected;
- the department uses a different tie convention;
- damage is found during strike.

### Practical observation

A reviewer observes the learner:

- confirms assignment and method;
- protects connectors;
- forms at least six relaxed alternating loops;
- avoids forced twist and small-radius kinks;
- secures and stages the coil correctly;
- identifies at least two stop-and-ask conditions.

Record **knowledge completed** separately from **practice observed**. Practical observation on one cable does not authorize handling every cable family.

## Authority boundary

The responsible technician chooses the method, diameter, connector treatment, tie convention, destination, and acceptance standard for the actual cable. Manufacturer instructions control model-specific handling. The course teaches assistance and repeatable hand skill; it does not transfer department ownership, repair authority, testing authority, or permission to disconnect live/show-critical systems.

## Review required before curriculum handoff

- Audio or department technician review of the physical sequence.
- Fiber/video specialist review of exception language.
- Owner review for real-call authenticity.
- Novice learner test on both hand orientation and stop/ask judgment.
