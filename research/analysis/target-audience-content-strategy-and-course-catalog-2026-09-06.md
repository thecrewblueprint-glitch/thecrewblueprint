# The Crew Blueprint — Target-Audience Content Strategy & Course Catalog

**Date:** 2026-09-06  
**Status:** analytical proposal for owner review; not automatic curriculum approval  
**Inputs:** Crew Blueprint evidence matrix and course history; Roadmapdev client-discovery research; Production Atlas production-branch/employer intelligence; current owner publication direction; prior UI/UX audit findings.

## Executive conclusion

The Crew Blueprint should **not** present its 143 registered curriculum identities as 143 public courses.

The 143-record inventory is useful as an internal knowledge/research universe. It includes real course candidates, specialist domains, future containers, career topics, reference material, research questions, role maps, and historical/advanced content. Treating every identity as a learner course creates the wrong product shape.

The strongest audience and employer evidence points to a much smaller public learning system organized around five jobs:

1. reduce first-call uncertainty;
2. teach the unwritten operating system of a production call;
3. help a new worker become easier to direct, trust, and call again;
4. give bounded department-entry literacy without false confidence;
5. connect learning to real career/employer routes without turning the curriculum into a job board.

The recommended public structure is therefore **one foundation course, one Field Skills library, five department starter courses, and a small set of guides/resources**. Advanced technical, lead, supervisor, infrastructure, specialist, and research material remains valuable, but most of it should be reorganized behind Owner Review as modules, reference packs, specialist awareness, or future advanced containers rather than marketed as separate beginner-facing courses.

## 1. Evidence synthesis

### 1.1 What workers repeatedly say they need

Roadmapdev's public-community discovery sample repeatedly surfaced:

- career and route navigation;
- first-call uncertainty and hidden crew expectations;
- listening and asking for clarification instead of bluffing;
- reliability, attendance, follow-through, and staying findable;
- networking as accumulated professional trust;
- choosing a department and understanding progression;
- irregular work, seasonality, pay/admin friction, and multiple work sources;
- touring/festival travel and sustainability realities;
- role/ownership boundaries and preserving other people's systems;
- professionalism, discretion, culture, mentorship, and clean handoffs.

The qualitative sample should not be treated as population prevalence, but its strongest signals are consistent across newcomer questions and experienced-worker complaints.

### 1.2 What employers and the labor ecosystem imply

Production Atlas shows that a live event is not served by one universal employer or one linear ladder. Work can route through:

- labor providers and staffing companies;
- IATSE locals and venue labor pools;
- staging/structure providers;
- lighting, audio, video/LED, power, scenic, and backline vendors;
- touring production companies;
- venue operations;
- site operations and logistics firms;
- producers/promoters and production offices.

Production Atlas's current production taxonomy includes staging/structures, rigging, lighting, audio, video/LED, power/electrical, site operations, logistics, scenic/carpentry, backline, stage management, and production-office work. Employer research also indicates that stagehands commonly enter through vendors, unions, staffing/labor companies, or venue pools rather than directly through a festival brand.

This means the curriculum should teach a durable **industry map and professional behavior model**, while routing current employer, local, market, and opportunity lookup to Production Atlas.

### 1.3 What the previous UI/UX audit got right

The August 31 audit correctly identified several product problems that remain relevant:

- preserve the v2 dark/gold visual identity rather than redesigning the brand;
- About needs a more human story;
- Courses should be a learner catalog, not an owner/audit console;
- public language should be direct, concrete, field-aware, calm, and specific;
- internal terms such as canonical state, publication state, evidence state, and owner-audit mechanics should not dominate the client experience;
- safety/legal limitations should remain clear but should not overwhelm every marketing section;
- the course shell/progression experience is stronger than the marketing/catalog information architecture.

The older audit's publication assumptions are now superseded by the owner's current GitHub Pages staging model. Its useful observations should be treated as incorporated here rather than maintained as a competing current audit.

## 2. Target audiences

### Audience A — First-call worker

**Problem:** "I do not know what I am walking into."  
**Needs:** call information, preparation, crew structure, vocabulary, communication, jobsite awareness, confidence to ask, realistic expectations.

### Audience B — General stagehand building trust

**Problem:** "I can work calls, but I do not know what makes someone consistently useful."  
**Needs:** reliability, closed-loop communication, handoffs, awareness, staying findable, role boundaries, reputation, department exploration.

### Audience C — Department-curious worker

**Problem:** "I want to move toward lighting/audio/video/staging/backline, but I do not know what to learn first or what remains someone else's responsibility."  
**Needs:** bounded department support literacy, workflow/context, vocabulary, ownership boundaries, role families, next-learning choices.

### Audience D — Local worker seeking broader markets or touring work

**Problem:** "I can do calls, but I cannot see the route to the next employer, market, department, or role."  
**Needs:** employer-family maps, union/vendor/venue distinctions, professional trust/networking, current opportunity routes, realistic lifestyle/economic context.

### Audience E — Lead/employer/experienced technician evaluating the product

**Problem:** "New people arrive missing baseline behaviors I repeatedly have to reteach."  
**Needs:** confidence that the product teaches useful baseline behaviors without overstating qualifications, and a clear way to suggest/correct missing baseline topics.

## 3. Recommended public learning architecture

### 3.1 Course 1 — Stagehand Fundamentals

**Keep as the primary foundation course.**

It should concentrate on:

- what a production call is and how load-in/show/load-out differ;
- reading call information and showing up prepared;
- who gives direction and how crew/department structure works;
- listening, clarification, closed-loop communication, and reporting back;
- situational awareness and recognizing when to stop/ask;
- professional reliability and reputation;
- department overview and how to choose a next learning direction;
- how the industry actually routes work through labor companies, unions, venues, vendors, tours, and producers;
- concise career and economic expectation-setting.

**Audit direction:** simplify academic/abstract language; move advanced specialty content out; preserve evidence-backed safety boundaries.

### 3.2 Field Skills Library — not 18 separate public courses

The 18 Field Skills are better presented as a **practice/reference library** attached to Fundamentals rather than eighteen equal-weight courses.

Recommended learner groupings:

1. **Moving Gear & Working as a Team**  
   Case movement, team movement, carts/dollies/hand trucks, controlled transfer and handoff awareness.

2. **Cable & Route Care**  
   Coiling, deployment/gathering, route awareness, cable protectors, labeling and preserving the current crew/system plan.

3. **Worksite Organization & Handoffs**  
   Boneyard/case organization, reset, marks/labels, dock/trailer handoff awareness.

4. **Ground-Level Support Awareness**  
   Soft goods, pipe-and-drape support, riser/deck component support, basic tool recognition/handoff, scenery/wagon movement support.

5. **Assigned Support With Extra Authority Boundaries**  
   Topics such as ratchet-strap support, barricade support, and flatbed/cargo support remain recognition/assigned-support material whose exact equipment, securement, structural, traffic, or acceptance decisions stay with the responsible qualified/authorized person and current site/product procedure.

**Product rule:** Field Skills may have individual pages for navigation and evidence lineage, but the customer should experience them as one library, not as a claim that the product has eighteen additional major courses.

### 3.3 Department Starter — Lighting

Current canonical entry route: `C-LTG-SUPPORT` / Supporting a Lighting Hang.

Teach:

- where lighting sits in the production workflow;
- common gear families and vocabulary at recognition level;
- assigned support behavior and handoff discipline;
- how to preserve a technician's/system's current state;
- when the learner should stop and ask;
- role families around local hands, lighting technicians, shop/prep, console/programming, lead/design work;
- employer-family/context links to Production Atlas.

Do not turn the starter into console programming, electrical qualification, system-design authority, or energized-work procedure.

### 3.4 Department Starter — Audio

Current canonical entry route: `C-AUD-SUPPORT` / Supporting an Audio Load-In.

Teach:

- where audio sits in the production workflow;
- recognition-level system/vocabulary context;
- assigned handling/staging/support behaviors;
- preserving patch/system ownership;
- communication and clean handoffs;
- role families from general support to audio technicians, system techs, RF/comms, shop/touring work;
- employer-family/context links to Production Atlas.

Keep deeper signal, console, networking, RF, PA, and systems material as later modules/reference/advanced review rather than separate required beginner courses.

### 3.5 Department Starter — Video / LED

Current canonical entry route: `C-VID-SUPPORT` / Supporting an LED Video Wall Build.

Teach:

- where video/LED sits in the production workflow;
- recognition-level panel/case/signal/display context;
- careful assigned support and equipment-state preservation;
- escalation when physical, signal, processing, or structural conditions are outside the learner's assignment;
- role families across LED, projection, playback/media servers, camera/broadcast, and video systems;
- employer-family/context links to Production Atlas.

Structural support, rigging, powered access, energized/electrical work, and specialist commissioning remain outside novice procedural scope.

### 3.6 Department Starter — Staging / Scenic

Current canonical entry route: `C-STG-SUPPORT` / Supporting Staging & Carpentry.

Recommended public label: **Staging / Scenic** (with Carpentry explained inside the course) to align more closely with real production-branch language while retaining the existing course identity until owner review approves any rename.

Teach:

- what staging/scenic crews build and support;
- recognition of common component/workflow families;
- assigned material staging, movement, organization, and handoff;
- difference between carrying/staging/support and structural/assembly acceptance authority;
- scenic/shop/venue/touring interfaces;
- employer-family/context links to Production Atlas.

Do not teach structural assembly or acceptance procedures to unqualified beginners.

### 3.7 Department Starter — Backline / Props / Wardrobe

Current canonical entry route: `C-BPW-SUPPORT` / Supporting Backline, Props & Wardrobe.

Keep the current Course 1 starter while the larger pathway remains under targeted research/audit.

Emphasize:

- ownership sensitivity;
- artist/tour/department boundaries;
- preserving arrangement, labels, presets, state, and chain of custody;
- discretion and artist-area professionalism;
- clean handoff and escalation;
- distinction between moving/supporting an item and changing/adjusting a department-owned item;
- current backline employer/context links through Production Atlas.

Do not force-complete the current 2-of-3 pathway merely to satisfy an old three-course structure.

## 4. Public guides/resources — useful content that should not become dozens of courses

### Guide A — How Live Production Fits Together

A visual map of:

- promoter/producer;
- venue/site;
- production management;
- labor company / local / staffing route;
- department vendors;
- touring crew;
- site operations/logistics;
- show-day authority and handoffs.

Use durable concepts in Crew Blueprint and current employer/market links in Production Atlas.

### Guide B — Finding Work & Building Professional Trust

Cover:

- realistic first work routes;
- why workers often use multiple companies/locals/venues/vendors;
- referrals and networking as accumulated trust rather than self-promotion;
- records of work/skills/credentials;
- moving from general labor toward a department;
- current employer/union/market lookup through Production Atlas.

No employment guarantee or earnings promise.

### Guide C — Touring & Festival Work Reality

Cover at high level:

- irregular schedules and travel;
- organization and readiness away from home;
- seasonality and downtime;
- long-call/fatigue awareness and employer/site resources;
- close-quarter professionalism and boundaries;
- how festival work differs from venue/corporate/theatre/touring contexts.

Keep health/medical treatment and dangerous-work procedure outside the guide.

### Guide D — Worker Business & Admin Basics

Consolidate many currently separate registry topics into one practical guide/resource layer:

- employee vs contractor literacy;
- rates/scope/expenses/payment terms;
- time/work records;
- payment reconciliation;
- worker-rights/reporting awareness;
- keeping current professional records.

This content should be jurisdiction-aware and should route changing legal/tax rules to current authoritative resources rather than present itself as individualized legal/tax advice.

### Guide E — Union / Credential / Training Landscape

Explain:

- what IATSE/local jurisdiction means at a high level;
- why one national call-list model does not exist;
- what external credentials/certifications are for;
- which specialist areas require employer/external qualification;
- how to find current local/credential information via Production Atlas and the relevant external body.

## 5. Owner Review / advanced architecture

The following material should remain accessible for owner/agent review but should not be marketed as the normal beginner catalog merely because a registered ID or historical HTML body exists.

### 5.1 Department systems/depth packs

Existing C2/C3/domain material is valuable as a research-backed **module/reference pool**. During owner review classify each block as:

- merge into the department starter;
- advanced module;
- reference page;
- specialist-awareness page;
- future advanced course candidate;
- duplicate/remove;
- needs practitioner validation;
- needs additional authoritative evidence.

Do not preserve an arbitrary three-course ladder solely because it exists historically.

### 5.2 Lead / Crew Chief

Recommended eventual public advanced container: **Lead / Crew Chief Foundations**.

Department-specific Lead pages should initially behave as owner-review reference packs attached to that common leadership foundation, not seven equal public courses.

The common container can focus on responsibility transitions such as planning, assignment, communication, verification, change control, handoffs, escalation, documentation, and protecting specialist authority. Department-specific technical decisions remain bounded by real competence/appointment/qualification.

### 5.3 Supervisor / Production Coordination

Do not position "Supervisor" as a universal rung above every live-event role. Different employers/tours/venues use different structures.

Future content should be framed around **production coordination / operational responsibility** and role-specific context rather than one universal rank ladder.

### 5.4 Rigging

Beginner-facing Crew Blueprint content should be **role/career/awareness bounded**. Use ETCP and qualified industry sources to map role families, prerequisites, external training/qualification, and what general hands should recognize or escalate.

Do not create novice rigging, work-at-height, structural-load, or rescue procedure from the registry simply to fill a pathway.

### 5.5 Production Power / Electrics

Beginner-facing material should distinguish Lighting support from temporary power/electrical specialist work. Use ETCP/ESTA/OSHA/manufacturer sources for role and authority boundaries.

Do not substitute the existing advanced load-planning identity for a missing Course 1 starter and do not teach energized-work/tie-in/testing procedures to general beginners.

### 5.6 Infrastructure and Career branches

Current infrastructure/career pages belong in Owner Review/resource architecture, not the public starter-course catalog. Valuable parts should be redistributed into:

- Production Power awareness/specialist role map;
- How Live Production Fits Together;
- Finding Work & Professional Trust;
- Worker Business/Admin Basics;
- Production Coordination/advanced review.

## 6. Reclassification model for the 143 registered identities

Every registered identity should receive exactly one primary product classification during the next architecture pass:

- `PUBLIC_COURSE`
- `FIELD_SKILL`
- `MODULE`
- `GUIDE`
- `REFERENCE`
- `SPECIALIST_AWARENESS`
- `ADVANCED_OWNER_REVIEW`
- `RESEARCH_DOMAIN`
- `HISTORICAL_VARIANT`
- `DEFER_OR_REMOVE`

Secondary tags may preserve department, competency, authority, source, and historical relationships.

This preserves all research and IDs while stopping the registry from dictating the customer catalog.

### Target scale

The current evidence supports approximately:

- **6 major public course containers now:** Fundamentals + five department starters;
- **1 Field Skills library** containing the current individual Field Skill pages;
- **3–5 public guide/resource hubs**;
- a smaller set of owner-review advanced containers/reference packs.

This is a product architecture target, not a forced final count. The owner audit may merge/split items after reviewing evidence and learner testing.

## 7. Difficulty of converting the current repository

### Overall: Moderate, not a ground-up rebuild

The repository already has an unusually strong research/evidence substrate. The main challenge is **information architecture and editorial reconciliation**, not discovering everything from scratch.

### Relatively easy

- hide/reclassify excess catalog entries without deleting them;
- preserve old course bodies on archive branches;
- change catalog labels/navigation;
- create guide/resource containers from already researched topics;
- add Production Atlas career/context links;
- remove stale future-release language;
- humanize marketing copy.

### Moderate

- merge duplicated C2/C3/domain material into coherent advanced modules;
- reconcile historical full versions against current safer/shorter versions;
- convert 143 identities into the new classification model without breaking lineage;
- rewrite assessment questions to match the reduced learner claims;
- align course/source/competency matrices with new container IDs while preserving aliases.

### Hard / requires real review

- practitioner validation for work that depends on real department responsibility;
- learner usability/retention testing;
- employer/crew-chief interviews to validate the community-discovery sample;
- a defensible live-production role/branch map across venue/touring/festival/corporate/theatre contexts;
- specialist boundaries for Rigging and Electrics/Production Power;
- exact manufacturer/product procedures where future advanced content legitimately needs them.

## 8. Next research with highest expected value

1. **Direct employer/lead interviews**  
   Ask labor coordinators, crew chiefs, department leads, venue supervisors, touring technicians, and shop managers what they repeatedly reteach new hands and what causes callbacks/non-callbacks.

2. **Direct learner interviews/usability tests**  
   Observe what first-call and early-career workers misunderstand before/after Fundamentals and which parts actually improve confidence without increasing false confidence.

3. **Real role-family map**  
   Combine Production Atlas branch taxonomy, employer job families, IATSE craft structures, ETCP specialist scopes, and representative venue/touring structures. Output a branching map, not one universal hierarchy.

4. **Training-transfer research**  
   Review adult/vocational learning, scenario judgment, retrieval practice, and supervised-practice literature to decide which content belongs in reading, scenario checks, field observation, or external employer training.

5. **Source-freshness registry**  
   Flag regulations, standards, credential scopes, manufacturer documents, employer links, and directories that require periodic recheck. Stable principles and volatile current data should not be managed the same way.

6. **Archived-course salvage audit**  
   Diff fuller historical Lead/Supervisor/Electrics bodies against current evidence. Salvage supported concepts and examples without automatically restoring old operational claims.

7. **Backline/Props/Wardrobe targeted validation**  
   Combine the existing Crew Blueprint work, Production Atlas backline research, experienced backline/wardrobe/props interviews, and authoritative product/venue/tour guidance before deciding whether the area needs multiple courses or one starter plus role-specific references.

## 10. Decision rule for the next content wave

A topic should become a standalone public course only when it has all of the following:

1. a distinct target-audience problem;
2. enough coherent material to justify a separate learning journey;
3. a clear learner claim that does not overstate authority;
4. evidence/source support appropriate to the claim;
5. little unnecessary duplication with existing courses/guides;
6. a credible assessment/validation method;
7. a clear place in the real-world role/employer ecosystem.

If a topic fails this test, preserve it as a module, guide, reference, research domain, or Owner Review item rather than manufacturing another course.
