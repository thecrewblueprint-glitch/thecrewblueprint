# The Crew Blueprint — Recommended Public Catalog

**Date:** September 6, 2026  
**Status:** final analytical recommendation for owner review; not automatic publication approval  
**Machine-readable companion:** `research/analysis/final-recommended-public-catalog-2026-09-06.json`  
**Discovery companion:** `research/analysis/audience-employer-discovery-synthesis-2026-09-06.md`

## Executive decision

The Crew Blueprint should **not** become a 143-course product.

The 143 registered identities are useful as an internal knowledge universe: they preserve research domains, historical builds, specialist topics, career questions, role maps, modules, advanced material, and future possibilities. They should not dictate what a learner sees as a course.

The recommended public learner architecture is:

- **6 major course containers**
  - Stagehand Fundamentals
  - five department Course 1 starters
- **1 Practical Field Skills Library**
  - containing the current **18 bounded Field Skill routes**
- **5 career/resource guides**

That produces **30 learner-facing content items, but only six courses**.

The public product therefore becomes substantially smaller and easier to understand without deleting the research that made the larger system valuable.

---

# 1. What the audience actually wants

The strongest discovery signals are not requests for more advanced engineering theory. New and early-career workers repeatedly want to know:

- what a first call is actually like;
- what information to read before arriving;
- who gives direction;
- how to ask a question without bluffing;
- what makes someone reliable and call-back worthy;
- how to become useful in a department without overclaiming expertise;
- how local work connects to vendors, venues, unions, touring, festivals, and specialist roles;
- how irregular scheduling, transportation, travel, seasonality, and pay/admin realities affect the work;
- which training or credentials actually matter for the path they want.

The repeated worker-side need is **context**: the unwritten operating system around the task.

# 2. What employers and leads actually need

Current employer/venue material and experienced-worker discussion converge around:

- punctuality and dependable attendance;
- reading call/work information;
- following direction accurately;
- communicating clearly;
- asking instead of guessing;
- staying with the assignment and remaining findable;
- equipment care and clean storage/reset;
- adapting to changing production needs;
- respecting chain of command and department ownership;
- reporting damage, incomplete work, or uncertainty;
- professional conduct and discretion;
- staying within training, qualification, authorization, and assignment boundaries.

The most important overlap between learner demand and employer demand is therefore **reliable, bounded, communicative support work**.

# 3. The cross-course operating model

Three behaviors should become visible throughout the product.

## Listen → Clarify → Report Back

A learner should not be rewarded for pretending to understand. The durable beginner behavior is to hear the assignment, clarify what is unclear, complete only the assigned support, and reconnect with the responsible person.

## Preserve → Verify → Report

Do not silently unplug, untape, relabel, reorganize, repatch, substitute, move, or “fix” another department’s system simply because the learner recognizes it. Preserve the current state, verify uncertainty with the responsible person, and report what was found or completed.

## Useful before impressive

The beginner goal is not to perform an advanced identity. It is to become dependable enough that the crew can safely give the worker more context and responsibility over time.

# 4. Recommended major public course catalog

## Course 1 — Stagehand Fundamentals

**Audience:** first-call and early-career stagehands.  
**Problem:** the learner does not know what they are walking into or how a production call operates socially and organizationally.  
**Why research supports it:** first-call uncertainty, reliability, clarification, career confusion, call-document literacy, and role boundaries repeatedly surface in worker evidence; current employers independently ask for the same baseline behaviors.  
**Learning outcome:** understand the call lifecycle, read basic call information, identify who directs the work, use listen/clarify/report-back behavior, recognize department boundaries, and explain what dependable stagehand behavior looks like.  
**Format:** **course**.  
**Reuse:** current `C-SHF-FUNDAMENTALS`; archived 10-module Fundamentals; useful parts of the standalone First Five Calls material.  
**Missing:** direct learner testing, employer/lead interviews, editorial simplification, explicit call-document scenarios, and Preserve → Verify → Report integration.  
**Readiness:** **high** — a real live foundation exists.  
**Owner review:** required before substantive rewrite or production-port approval.  
**Boundary:** does not certify or qualify the learner for specialist work; employer/site rules and responsible qualified personnel remain controlling.

### Recommended internal emphasis

Fundamentals should absorb or foreground:

- What a production call is
- Reading the call
- Who directs what
- Load-in / show / load-out context
- Staying available and findable
- Listen → Clarify → Report Back
- Preserve → Verify → Report
- Equipment stewardship
- Department overview
- Reliability and professional trust
- First-call / first-five-call reality
- Choosing a next learning direction

This is a better use of the existing foundation material than spinning those subjects into many separate courses.

# 6. Five public career/resource guides

## Guide 1 — How Live Production Fits Together

**Audience:** workers confused by industry structure.  
**Problem:** a festival/show brand hides the network of promoter, venue, production management, labor provider, union/local, vendors, touring crew, logistics, site ops, and production office.  
**Research support:** career navigation is the strongest discovery need; Production Atlas explicitly models the event as a multi-employer ecosystem.  
**Outcome:** navigate a branching role/employer map and understand common authority/hiring relationships without assuming a universal ladder.  
**Format:** **career guide**.  
**Reuse:** Fundamentals department overview; Production Atlas taxonomy/employer research; existing role-map backlog.  
**Missing:** cross-context validation with venue, touring, festival, corporate, theatre, union, non-union, vendor, and production-office workers.  
**Readiness:** medium — research substrate exists, guide does not.  
**Owner review:** required.  
**Boundary:** role map does not confer appointment, title, qualification, or authorization.

## Guide 2 — Finding Work & Building Professional Trust

**Audience:** workers seeking first calls, callbacks, department opportunities, or touring/vendor connections.  
**Problem:** “networking” is vague and the real labor entry paths are hard to see.  
**Research support:** reliability, referrals, specialization, and reputation repeatedly appear in community evidence; Production Atlas shows stagehands commonly enter through labor providers, locals, venues, and vendors.  
**Outcome:** identify realistic work-source categories, explain trust accumulation, maintain truthful experience records, and choose a next employer/department research path.  
**Format:** **career guide**.  
**Reuse:** archived Fundamentals career material; Roadmapdev discovery; Production Atlas employer intelligence.  
**Missing:** labor-coordinator interviews and current-market usability testing.  
**Readiness:** medium.  
**Owner review:** required.  
**Boundary:** no employment guarantee, earnings promise, or claim that relationships substitute for qualifications.

## Guide 3 — Touring & Festival Work Reality

**Audience:** local workers considering festival or touring work.  
**Problem:** the public event hides irregular schedules, travel, seasonality, downtime, close crew environments, and changing worksites.  
**Research support:** worker discovery repeatedly raises travel, fatigue, personal organization, downtime and work-life sustainability; Production Atlas describes festival labor as seasonal and vendor-mediated.  
**Outcome:** compare local/venue/festival/touring work and identify personal/logistical questions to resolve before pursuing a path.  
**Format:** **career guide**.  
**Reuse:** Roadmapdev discovery + Production Atlas festival labor research.  
**Missing:** touring/festival worker interviews and learner decision-usefulness testing.  
**Readiness:** medium.  
**Owner review:** required.  
**Boundary:** high-level career/work-life literacy only; no medical treatment or hazardous-work procedure.

## Guide 4 — Worker Business & Admin Basics

**Audience:** workers navigating pay, records, engagement type, expenses, or contractor/employee terminology.  
**Problem:** irregular work creates confusion about timekeeping, scope, payment terms, expenses, and legal/tax labels.  
**Research support:** community research repeatedly surfaces pay/admin friction; Production Atlas cautions that calls may be W-2, union referral, staffing payroll, vendor payroll, contractor, volunteer, or another arrangement depending on context.  
**Outcome:** keep basic work records, understand common engagement vocabulary without making legal conclusions, and know when current authoritative resources are needed.  
**Format:** **career guide**.  
**Reuse:** existing career/admin research; archived Fundamentals career material; Production Atlas engagement model.  
**Missing:** jurisdiction-aware current legal/tax authority framework and professional review of final wording.  
**Readiness:** medium-low.  
**Owner review:** required.  
**Boundary:** general literacy only; no individualized legal, tax, payroll-classification, or financial advice.

## Guide 5 — Training, Credentials & Specialist Paths

**Audience:** workers deciding how to progress beyond general support.  
**Problem:** people do not know which credentials, employer training, union pathways, specialist education, or external qualifications matter for a chosen role.  
**Research support:** credential questions recur in career discovery; Rhino explicitly stresses scope and qualified guidance; Production Atlas separates Rigging, Power/Electrical, department vendors, and IATSE contexts; Crew Blueprint already holds substantial ETCP/ESTA/OSHA research.  
**Outcome:** distinguish experience, employer training, role appointment, credential/certification, and specialist qualification, then locate the current authority for a desired path.  
**Format:** **career guide**.  
**Reuse:** ETCP/ESTA/OSHA source records; Production Atlas IATSE/role research; archived Rigging/Electrics material only as awareness/source material.  
**Missing:** credential/source freshness registry and cross-market role validation.  
**Readiness:** medium.  
**Owner review:** required.  
**Boundary:** no rigging procedure, energized electrical work, temporary-power tie-ins, work-at-height procedure, structural assembly, powered-equipment operation, pyro, laser procedure, or other hazardous specialist instruction.

# 7. What should not be a normal public course

## Course 2 / Course 3 department material

Do not preserve a three-course ladder merely because one exists historically. Reclassify each block as one of:

- module inside Course 1;
- advanced module;
- reference;
- specialist awareness;
- future advanced course candidate;
- Owner Review material;
- duplicate/superseded;
- needs practitioner evidence.

## Lead material

Keep **Lead / Crew Chief Foundations** as a possible advanced owner-review container. Department-specific Lead bodies should remain reference/reconciliation packs until real role/appointment/practitioner validation supports publication.

## Supervisor material

Do not restore **Supervisor** as a universal rung. Useful content should move toward production coordination, operational responsibility, communication, documentation, and role-specific management contexts.

## Rigging

Public beginner treatment should be role/career/awareness only: what the specialty is, why qualification matters, what a general hand should recognize, and when to stop/escalate. Existing Rigging research/history remains valuable but is not a novice procedure course.

## Electrics / Production Power

Do not substitute the existing advanced load-planning course for a missing Course 1. Beginner-facing content should explain the specialist role and authority boundaries; technical power work stays external to novice procedural instruction.

## Infrastructure / production research containers

Keep retrievable for agents and Owner Review. Redistribute useful concepts into guides or references rather than exposing internal research architecture as learner choices.

- career/admin material that could be read as individualized legal/tax conclusions;
- any copy implying Crew Blueprint certifies or authorizes specialist work.

# 12. What should simply remain archived or internal

- alternate historical presentations whose useful concepts already survive elsewhere;
- superseded UI experiments;
- old public Lead/Supervisor/Electrics/Rigging presentations pending reconciliation;
- internal evidence/research containers that solve agent/research problems rather than learner problems;
- volatile employer/opportunity snapshots better served by Production Atlas.

Do not delete unique research or substantive historical course bodies. Archive status means **retrievable but not presented as current learner truth**.

---

# Final recommendation

The Crew Blueprint should present itself as a practical, evidence-aware training and resource project for people learning how to function well in live-event production—not as an encyclopedia of every technical subject and not as a universal promotion ladder.

The strongest product is smaller:

**Fundamentals → Field Skills → five Department Starters → practical career/resources → real employer/specialist pathways outside the site where appropriate.**

That architecture is better aligned with what workers repeatedly ask, what employers repeatedly expect, what the existing repository can already support, and the safety/authority limits the project needs to preserve.
