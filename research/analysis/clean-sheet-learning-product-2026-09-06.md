## Owner mandate

This build intentionally does **not** inherit the current course taxonomy, module sequence, lesson names, or wording. Existing Crew Blueprint content remains available as evidence and historical reference, but it is not the structural template for this candidate.

No prior course version is deleted or overwritten. This experiment sits beside the current v2/public/Owner Review system and the permanent historical archive.

## Product goal

Build the learning product that best helps a worker:

1. understand what live-production work actually is;
2. arrive ready and become useful quickly;
3. build ordinary field competence and judgment;
4. understand departments before claiming technical identity;
5. adapt the same principles across very different production environments;
6. find legitimate work through the real employer ecosystem;
7. earn repeat calls through observable professional behavior; and
8. choose deeper training from real work, market demand, mentors, and qualification requirements.

The resulting customer journey is:

**Get the call → Understand the call → Become useful → Build field competence → Read the operating context → Choose a department → Find more work → Grow deliberately**

## Core product insight

Live production is both highly variable and highly patterned.

A festival, arena, theatre, convention center, club, rental shop, and touring package may use different labor structures, access routes, schedules, equipment, pace, site rules, hazards, terminology, and authority relationships. At the same time, recurring production logic remains recognizable:

- work has owners;
- events move through phases;
- equipment and information follow planned flows;
- departments hand work to one another;
- production state must be preserved until the responsible person changes it;
- environment changes which hazards deserve the most attention;
- qualification and authorization boundaries remain role- and context-specific.

Therefore the curriculum should teach **transferable reasoning + context recognition**, not one sacred workflow.

## Evidence classes and controlling weight

### 1. Regulatory / legal / recognized safety authority

**Controls:** safety boundaries, employer duties, qualification distinctions, prohibited learner claims, and escalation rules.

Representative sources include OSHA General Industry requirements for walking-working surfaces, PPE, hazardous-energy control, materials handling/storage, powered industrial trucks, and electrical work.

Community discussion, employer marketing, and Crew Blueprint prose never override this class.

### 2. Current employer / hiring demand

**Controls:** which employability behaviors deserve early teaching time and which job-facing competencies should be visible.

Current evidence repeatedly emphasizes some combination of:

- ability to work under direction;
- desire and ability to learn;
- communication;
- responsiveness;
- professionalism;
- organization;
- safety awareness;
- physical readiness where applicable;
- progressive experience for deeper technician roles;
- role-specific technical knowledge rather than one universal “stagehand” expertise.

The PRG Crew Portal is particularly useful because it exposes distinct stagehand, lighting, audio, video, rigging, electrical, production, logistics, backline, network, and management role families rather than collapsing them into one ladder.

### 3. Worker / learner demand

**Controls:** recurring confusion, pain points, career questions, hidden rules, and language learners actually use.

Roadmapdev and Crew Blueprint public-community research repeatedly identifies:

- first-call uncertainty;
- not knowing what “useful” looks like;
- asking versus bluffing;
- staying with the assigned team;
- situational awareness;
- equipment care;
- career-path uncertainty;
- networking and repeat-call reputation;
- long hours, travel, seasonality, and sustainability;
- the difference between local support and department/touring authority.

This evidence identifies what deserves teaching attention. It does **not** establish technical or safety procedure.

### 4. Production Atlas / industry structure

**Controls:** the model of the production ecosystem and the changing employer/opportunity layer.

Production Atlas currently models staging, rigging, lighting, audio, video/LED, power, site operations, logistics, scenic, backline, stage management, and production-office branches. Its changing employer and opportunity records belong outside static course prose.

Crew Blueprint should teach **how the ecosystem works**. Production Atlas should answer **who/where/what is current**.

### 5. Existing Crew Blueprint corpus

**Use:** factual knowledge reservoir, prior research, examples, source discovery, gap detection, and historical comparison.

**Non-use:** it does not dictate the clean-sheet product architecture, module count, lesson order, course names, or wording.

## Learning design rules

1. **One lesson = one mental model or work decision.**
2. **Short prose, deep enough to explain why.** Avoid both bullet-only under-service and standards-dump overload.
3. **Visualize process.** Use flow diagrams, ownership maps, lifecycle models, and context maps.
4. **Make the learner retrieve.** Each lesson uses a check, scenario, or short recall prompt.
5. **Teach transfer.** Context Labs deliberately show how the same principle changes shape across festival, arena, venue/theatre, corporate/convention, and shop/warehouse settings.
6. **Keep safety boundaries explicit but concise.** Put specialist authority where it changes the learner’s decision; do not flood ordinary lessons with legal prose.
7. **Never imply qualification by completion.** Knowledge, demonstrated competence, credential/qualification, and employer/site authorization remain separate.
8. **Career content must connect to real hiring routes.** Avoid a fake universal promotion ladder.
9. **Volatile market facts stay live.** Link to Production Atlas/current employer pages instead of freezing changing claims into the course.
10. **Old versions remain intact.** This experiment is disposable; the historical corpus is not.

## Clean-sheet program set

### Start Working
First-call mental model, reading the call, readiness, check-in, assignment ownership, clarification, mistakes, and first-five-call observation.

### Become the Hand They Want Back
Reliability, attention, production-state preservation, labels, equipment stewardship, reset quality, situational awareness, and clean handoffs.

### Field Skills Studio
Ordinary ground-hand work taught as material-flow and ownership systems: cases/carts, team moves, released cable handling, cable routes/protectors, staging/boneyards, tools/marks, soft-goods support, dock/trailer handoffs, and work-area reset.

### Context Labs
The same production reasoning under different conditions: outdoor festival, arena/dock, venue/theatre, corporate/convention, and shop/warehouse.

### Choose a Department
Starter routes for Lighting, Audio, Video/LED, Staging/Scenic, and Backline/Props/Wardrobe. Each route teaches system picture, useful beginner support, vocabulary, authority boundary, and realistic growth route.

### Find Work
Employer ecosystem, Production Atlas integration, transferable resume evidence, reputation/networking, availability, transportation, seasonality, and personal market tracking.

### Grow Deliberately
Career lattice, shop/prep learning, knowledge-versus-authorization distinctions, 90-day learning plans, and career sustainability.

## Deliberate exclusions from beginner procedural teaching

This candidate may teach recognition, interfaces, vocabulary, risk awareness, and stop/escalate decisions around the following, but not procedural execution:

- energized electrical / temporary-power work;
- rigging and work at height;
- structural acceptance or engineered modifications;
- powered industrial trucks or other powered access/equipment;
- automation/machinery operation;
- pyro and laser systems;
- crane operations;
- specialist commissioning, programming, tuning, calibration, network administration, or repair where the actual role requires training/authorization.

## Review question

Do not ask only whether this candidate contains enough information.

Ask:

> If a motivated person completed this site before and during their first months in live production, would they understand the ecosystem, become easier to employ and teach, make fewer avoidable mistakes, recognize where they need real-world training, and know how to find the next legitimate opportunity?
