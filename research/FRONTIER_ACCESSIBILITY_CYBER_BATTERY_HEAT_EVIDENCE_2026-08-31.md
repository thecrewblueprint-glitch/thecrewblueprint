# Frontier Evidence — Accessibility, Production Cyber Hygiene, Battery Awareness, and Heat/Changing Conditions

**Prepared:** 2026-08-31  
**Status:** Draftable awareness baseline

## Accessibility / public interface

ADA Title III applies to many businesses open to the public and includes organizations offering courses or examinations related to professional/trade credentialing. DOJ guidance states that public accommodations must provide equal opportunity to access offered goods/services and treats inaccessible web features as a potential barrier to those goods/services.

Sources:
- https://www.ada.gov/topics/title-iii/
- https://www.ada.gov/resources/web-guidance/

For event workers, Crew Blueprint should teach **accessibility awareness**, not compliance certification. Relevant interfaces include accessible/public routes, cable crossings, temporary surfaces, ramps/platform interfaces, aisle/door obstruction, caption/display systems, assistive-listening interfaces, and communication/access requests. The responsible venue/event/accessibility authority controls actual compliance decisions.

## Production-network cyber hygiene

CISA's basic cyber hygiene guidance emphasizes recognizing/reporting phishing, strong passwords/password managers, multifactor authentication, and timely software updates. Those principles transfer safely to production technology without teaching intrusion or offensive techniques.

Source:
- https://www.cisa.gov/secure-our-world

Draftable production context:
- approved accounts and credential ownership;
- MFA/password-manager awareness where supported;
- unknown links/attachments/removable media as escalation points;
- approved software/firmware/update ownership;
- backups and known-good show-file/version discipline;
- unauthorized devices and unplanned network changes;
- least-change principle during live production;
- incident reporting and responsible IT/system-owner handoff.

This course does **not** teach penetration testing, credential bypass, exploit use, covert access, or unauthorized network discovery.

## Battery and charging awareness

OSHA has recently highlighted workplace risks from lithium-ion batteries, including fire/explosion/chemical hazards in certain conditions and work contexts. Product chemistry and employer operations vary; Crew Blueprint should keep exact charging/storage/emergency procedures with the manufacturer and employer.

Sources:
- https://www.osha.gov/quicktakes/013026
- https://www.osha.gov/news/newsreleases/osha-trade-release/20260209
- https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/standardinterpretations/2021-06-23

Draftable awareness:
- device/battery identity and manufacturer instructions;
- visible damage, swelling, leakage, unexpected heat/odor, or impact as report/hold conditions;
- designated charging/storage locations;
- approved charger/adapter ownership;
- separating suspect equipment into the employer's controlled process;
- no opening, repair, improvised charging, disposal, or emergency-response improvisation.

## Heat and changing outdoor conditions

OSHA issued an updated National Emphasis Program for Outdoor and Indoor Heat-Related Hazards on April 10, 2026. Crew Blueprint can teach recognition of changing heat/work conditions, employer heat-plan ownership, acclimatization awareness, hydration/rest/access awareness, symptom reporting, and stop/escalate behavior without inventing universal exposure thresholds.

Sources:
- https://www.osha.gov/news/newsreleases/osha-national-news-release/20260410
- https://www.osha.gov/enforcement/directives/cpl-03-00-024-0
- https://www.osha.gov/heat-exposure

## Release rule

All five areas are cross-domain. The online course teaches recognition, communication, records, and authority boundaries; site-specific compliance, technical configuration, medical judgment, emergency response, and employer authorization remain external.
