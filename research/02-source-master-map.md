## PROMPT 1: Manual Material Handling

**Prompt:** "What are OSHA and NIOSH guidance documents on manual lifting, team handling, and load positioning for workers in entertainment/event production? Include ergonomic standards and pinch-point hazard sources."

**Coverage:** Lifting, team handling, load positioning, pinch zones, fatigue, floor-to-shoulder handling zones.

| OSHA 1910 Subpart N | 29 CFR 1910 Subpart N: Materials Handling and Storage | Regulation Index | General-industry materials handling and storage governing subpart | https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910SubpartN |

| OSHA Push/Pull/Carry | Pushing, Pulling, and Carrying | Government Guidance | Avoid pulling when possible; eliminate sharp edges/rough areas; prevent hand cuts/pinches | https://www.osha.gov/etools/electrical-contractors/materials-handling/pushing |

#### Practical Interpretation (per Perplexity analysis)

| Finding | Source(s) | Application to Course 1 |
|---------|-----------|-------------------------|
| **Manual lifting scope** | NIOSH RNLE [1] | Apply RNLE only to defined manual lifting within its scope; use outcome to redesign load, lift geometry, frequency, or handling method. *Not all lifts fit the formula—some require engineering controls or team handling.* |
| **Team handling controls** | NIOSH Ergonomics Program [5][10] | Designate a caller; establish commands and set-down locations; confirm all handlers can see/communicate; use dollies/lifts for awkward/unstable/vision-obstructing loads. Team lift is a planned control, not an automatic solution. |
| **Optimal load positioning** | OSHA Shipyard [6], Warehousing [7] | Waist-to-mid-chest zones preferred; keep load near body; reduce twisting and reach; stage cases to avoid repeated floor-level or above-shoulder lifting. |
| **Pinch-point hazard zones** | OSHA 1910.176 [2], Warehousing [7], Push/Pull/Carry [8] | Check caster wheels, ramp/dock transitions, lift gates, doors, stacked cases, cart-to-wall clearances, rolling loads, moving parts. Guard and lock out machine/conveyor pinches. |

#### Operational Triage (per Perplexity analysis)

| Finding | Source(s) | Application to Course 1 |
|---------|-----------|-------------------------|
| **Caster inspection** | SKB [3][4], Pelican [1] | Before each move: inspect wheels, tires/tread, bearings, fasteners, mounting plates, welds, brake mechanisms. Remove case from service if wheel wobbles, binds, cracks, is loose, or won't lock. *No universal failure threshold across brands.* |
| **Brake limitations** | OSHA Warehousing [10], OSHA 1910.176 [2] | Brakes are parking controls only, not sole restraint on ramps, liftgates, docks, trucks, or inclines. Use chocks, straps, load bars, or blocking where movement could cause struck-by or runaway hazard. |
| **Load limit hierarchy** | SKB [3][1], Gator [6] | The controlling limit is the **lowest-rated component** (case shell, rack rails, casters, handles, stacking interface, cart, ramp, liftgate, vehicle securement). Do not assign a load rating if manufacturer does not publish one for the exact assembly. |
| **Stacking safety** | SKB [3], OSHA 1910.176 [2], 1926.250 [7], 1917.14 [8] | Stack only manufacturer-approved models/orientations; maintain stable base and low center of gravity; keep stacks out of travel ways; block/interlock against sliding or collapse. |

## PROMPT 3: Cable Handling

**Prompt:** "What do cable manufacturers (Belden, Sommer, Neutrik) and live-event standards (IATSE, USITT) specify about cable over-under coiling, connector protection, live-cable awareness, and departmental ownership?"

**Coverage:** Over-under coiling, connector protection, live-cable awareness, departmental ownership, bend radius, pull force limits.

**Key finding:** No universal IATSE or USITT national standard prescribes one over-under method or assigns universal cable ownership. Manufacturer guidance and production-level work plans establish the controls.

### Sources

#### Manufacturer

| Source | Citation | Type | Coverage | URL |
|--------|----------|------|----------|-----|

| Belden Cable Pull | What it Takes to Pull Category Cable for AV Projects | Manufacturer | Maximum pull tension, pair geometry preservation, conduit fill, approved lubricants, bend-radius limits (4x outside diameter example) | https://www.belden.com/blog/what-it-takes-to-pull-category-cable-for-av-projects |
| Belden Fiber Pulling | Right Fiber Pulling Eye Makes All the Difference | Manufacturer | Pulling eyes and protective socks distribute force to cable jacket; protect end connectors during pulls | https://www.belden.com/blog/right-fiber-pulling-eye-makes-all-the-difference |
| Belden Bonded-Pair | 3 Ethernet Failures that Bonded-Pair Cable Prevents | Manufacturer | Pull force, bending, pair separation degrade transmission; larger bend radius may be required | https://www.belden.com/blog/3-ethernet-failures-that-bonded-pair-cable-prevents |

#### Operational Application (per Perplexity analysis)

| Finding | Source(s) | Application to Course 1 |
|---------|-----------|-------------------------|
| **Over-under coiling method** | Sommer Cable [1] | Use only as twist-management practice: keep coils loose, respect bend radius, do not kink or sharply reverse jacket. Stop if cable develops corkscrew or persistent twist. *Not the only method; controlled practice matters more than technique name.* |
| **Connector protection** | Belden Pulling [4], Neutrik TOP [3], speakON [6], etherCON [7] | Never drag, lift, or tension cable by connector. Protect terminated ends during pulls. Fit dust caps in storage/transport. Inspect locking, latch, boot, strain relief, contacts before deployment. |
| **Live-cable awareness** | Neutrik [3][7], Belden [2] | Treat unidentified or connected cable as potentially energized or signal-critical. Do not disconnect, re-route, coil, or repair without confirmation from responsible department. Prevents damage and unauthorized changes. |
| **Departmental ownership** | IATSE [8] | Establish in written work plan: identify responsible department for power, audio, video/data, lighting, comms, temporary cable crossings. Require approval before any crew moves, unplugs, tapes, or re-terminates departmental cable. *Not a universal national rule; varies by local and production context.* |
