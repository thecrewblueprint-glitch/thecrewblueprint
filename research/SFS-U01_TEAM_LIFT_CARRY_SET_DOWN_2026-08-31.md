# SFS-U01 — Team Lift, Carry, and Set Down

**Prepared:** 2026-08-31  
**Status:** Source pack complete for bounded curriculum architecture; workplace/practitioner review still required before learner release  
**Parent:** MDQ-010 Stagehand Field Skills Universe  
**Tier target:** T2 Field Skill, only inside an assigned and supervised workplace context  
**Boundary:** This packet does not establish a universal safe weight, crew size, lifting method, or permission for a learner to move heavy/awkward production items independently.

## 1. Research question

What can Crew Blueprint responsibly teach about team handling of ordinary production items without replacing employer ergonomics controls, mechanical aids, object-specific instructions, or the responsible lead's judgment?

## 2. Evidence classification

- **Authoritative:** OSHA workplace ergonomics/material-handling guidance.
- **Government/occupational-safety guidance:** HSE and CCOHS manual-handling guidance.
- **Industry-primary:** current stagehand job descriptions and IATSE material already preserved in Packages 15 and 18.
- **Crew Blueprint framing:** the bounded course sequence and stop/escalate model derived from the evidence.

## 3. Executive finding

A defensible course can teach **planning, communication, route control, use of available handling aids, coordinated movement, controlled set-down, and stop/escalate judgment**.

It should not teach one fixed maximum weight, one fixed number of workers, one universal set of call words, or one handling posture for every road box, scenic item, speaker, riser component, trunk, table, or other production object.

The strongest repeated principle across sources is that the task must be assessed as a whole: the load, available grip, route, visibility, floor/surface, carrying distance, worker coordination, repetition, and available mechanical aids all change the risk.

## 4. Claim register

### Claim A — Do not invent a universal OSHA lifting limit

**Evidence:** OSHA's ergonomic guidance evaluates lifting risk using multiple variables including load weight, distance from the body, vertical travel, twisting, grip, frequency, floor/traction, space and load stability. Existing Crew Blueprint Package 18 already records OSHA's explicit position that federal OSHA does not set one universal maximum manual-lift weight.

OSHA's electrical-contractor material-handling eTool uses about 50 lb as a screening/control point in that specific guidance and recommends two-person or mechanical handling above it. That number must not be converted into a universal stagehand law or guarantee of safety.

**Course use:** Never say “one person can safely lift X pounds” as a national rule. Teach the learner to use the employer/lead's assignment and get help or a handling aid when control is uncertain.

Sources:
- OSHA Technical Manual, Ergonomics: https://www.osha.gov/otm/section-7-ergonomics/chapter-1
- OSHA Materials Handling — Heavy Lifting: https://www.osha.gov/etools/electrical-contractors/materials-handling/heavy
- Crew Blueprint Package 18 for the federal-weight-limit correction.

### Claim B — Mechanical handling or redesign can be preferable to manual team lifting

**Evidence:** OSHA, HSE and CCOHS all place strong emphasis on reducing manual-handling exposure through carts, pallet trucks, lifts, improved layout, smaller loads or other aids where feasible.

**Course use:** The learner should not treat “add more people” as the automatic answer. If the assigned crew cannot control the item or a suitable handling aid is required, stop and return the decision to the responsible lead.

Sources:
- OSHA Heavy Lifting eTool: https://www.osha.gov/etools/electrical-contractors/materials-handling/heavy
- HSE Control Measures for Team Handling: https://www.hse.gov.uk/msd/mac/guidance03a.htm
- CCOHS Back Injury Prevention: https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/ergonomics/inj_prev.html

### Claim C — Plan the route and the set-down before movement

**Evidence:** HSE and CCOHS manual-handling guidance repeatedly treats floor condition, obstructions, carrying distance, visibility, stairs/level changes, grip and destination as material risk factors. Crew Blueprint Package 18 reaches the same conclusion for live-event load-in/load-out routes.

**Course use:** Before an assigned team move begins, the crew should know the destination and whether the route includes obstacles, surface changes or limited visibility. If the route cannot be controlled, the move should not start.

Sources:
- HSE Manual Handling / construction risk factors: https://www.hse.gov.uk/construction/healthrisks/physical-ill-health-risks/manual-handling.htm
- HSE lifting-task guidance: https://www.hse.gov.uk/msd/mac/guidance01b.htm
- CCOHS Team Handling: https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/ergonomics/mmh/team_handling.pdf

### Claim D — Team handling requires deliberate coordination

**Evidence:** CCOHS team-handling guidance recommends selecting a team appropriate to the task, assigning leadership/coordination, agreeing commands, and coordinating the movement. HSE notes that team handling can introduce uneven load sharing and coordination problems.

**Course use:** Teach the concept of **one coordinated movement plan** and an agreed stop signal. Do not claim that one particular word set is universal across live events.

Sources:
- CCOHS Team Handling: https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/ergonomics/mmh/team_handling.pdf
- HSE team-handling risk/control material: https://www.hse.gov.uk/msd/mac/teamintro.htm

### Claim E — Visibility, grip, instability and awkward geometry can make a nominally light object unsuitable for a simple team move

**Evidence:** OSHA/HSE/CCOHS treat grip quality, blocked sightlines, unstable/shifting loads, awkward dimensions, twisting, carrying distance and environment as independent risk factors.

**Course use:** The learner should not decide solely by weight. A large, unstable, hard-to-grip or sightline-blocking item can require a different crew plan or mechanical aid.

Sources:
- OSHA Technical Manual: https://www.osha.gov/otm/section-7-ergonomics/chapter-1
- HSE manual-handling risk filters: https://www.hse.gov.uk/msd/manual-handling-risk-filters.htm
- CCOHS MMH guidance: https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/ergonomics/mmh/sacks.pdf

### Claim F — Smooth control matters more than speed

**Evidence:** HSE's manual-handling guidance advises planned, smooth handling and warns against jerking/snatching loads; live-event source packets independently emphasize controlled material movement and not rushing around docks/cases.

**Course use:** The course can teach that the move stops when the team loses coordination, control or clear route awareness. Production schedule pressure does not override the responsible lead's safe-work plan.

Source:
- HSE Good Handling Technique: https://www.hse.gov.uk/msd/manual-handling/good-handling-technique.htm

## 5. Live-event translation

### Suitable general-stagehand examples for later reviewer selection

The responsible employer/lead should select low-risk, ordinary items representative of the actual workplace. Candidate categories include:

- empty or lightly loaded non-specialist production cases;
- tables or similar ordinary event-support items;
- non-energized, non-rigged staging/scenic components selected by the responsible lead;
- other objects the employer already permits entry-level crew to move as a team.

The course should **not** use an online lesson to authorize learners to practice with unusually heavy, overhead, powered, energized, suspended, unstable, regulated, or specialist equipment.

## 6. Course architecture recommendation

### Explain

The learner should be able to identify:

- the assigned object and destination;
- whether a handling aid or additional help is required;
- route/surface/visibility problems;
- the person coordinating the move;
- the stop condition and set-down location.

### Show

Use an employer-approved demonstration with an ordinary object and controlled route. The visual should emphasize planning, communication, route awareness and a controlled finish rather than presenting one body posture as universally correct for every load.

### Practice gate

Any observed practice must record:

- object/equipment used;
- approximate handling context rather than a universal “safe weight” claim;
- route/surface conditions;
- team/observer;
- handling aid used, if any;
- result: accepted / rework / stop.

### Teach-back

The learner explains why the selected crew/method was appropriate **for that recorded task**, and names a condition that would make them stop and return the decision to the lead.

## 7. Stop/escalate conditions

The curriculum should instruct the learner to stop and get the responsible lead when:

- the destination or route is unclear;
- the item is unstable, shifting, damaged, unusually awkward or hard to grip;
- sightlines are blocked and the team cannot restore route awareness;
- the surface or route changes materially;
- the assigned crew cannot start, carry, steer or set down the item under control;
- the item requires powered/mechanical handling or specialist equipment;
- a worker loses coordination or calls the agreed stop;
- the real task does not match the reviewed demonstration.

## 8. Explicit non-claims

This course must not claim that:

- OSHA permits every worker to lift up to one fixed weight;
- two people can safely lift any item above a threshold;
- a course completion proves strength or medical fitness;
- an observed practice authorizes every later team lift;
- a learner may ignore an employer lifting plan, mechanical aid, object-specific instruction or supervisor decision;
- this training substitutes for powered-equipment, rigging, electrical, machinery or other specialist qualification.
