## What this is

Package 02 already established the underlying finding: there is no single U.S. live-events career ladder. Real progression is department-specific and best modeled as five competency levels, with multiple real job titles mapping onto each level rather than one title per level:

| Level | Meaning | Example titles (from Package 02) |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Awareness / assist** | Follows direction, performs defined handling/setup tasks, recognizes when a task exceeds authorization | Audio Assist/A2, Lighting Hand, Video Assist, Ground Rigger, Electrical Assist, Carpentry Assist, Stagehand/Stage Builder Assist |
| **B — Technician under limited supervision** | Routine setup/strike/maintenance, reads basic documentation, troubleshoots common problems | (transition band within each department; titles overlap with Level C) |
| **C — Independent operator / system technician** | Owns an assigned system or subsystem, makes routine decisions within scope, troubleshoots under show pressure | A1/FOH/Monitor, Lighting Technician/Programmer, Video specialist (LED/projection/switching/media server/camera), qualified Rigger, Electrician/PPDT technician, Deck Carpenter, independent Stage Builder |
| **D — Lead / crew chief / department head** | Plans and advances departmental work, allocates labor, owns safety/quality, resolves non-routine problems, trains others | Audio Crew Chief/Head Audio, Lighting Crew Chief/Head Electrician, Video Crew Chief/Head Video, Head Rigger, Head/Production Electrician, Head Carpenter, Staging Crew Chief/Supervisor |
| **E — Design / systems / management branch** | Not a promotion from D — a parallel branch | Audio Designer/Systems Engineer, Lighting Designer, Video Designer/Director, Rigging Manager, Production Electrician, Technical Director, Production/Technical Manager |

This document maps the current Crew Blueprint course catalog onto this A–E structure, department by department, and names what's missing.

## Segments of the real live-events workforce with no dedicated Crew Blueprint course at any level

- **Security / crowd management.** Touched only tangentially (the Barricade Setup field skill, Predictive Hazard Recognition for supervisors). No department-level course.
- **Transportation / production trucking.** Only the Flatbed Cargo-Securement field skill (a helper-assist task), which deliberately grants no driver qualification. The transportation/logistics coordinator role itself — a real, named function in touring (advancing routes, truck packs, load schedules) that Package 02 surfaces under Carpentry's Head Carpenter duties — has no course of its own.
- **Pyrotechnics / special effects.** Not represented anywhere in the catalog.
- **Broadcast / camera / vision-mix.** Package 02 names Camera Engineering, Vision Mix/Switcher, and Media/Content Design as distinct Video branches; Crew Blueprint's Video Course 3 mentions cameras briefly but has no dedicated broadcast track.
- **Hospitality / catering / guest services.** Not represented.
- **Merch.** Tracked elsewhere in the ecosystem as a separate venture ("Stagehand Merch" in Roadmapdev's source registry), not as a Crew Blueprint workforce-role course.
- **Production Management, Stage Management, Venue/Event Operations.** Only one shared orientation course exists — `production-coordination-career-branch.html` — covering all three tracks together as a single "compare and enter" overview rather than three depth tracks. This gap is already named and queued: **ECQ-13** in the Expanded Curriculum Research Queue calls for track-specific practitioner interviews and a scenario/rubric per track. Nothing further is needed from this document on that point — it's already the correct next research action.

## What this changes for the curriculum architecture

Package 02's own conclusion — teach competency levels first, map multiple real titles onto each level, and never imply one universal ladder — is already how the Lighting, Video, Audio, and Staging/Carpentry course sequences are built (Course 1 = Level A, Course 2 = systems understanding bridging toward B/C, Lead = Level D, Course 3 = Level E). That architecture holds up under this mapping. The two real breaks from it are Rigging (missing the entire A–C layer) and Electrics (missing A/B, present at D/E).

## Recommended next actions, in priority order

1. **Rigging Course 1 (Level A — Ground Rigger awareness/assist).** Highest-priority gap: real, active, entry-level hiring demand exists for this exact role (Package 02, §4), and Crew Blueprint currently offers nothing between "general Fundamentals overhead-hazard awareness" and "Lead-tier decision content only." This does not require teaching physical rigging technique — it can follow the same "recognize, assist, stay within bounds" model as every other Course 1, scoped to ground-rigging support tasks, hardware recognition, and communication, with the same explicit no-high-work/no-point-selection boundary already used elsewhere.
2. **Electrics Course 1 (Level A — assigned electrical support).** Second-priority: closes the asymmetry with every other department and gives learners heading toward Electrics Lead an actual entry point, rather than folding department-specific content into general Fundamentals/Infrastructure awareness.
3. **A Package-02-equivalent research packet for Backline/Props/Wardrobe.** Lower urgency than the two above, but a real evidence gap: this pathway's title/progression claims currently rest on weaker primary sourcing than the other departments.
4. **Everything already queued stays queued as-is:** ECQ-13 (Production & Coordination three tracks) and ECQ-14 (Backline/Props/Wardrobe Course 3 build-or-split decision) already correctly name two of the gaps this map surfaces. This document does not propose new research for those — it confirms the existing queue items are the right next steps.
5. **Security, transportation-coordinator, pyro/SFX, broadcast/camera, hospitality, and merch** remain outside the current build scope. Naming them here is for completeness of the coverage map, not a recommendation to start research immediately — that's an owner prioritization decision, not one this document makes for you.

## What this document does not do

- It does not create new course content or research packets — it maps what exists against what Package 02 already established about the real workforce.
- It does not second-guess the deliberate "no hands-on independent-operator instruction" boundary in Lighting/Video/Audio Course 2 — that's a legal/safety design choice already documented, not a gap.
- It does not rank the workforce-segment list (Security, transportation, pyro, broadcast, hospitality, merch) as things Crew Blueprint should build — that's flagged as a coverage fact, with the actual go/no-go left to the owner.
