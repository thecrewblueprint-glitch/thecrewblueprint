# SFS-U06 — Ground-Level Soft Goods Handling

**Prepared:** 2026-08-31  
**Status:** Source packet complete enough for bounded initial drafting; practitioner/venue review remains  
**Domain:** Stagehand/general-hand practical evidence  
**Course-format decision:** Deferred

## Research question

What can The Crew Blueprint responsibly teach a new stagehand about identifying, moving, handling, folding/staging and protecting theatrical/event soft goods at ground level without implying fly-system, rigging, fire-code, installation or final-acceptance authority?

## Bottom-line finding

Soft goods are ordinary stagecraft/event-production material and belong in the early-career evidence universe. A beginner can be taught transferable handling principles—identify the item, confirm ownership/destination, keep fabric clean/dry/protected, avoid dragging/crushing/contaminating it, use the assigned fold/roll/storage method, report damage or missing flame-retardancy documentation concerns, and hand the item back to the responsible department.

The course must **not** teach one universal fold, attachment, fullness, track, fly, flame-retardancy approval or installation method. Exact practices depend on the fabric, curtain construction, venue, department, hardware, manufacturer and local fire/life-safety authority.

## Evidence

### 1. Soft goods are established stagecraft material

Rose Brand identifies stage curtains, masking, backdrops, scrims, cycs, arena curtains and other drapery as standard theatre/event products used in venues, arenas and convention environments.

Source:
- Rose Brand — Stage Curtains, Backdrops & Drapery  
  https://www.rosebrand.com/custom-curtain-services/stage-curtains-backdrops-drapery

IATSE training/reference material already used in the Crew Blueprint corpus identifies soft goods and related stagecraft as routine stagehand knowledge.

Sources in current corpus:
- IATSE Local 193 — Soft Goods  
  https://www.iatselocal193.com/soft-goods
- IATSE Local 15 — Entry Test Study Guide  
  https://ia15.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Local-15-Entry-Test-Study-Guide-ver-1.2-compressed.pdf

### 2. Clean handling and protected storage matter

Rose Brand's care guidance for velour draperies warns against crushing stored drape under heavy objects and describes dust/contamination as a significant maintenance concern. Its guidance recommends protecting drape from dirty surfaces when laid out and maintaining clean handling/storage conditions.

Source:
- Rose Brand — Care & Maintenance of Velour Draperies  
  https://www.rosebrand.com/downloads/Care-Maintenance-Velour-Draperies.pdf

Curriculum implication:

Teach a general hand to:

- avoid dragging fabric across dirty/rough surfaces;
- keep fabric away from standing water, oil, paint and uncontrolled contamination;
- do not put heavy cases/objects onto bagged or folded drape unless the responsible department explicitly directs a storage method that permits it;
- use the assigned hamper/bag/cart/roll/fold system;
- report damage/contamination rather than silently packing it away.

Do not teach cleaning chemicals or repair methods as beginner practicals.

### 3. Flame-retardancy is an authority/documentation issue, not a beginner judgment call

Rose Brand distinguishes inherently flame-retardant (IFR), treated flame-retardant (FR) and non-flame-retardant (NFR) fabrics and notes that certifications/acceptance depend on jurisdiction. Rose Brand's fire-retardant guidance states that the local fire marshal/building inspector is the final authority for approval.

Sources:
- Rose Brand — Stage Curtains / FR FAQ  
  https://www.rosebrand.com/custom-curtain-services/stage-curtains-backdrops-drapery
- Rose Brand — Fire Retardants  
  https://www.rosebrand.com/products/scenic-paint-shop-supplies/fire-retardants

Curriculum implication:

A beginner may be taught to recognize that flame-retardancy documentation can matter and to escalate missing/uncertain documentation. The learner should **not** be taught to self-certify fabric, improvise a field flame test, apply chemical treatments, or decide that a drape is code-compliant.

### 4. Cleaning can affect treated fabric

Rose Brand's care guidance warns that some cleaning processes can remove or affect flame-retardant treatment and therefore require proper re-treatment/re-certification processes.

Source:
- Rose Brand — Care & Maintenance of Velour Draperies  
  https://www.rosebrand.com/downloads/Care-Maintenance-Velour-Draperies.pdf

Curriculum implication:

Do not teach a new stagehand to spot-clean or chemically treat drape unless explicitly assigned under the controlling department/manufacturer process.

## Transferable learner outcome

A bounded early-career learner should be able to:

1. recognize common soft-goods categories at a basic level;
2. identify the responsible department/lead before handling;
3. confirm destination/orientation/storage instruction;
4. move/pass/fold/roll/stage fabric using the method assigned for that item;
5. keep fabric off dirty/wet/abrasive surfaces where practicable;
6. avoid crushing, uncontrolled dragging or contamination;
7. report tears, hardware damage, contamination, missing labels or documentation concerns;
8. stop when the task crosses into fly-system operation, overhead attachment, track/motor work, fire-code acceptance or an unfamiliar system.

## What the evidence does not support as universal

Do not claim:

- one universal fold for all drape;
- one universal orientation/centerline method;
- one universal number of workers;
- one universal storage method;
- every black curtain is flame retardant;
- every IFR/FR label is automatically accepted by every AHJ;
- a worker can determine code compliance by appearance;
- soft-goods handling grants fly/rigging/track authority.
