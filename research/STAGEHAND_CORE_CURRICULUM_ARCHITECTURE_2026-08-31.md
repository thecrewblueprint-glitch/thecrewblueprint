# Stagehand Core — Evidence-First Working Hypothesis

**Prepared:** 2026-08-31  
**Status:** Research lens only — final curriculum structure intentionally deferred  
**Branch:** `research/mdq-010-stagehand-field-skills-universe`  
**Purpose:** Preserve useful observations about how stagehand work unfolds without prematurely locking The Crew Blueprint into a course structure before the evidence corpus is complete.

## Controlling decision

The Crew Blueprint is now in an **evidence-completion phase**, not a curriculum-format phase.

Do **not** treat the current Stagehand Core layout, the existing four-part Fundamentals layout, a single catalog, or the call-cycle model as the final course architecture.

The current evidence suggests that real stagehand work can be studied through a recurring call cycle:

> **Prepare → Arrive → Check in → Orient → Get assigned → Understand the task → Execute → Verify / hand off → Become available for the next task → Standby / break when directed → Repeat → Strike / load out → Final handoff → Release / timekeeping → Grow**

That sequence is useful for finding evidence gaps. It is **not yet a product/navigation decision**.

Likewise, a transferable task loop appears repeatedly across beginner work:

> **Receive → Confirm → Inspect → Plan → Execute → Protect → Verify → Hand off → Reset → Seek next assignment**

This is a working instructional model that must remain labeled as Crew Blueprint framing unless external evidence supports a stronger claim.

## Evidence-first rule

Before deciding whether the final learner experience should be:

- one large course;
- Fundamentals plus a field-skills catalog;
- a linear call-cycle course;
- modular skill cards;
- multiple prerequisite courses;
- a competency graph;
- adaptive learning paths;
- or another structure;

complete the content evidence program and inspect the resulting competency relationships.

The final structure should be chosen **after** the project can answer:

1. What does a new stagehand actually encounter in the first 3–6 months?
2. Which tasks are genuinely frequent across markets and sectors?
3. Which tasks vary by employer, venue, union local, manufacturer, department, equipment model, or jurisdiction?
4. Which knowledge must precede a physical task?
5. Which tasks require observed practice?
6. Which tasks require employer authorization or external qualification?
7. Which concepts repeat across many tasks and therefore belong in shared instruction?
8. Which concepts are better taught at the moment of need?
9. What progression actually helps workers become more useful and move toward department, technical, leadership, or management roles?
10. What claims can be supported strongly enough for public publication?

## Beginner scope hypothesis

Current evidence supports treating the following as ordinary early-career/general-stagehand subject matter, although frequency and exact method vary:

- road-case movement and parking;
- team lifting/carrying;
- carts, dollies and hand trucks;
- cable handling, coiling, deployment and gathering;
- cable-path protection;
- case/boneyard/work-area organization;
- housekeeping/reset/handoff;
- barricade handling/setup under the responsible plan;
- pipe-and-drape support;
- soft-goods handling;
- riser/deck/platform component support;
- scenery/flat movement;
- ordinary stagehand hand tools;
- tape/label use;
- dock/ramp/trailer awareness;
- ratchet straps when assigned;
- cargo-securement support when assigned and bounded from regulated/driver/load-lead responsibility;
- communication, check-in, task handoff, reassignment, standby, breaks, release and timekeeping awareness.

This list is a **research universe**, not a final lesson list.

## Controlled / specialist boundary hypothesis

Current evidence still supports keeping the following outside a general self-study practical until a stronger qualification model exists:

- overhead rigging and hoist work;
- climbing/fall-protection work;
- energized temporary-power connection/testing/fault work;
- powered industrial-truck operation;
- MEWP operation;
- stage-automation machinery operation;
- pyrotechnics/flame/cryo/laser operation;
- regulated transport/cargo authority;
- any procedure inseparable from a legal, employer, manufacturer, competent-person, qualified-person, venue or AHJ authorization requirement.

Awareness, role architecture, hazards, stop conditions and career pathways can still be researched and taught where appropriate.

## Professional-work-rhythm hypothesis

A recurring practical principle is:

> **Do available assigned work well. When the task is complete, hand it off and become available for the next legitimate assignment. When production itself is waiting, remain appropriately available.**

The site should not teach artificial constant activity, unauthorized work creation, hiding after a task, or one universal break/timekeeping rule.

Breaks, meal periods, standby, show-call waiting, release and timekeeping can be governed by employer policy, local/CBA terms, venue practice, production rules and law. Those subjects require their own source comparison before final learner wording.

## Evidence/liability states to preserve regardless of final UX

The final system should continue to distinguish at least:

1. content viewed;
2. knowledge demonstrated;
3. practice prepared;
4. observed practice completed on named equipment/conditions;
5. employer/responsible authority permission for actual work;
6. external qualification/certification/license where relevant.

No earlier state should automatically imply a later state.

## Decision gate

Final curriculum structure is deferred until:

- the Stagehand/general-work evidence universe is substantially complete;
- the major department research waves are complete enough to expose dependencies;
- the site-wide evidence matrix can show overlap and gaps;
- customer/learner research has tested what users actually understand and need;
- product/backend requirements are known well enough to support the selected learning model.

At that point, choose the course architecture from evidence rather than from the shape of the current website.
