# The Crew Blueprint — Full-Project Evidence-First Research Program

**Prepared:** 2026-08-31  
**Status:** Active master research program  
**Scope:** Curriculum/content, learner/customer, market/business, product/backend, data/analytics, compliance/risk, testing/validation, operations and release strategy.

## 1. Program decision

The Crew Blueprint is past an MVP-only research posture.

The next phase is **broad evidence accumulation before major product/curriculum architecture decisions**.

The project should not optimize around the current static site simply because it exists. The static site is a useful delivery surface and research prototype. The eventual system may include accounts, progress tracking, practice records, reviewer/instructor workflows, analytics, versioned content, scheduled releases and other backend capabilities.

The correct sequence is:

> **Gather evidence → map evidence → identify gaps/conflicts → synthesize requirements → choose curriculum/product architecture → build backend/infrastructure → test with real users → stage releases → measure → improve**

This program intentionally separates **what must be learned** from **what will eventually be built**.

### FPR-LRN — Learner / customer understanding

**Question:** Who uses the product, what are they trying to accomplish, what do they already know, where do they fail, and what experience actually helps them learn and progress?

Research families:

#### Learner segments

- never-worked-a-call beginner;
- first 1–6 months stagehand;
- recurring general hand seeking department exposure;
- department-support worker;
- technician/operator learner;
- worker pursuing lead/supervisor responsibility;
- career changer;
- venue/rental-shop worker;
- union/nonunion/labor-provider worker;
- mobile/touring worker;
- employer/crew lead assigning or reviewing training;
- practitioner/reviewer/instructor.

#### Jobs to be done

Examples to validate rather than assume:

- understand what will happen on the first call;
- avoid looking lost or unsafe;
- recognize equipment and vocabulary;
- perform common assignments better;
- know when to stop and ask;
- gain confidence without overstating competence;
- move toward Lighting/Audio/Video/Staging/etc.;
- prove learning or observed practice;
- find legitimate training/credential pathways;
- let a lead/employer see what a worker has actually learned/practiced;
- refresh a skill before a call.

#### Research methods

- structured learner interviews;
- practitioner/crew-lead interviews;
- first-call diary studies;
- task observation where permitted;
- mobile usability tests;
- comprehension tests;
- scenario-based assessment validation;
- delayed-retention checks;
- observed-practice reliability testing;
- search/navigation studies;
- onboarding completion analysis;
- support/contact themes;
- accessibility testing with real assistive technology/users where possible.

#### Key questions

- Do users want a linear course, task lookup, role pathway, competency map or hybrid?
- What do workers need immediately before/during/after a call?
- Which visuals materially improve understanding?
- How much text is usable on a phone at a jobsite?
- What progress/status language do learners interpret as certification or permission?
- What makes users trust a source-backed course?
- What causes drop-off?
- Which achievements feel meaningful without creating false credential claims?

**Done enough for architecture when:** learner segments and needs are evidence-backed and the project can distinguish content requirements from interface preferences.

## 4. Evidence-before-architecture gates

### Gate A — Content evidence

Before final curriculum restructuring:

- major content domains researched;
- Stagehand evidence universe substantially complete;
- major department gaps researched;
- source matrix exposes overlap and unsupported claims;
- practitioner/external-boundary questions visible.

### Gate B — Learner/customer evidence

Before final UX/pathway decisions:

- learner segments validated;
- task lookup versus linear/pathway needs tested;
- mobile/visual requirements tested;
- customer/buyer requirements known.

### Gate C — Business evidence

Before paid-product architecture:

- viable customer/revenue models compared;
- infrastructure/support/review costs modeled;
- B2B/B2C priorities evidence-backed.

### Gate D — Backend/compliance evidence

Before choosing database/auth/data model:

- user roles/workflows known;
- progress/practice/authorization semantics resolved;
- privacy/security/accessibility requirements mapped;
- analytics purpose defined.

### Gate E — Release/testing evidence

Before scheduled public expansion:

- validation methods defined;
- beta/audit workflow works;
- correction/versioning process exists;
- release cadence is sustainable.

## 7. Decision log principles

- **Evidence can change architecture.** Do not protect earlier design choices from better research.
- **Current implementation is not authority.** Existing HTML/navigation is a baseline, not the final product specification.
- **Do not confuse demand with anecdotes.** Customer/business claims need actual evidence.
- **Do not confuse engagement with learning.** Learning effectiveness needs comprehension/retention/transfer evidence.
- **Do not confuse completion with qualification.** Practice, authorization and external credentials remain separate.
- **Do not over-collect data.** Backend tracking must have a clear purpose.
- **Design for correction.** Content and sources will change; versioning/auditability are core requirements.

## 8. End-state this program is trying to enable

The intended result is not merely a larger website.

The research should make it possible to build a system that can:

- teach accurate, highly visual live-event knowledge and skills;
- explain authority/liability boundaries clearly;
- support career progression;
- maintain exact evidence lineage;
- track meaningful learning/practice states;
- support accounts and role-based workflows where justified;
- give users and organizations useful progress information without false credential claims;
- test whether learning actually works;
- release new content deliberately over time;
- keep a backlog of researched material ahead of the public release schedule;
- allow infrastructure, testing and research to continue while users receive stable scheduled improvements.

That is the target research posture for the next phase of The Crew Blueprint.
