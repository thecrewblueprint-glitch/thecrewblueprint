# Frontier Product Operating Model

**Prepared:** 2026-08-31  
**Status:** Execution architecture; customer hypotheses remain hypotheses until validated

## 1. Product layers

### Layer A — Transferable Crew Blueprint core
Industry vocabulary, ordinary job readiness, mental models, bounded field skills, systems literacy, professional practice, and career navigation.

### Layer B — Employer / venue overlay
A future organization should be able to attach its own:
- policies and reporting channels;
- venue orientation;
- site maps/routes;
- call and timekeeping practices;
- approved equipment/model training;
- department-specific procedures;
- supervisor/observer assignments;
- authorization records.

**Rule:** Layer B may narrow or add requirements; it does not let Crew Blueprint silently convert education into employer authorization.

### Layer C — Evidence and progression
Keep distinct states:
1. learned/exposed;
2. knowledge check passed;
3. practice attempted;
4. observed practice recorded;
5. work experience self-reported;
6. experience externally verified;
7. employer/site authorized;
8. external training completed;
9. externally certified;
10. licensed/legally qualified where applicable.

Never collapse these into one badge.

## 2. Buyer architecture

Keep separate value propositions for:
- individual worker;
- labor provider;
- production company;
- venue/house operation;
- rental/AV company;
- union/local or referral hall;
- school/workforce program.

Do not finalize pricing from curriculum completeness. Primary customer discovery still controls packaging, procurement, integrations, record-retention needs, and willingness to pay.

## 3. Practitioner validation network

Every course should carry reviewer state:
- unreviewed research foundation;
- domain practitioner reviewed;
- employer/venue workflow reviewed;
- manufacturer/product reviewed where product-specific;
- legal/professional reviewed where legally sensitive;
- learner tested.

Reviewer comments should attach to claims/lessons, not merely a blanket course approval.

## 4. Accessibility release gate

Before commercial/organization release, require:
- keyboard-only review;
- semantic heading/forms review;
- focus visibility/order;
- contrast/zoom checks;
- text alternatives for meaningful visuals;
- caption/transcript for meaningful audio/video;
- screen-reader spot checks;
- quiz error/status announcements;
- reduced-motion review where motion exists.

Automation can find defects but does not replace manual/accessibility-user review.

## 5. Low-bandwidth/offline strategy

Course content should remain progressively usable without heavy video. Design source-of-truth content so it can support:
- text-first web;
- downloadable reference cards/PDF where appropriate;
- cached/offline packages later;
- optional video rather than video-only knowledge;
- transcripts and still sequences.

## 6. Plain-language / multilingual strategy

First stabilize canonical English terminology and authority boundaries. Then build controlled translations from versioned source text, with reviewer metadata and no machine-only translation of safety-critical instructions released as authoritative.

## 7. Release maturity labels

Suggested internal states:
- `mapped`
- `research_foundation`
- `authored`
- `matrixed`
- `practitioner_reviewed`
- `learner_validated`
- `organization_ready`

Public visibility and maturity are separate. A route can be visible for owner/workspace inspection while still clearly labeled a research foundation.

## 8. Immediate operating rule

Broad subject expansion is no longer the default priority. New routes should address a verified frontier gap. Most effort should now move into authored depth, assessment validity, media, practitioner review, learner testing, accessibility, and organization overlays.
