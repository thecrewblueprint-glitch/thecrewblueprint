# SFS-U08 — Riser / Deck / Portable-Platform Component Support

**Prepared:** 2026-08-31  
**Status:** Source packet complete for recognition, movement, staging and authority boundaries; detailed structural assembly procedures intentionally excluded  
**Domain:** Stagehand/general-hand practical evidence  
**Course-format decision:** Deferred

## Research question

What can The Crew Blueprint responsibly teach a new stagehand about recognizing, moving, staging and supporting portable riser/deck/platform components without teaching structural assembly, leveling, locking, guardrail/stair acceptance or load-capacity decisions that belong to the exact manufacturer system and responsible authority?

## Bottom-line finding

Portable risers, stage decks and platform systems are normal live-event/venue equipment and belong in the beginner/general-stagehand evidence universe. However, manufacturer systems vary substantially in supports, locks, bracing, heights, deck sizes, stairs, rails and allowable configurations.

A beginner can be taught transferable support behavior:

- identify the actual system and component type;
- confirm destination/layout and responsible lead;
- move components with appropriate team/mechanical assistance;
- protect fingers/feet and maintain route awareness;
- stage components where directed without blocking egress or creating unstable stacks;
- report obvious damage/missing/mismatched parts;
- wait for the responsible lead/manufacturer process before treating the platform as ready for use.

This packet does **not** authorize or teach independent structural assembly or acceptance.

### 3. Weight, configuration and support requirements vary

Manufacturer specifications show that individual components can be heavy and that different systems use different support/bracing arrangements and setup-team expectations.

Source examples:

Curriculum implication:

Do not teach a universal person-count threshold or one maximum manual-handling weight. Use the existing Crew Blueprint manual-handling evidence: assess the actual component, use mechanical/team assistance when needed and follow employer/manufacturer direction.

## Transferable learner outcome

A bounded learner can be prepared to:

1. recognize a deck/platform, support/riser frame and common accessory categories;
2. identify the actual manufacturer/system where possible;
3. confirm destination/layout and responsible lead before moving or placing components;
4. inspect for obvious cracks, deformation, missing hardware or mismatched parts and report them;
5. move/stage components using safe material-handling principles and adequate help;
6. keep routes/exits/public paths clear during staging;
7. protect components from uncontrolled drops/impacts/unstable storage;
8. return final assembly/acceptance to the responsible lead/manufacturer process;
9. stop when the system, support, height, accessory, outdoor/site condition or structural state is unfamiliar or unclear.

## Explicit exclusions

Do not teach as a general self-study practical:

- exact structural assembly sequence;
- independent leveling;
- locking/bracing acceptance;
- guardrail/stair/ramp installation procedure;
- load-capacity calculations;
- mixed-system substitutions;
- outdoor/uneven-ground engineering decisions;
- structural inspection/signoff;
- public-occupancy acceptance.

Those depend on exact manufacturer instructions and responsible/qualified authority.

## Visual evidence needs

Safe future visuals can focus on:

- component-category recognition;
- system labels/identifiers;
- damaged/mismatched component examples;
- stable directed staging versus blocked/unstable storage;
- team/material-handling planning;
- decision boundary: `move/stage/report` vs `assemble/level/accept`.

Do not publish generic step-by-step structural assembly illustrations as transferable rules.
