# MDQ-020 / 030 / 040 / 050 / 060 / 070 — Major Department Role Architectures

**Prepared:** 2026-08-31  
**Status:** Role-architecture synthesis complete enough to drive detailed competency research  
**Domains:** Lighting, Audio, Video/LED/Broadcast, Staging/Scenic, Rigging, Electrics/Production Power  
**Purpose:** Establish the real role/responsibility landscape before writing deeper department curricula. Titles vary by market, employer, venue, union local, tour and sector; this file maps responsibilities rather than claiming one universal title ladder.

---

# 1. Shared architecture rule

Career depth has at least three independent dimensions:

1. **technical depth** — how much of the system the worker understands/owns;
2. **work-package leadership** — whether the worker plans/assigns/verifies other people's work;
3. **external authority** — employer appointment, qualified-person status, certification, license, venue/AHJ authority, or other controlled permission.

A worker can be technically deep without being a crew lead, or a strong crew lead without being the deepest technical specialist.

Crew Blueprint therefore must not present each department as a single ladder where “Course 3 = boss.”

# 2. Lighting — MDQ-020 Role Architecture

## L0 — General Stagehand / Lighting Support Interface

Typical bounded contributions:

- move/stage lighting cases and assigned components;
- assist cable/material routing under lighting direction;
- recognize common fixture/cable/control categories;
- maintain routes/work areas;
- report obvious damage/missing labels;
- preserve technical setup and ask before altering it.

Not implied:

- independent electrical connection/energization;
- console programming;
- network configuration;
- rigging authority;
- fixture repair;
- design decisions.

## L1 — Lighting Department Support Technician

Potential responsibilities under department direction:

- fixture/component identification;
- prep/label/stage work;
- assigned cable/data support;
- accessories/hardware support;
- focus-support tasks where trained/assigned;
- shop/warehouse prep/QC support;
- documentation/checklist support.

The exact boundary depends heavily on employer/venue/local and power/rigging rules.

## L2 — Lighting Technician / Electrician / System Technician

Common responsibility families:

- fixture deployment and system build within assigned scope;
- data/control topology implementation;
- patch/address/device setup where authorized;
- fixture configuration;
- fault isolation;
- documentation updates;
- system verification with the department lead.

Title “electrician” is especially sector/jurisdiction dependent and must not be equated automatically with a licensed construction electrician or with unrestricted electrical authority.

## L3 — Console Operator / Programmer

Parallel specialist path rather than automatic promotion from technician.

Competencies may include:

- console architecture;
- patch/show-file management;
- cue/programming workflows;
- playback/timecode/show-control interfaces;
- data/device/network awareness;
- documentation and change control;
- show execution.

Brand-specific operation remains manufacturer/platform specific.

## L3b — Lighting Network / Controls Specialist

Parallel specialization:

- DMX/RDM/sACN/Art-Net/system topology;
- switching/network infrastructure;
- monitoring/diagnostics;
- redundancy and addressing architecture;
- documentation.

This should not be hidden inside “console programmer.”

## L3c — Followspot / Specialized Operator

Performance-critical operator role with its own cue/communication/optical/positioning context. Exact equipment and procedures vary.

## L4 — Lighting Lead / Head Electrician / Crew Chief

Responsibility families:

- interpret plan/advance information;
- assign and verify work;
- coordinate lighting labor;
- preserve department schedule/sequence;
- track changes/issues;
- coordinate with rigging, power, video/scenic and production;
- accept/reject completed work within assigned authority.

Leadership does not automatically grant design, engineering, licensing or venue/AHJ authority.

## L5 — Lighting Designer / Systems Designer / Advanced Specialist

Parallel advanced lane:

- requirements and artistic/technical intent;
- system architecture;
- fixture/control/network documentation;
- visualization/previs/interchange;
- programming/design collaboration;
- commissioning/acceptance planning;
- sector-specific design responsibility.

**Lighting architecture conclusion:** Technician, programmer, network specialist, lead and designer are related but distinct paths.

# 3. Audio — MDQ-030 Role Architecture

## A0 — General Stagehand / Audio Support Interface

- move/stage audio cases and assigned gear;
- assist cable/material flow under audio direction;
- recognize common audio equipment categories;
- maintain cable/work paths;
- preserve patches/settings unless assigned.

No independent system patch/configuration/mixing authority implied.

## A1 — Audio Department Support / Stage Audio Support

Potential responsibilities:

- microphone/stand/cable support;
- assigned stage patch/cable work;
- monitor/stage equipment placement;
- labeling/organization;
- shop prep/QC support;
- documentation support.

## A2 — Audio Technician / System Technician

Potential system ownership:

- signal-flow implementation;
- patching/routing under system plan;
- console/stagebox/system configuration within assigned scope;
- loudspeaker/electronics system support;
- network audio implementation;
- testing/troubleshooting;
- documentation.

Rigging/structural/electrical authority remains separate.

## A3a — FOH Mixer / Operator

Specialist performance path:

- artistic/technical mix execution;
- input processing;
- routing/bus/control workflows;
- show files/scenes/snapshots;
- communication with system/stage teams.

## A3b — Monitor Engineer

Separate specialist path:

- performer monitoring workflows;
- stage communication;
- mix/routing management;
- show execution;
- coordination with RF/stage audio.

FOH competence does not automatically equal monitor competence and vice versa.

## A3c — RF Coordinator / Wireless Specialist

Separate specialist path:

- wireless system inventory/coordination;
- spectrum planning/monitoring;
- frequency/intermodulation workflows;
- antenna/distribution ecosystem;
- documentation/troubleshooting.

FCC/regulatory and manufacturer/system rules apply.

## A3d — Audio Network Specialist

- AES67/Dante/other ecosystem awareness and implementation;
- clocking/synchronization;
- multicast/network concepts;
- redundancy;
- addressing/routing;
- troubleshooting/documentation.

## A4 — Systems Engineer / Optimization Specialist

Advanced technical path:

- system architecture;
- prediction/coverage/system tuning concepts;
- measurement/verification;
- deployment strategy;
- redundancy/failure planning;
- cross-team technical coordination.

## A4b — Audio Lead / A1 / Department Head

Leadership/work-package lane:

- advance/plan/assign/verify;
- coordinate FOH/monitors/RF/system/stage teams;
- manage changes/issues;
- preserve department schedule and production interfaces.

Titles such as A1/A2 vary strongly by sector and should be explained contextually.

## A5 — Audio Designer / Advanced Systems Designer

Requirements, system architecture, artistic/technical design, documentation and advanced sector-specific responsibilities.

**Audio architecture conclusion:** FOH, Monitor, RF, Networks, Systems Engineering and Department Leadership must not be collapsed into one “advanced audio technician.”

# 4. Video / LED / Broadcast — MDQ-040 Role Architecture

## V0 — General Stagehand / Video Support Interface

- move/stage cases, panels/components and assigned video equipment;
- assist material/cable flow under direction;
- recognize fragile/technical interfaces;
- maintain clear work routes;
- avoid changing mappings/patches/settings.

## V1 — Video / LED Support Technician

Potential responsibilities:

- LED component handling/organization;
- panel/module/cable recognition;
- assigned physical build support;
- displays/projectors/accessory prep;
- fiber/copper cable support;
- shop prep/QC support;
- documentation/label support.

Structural rigging, energized power and final system acceptance remain separate.

## V2 — Video / LED Systems Technician

Potential responsibility:

- processor/receiver/signal-chain implementation;
- mapping/configuration within assigned system;
- scaling/conversion/distribution;
- display/LED fault isolation;
- fiber/network signal transport;
- test patterns/documentation;
- system verification.

## V3a — Playback / Media-Server Operator

- media/content organization;
- playback/output mapping;
- cue/show execution;
- synchronization/timecode interfaces;
- show-file/version control;
- platform-specific operation.

## V3b — Switcher / Vision Mixer / Technical Director

Performance/broadcast/corporate lane:

- source switching;
- keying/layering/transition logic;
- cue/show execution;
- coordination with cameras/playback/graphics.

Title “technical director” varies by sector and must not be generalized.

## V3c — Camera Operator / Utility / Camera Systems Lane

Separate path including:

- camera/support equipment interfaces;
- utility/cable/support roles;
- operator framing/show execution;
- camera-chain/shading/CCU roles at deeper levels;
- tally/intercom integration.

## V3d — Video Network / Signal Transport Specialist

- fiber/network/video-over-IP transport;
- distribution/routing;
- timing/synchronization concepts;
- redundancy;
- diagnostics/documentation.

## V4 — Video Engineer / EIC / System Lead

Advanced integration/lead path:

- system architecture;
- processing/routing/network coordination;
- verification/troubleshooting;
- cross-domain synchronization;
- team/work-package coordination.

Titles such as EIC are sector-specific.

## V4b — Video / LED Lead

Leadership lane:

- advance/plan/assign/verify;
- coordinate physical LED/display deployment and technical system team;
- manage schedule/changes/spares/issues;
- interface with rigging/power/production/content teams.

## V5 — Video Systems Designer / Broadcast Engineer / Advanced Specialist

Requirements, architecture, documentation, commissioning and advanced sector-specific design responsibilities.

**Video architecture conclusion:** LED technician, playback, switcher, camera, video-network and engineering tracks are parallel specializations, not one linear ladder.

# 5. Staging / Carpentry / Scenic — MDQ-050 Role Architecture

## S0 — General Stagehand / Scenic-Staging Support

- move/stage deck, riser, scenic, soft-goods and pipe/drape components as assigned;
- maintain routes/work areas;
- recognize damage/labels/orientation;
- hand off completed physical work to responsible stage/scenic authority.

No structural/fly/automation authority implied.

## S1 — Staging / Deck Support Technician

Potential responsibilities:

- component identification;
- system-specific placement/build support under direction;
- stairs/rails/skirting/accessory support;
- assigned inspection/condition reporting;
- shop/storage/QC support.

Final leveling/locking/structural acceptance remains with the responsible system/lead/manufacturer/venue process.

## S1b — Scenic / Carpentry Support

- flats/wagons/scenic component movement;
- basic scenic hardware/tool support;
- presets/spike marks;
- shop/material organization;
- assigned carpentry support within training.

## S2 — Stage / Scenic Carpenter / Staging Technician

Deeper technical path:

- system-specific staging/scenic assembly;
- materials/fasteners/hardware knowledge;
- repair/fabrication within role;
- scenic movement/preset systems;
- technical interpretation/documentation;
- troubleshooting.

Powered tools/fabrication require training/controls; structural/fly/engineering boundaries remain separate.

## S2b — Soft Goods / Stagecraft Specialist

Potential deeper domain:

- masking/drapery systems;
- handling/storage/fire-treatment documentation interfaces;
- track/attachment systems;
- stagecraft planning.

Overhead/fly-system use remains controlled.

## S3 — Fly / Automation / Machinery Specialist

Separate controlled path, not a generic promotion step.

Role architecture should map:

- installed fly-system roles;
- automation operators/technicians;
- machinery/control interfaces;
- maintenance/inspection responsibilities;
- show-critical operation.

Crew Blueprint general curriculum stays at awareness/interface level until specialist research/authority is appropriate.

## S4 — Head Carpenter / Staging Lead / Scenic Lead

Leadership/work-package path:

- advance/plan/assign/verify;
- coordinate staging/scenic labor;
- manage sequence/changeovers;
- interface with rigging, automation, lighting, audio, video and production;
- accept completed work within assigned authority.

## S5 — Technical Director / Scenic Designer / Structural-Engineering Interface

These are distinct advanced roles. “Technical Director” can include planning, engineering coordination, fabrication/shop oversight, budgeting and production integration depending on sector.

Structural engineering/AHJ/licensed work stays external where applicable.

**Staging architecture conclusion:** portable staging, scenic carpentry, soft goods, fly/automation, leadership and technical direction are connected but materially different lanes.

# 6. Rigging — MDQ-060 Role Architecture

> Rigging is mapped for education/career/authority understanding. This file does not provide physical rigging instructions.

## R0 — General Stagehand Rigging Interface

Safe educational scope:

- recognize rigging zones/roles;
- understand that overhead loads/hoists/hardware are controlled work;
- know not to alter/interfere with rigging systems;
- follow exclusion/communication instructions;
- escalate to the responsible rigger/lead.

## R1 — Ground-Rigging Support / Ground Rigger Role Family

Professional role family to research, not automatically authorize through online completion.

Competency-map topics include:

- hardware/system recognition;
- communication/coordination with up-riggers/lead;
- hoist/truss/point workflow concepts;
- inspection/selection/documentation responsibility boundaries;
- venue/production procedures.

No step-by-step overhead rigging tutorial is part of general learner content.

## R2 — Arena Up-Rigger / High-Rigging Role Family

Experienced safety-critical specialist path involving work at height and overhead systems. ETCP Arena Rigging certification is an important external credential reference.

## R2b — Theatre Rigger / Fly-System Specialist

Distinct installed/theatre path involving theatre rigging/fly systems and venue-specific equipment/procedures. ETCP Theatre Rigging is an external credential reference.

## R3 — Rigging Lead / Head Rigger

Leadership/technical authority path:

- coordinate rigging crew/work packages;
- interpret venue/production plans;
- manage communication and sequence;
- verify assigned work within authority;
- interface with venue/production/engineering/department heads.

## R4 — Rigging Designer / Engineer / Qualified-Person Interface

Advanced planning/engineering/venue authority may involve:

- load information;
- structural/engineering review;
- point/plan documentation;
- code/AHJ/venue requirements;
- licensed/qualified engineering where applicable.

This is not created by Crew Blueprint training.

## External credential reference

ETCP Arena and Theatre Rigging certifications are designed for experienced technicians and use work-experience/eligibility requirements.

Sources:  
https://etcp.esta.org/certify/certify_rigger.html  
https://etcp.esta.org/certify/examination_rigger_arena.html

**Rigging architecture conclusion:** general awareness → professional ground/up/theatre role families → lead → engineering/venue authority, with qualification and work-at-height authority remaining external.

# 7. Electrics / Production Power — MDQ-070 Role Architecture

> This section maps professional responsibilities and learning boundaries; it does not provide live-power connection, testing, or fault-work instructions.

## E0 — General Stagehand Power Interface

Safe educational scope:

- recognize that production power is controlled work;
- identify obvious damaged-equipment/route hazards and report them;
- protect assigned cable routes under direction;
- avoid altering energized distribution/patching;
- know who owns the power system.

## E1 — Department Power Support Interface

Potential bounded support may include:

- equipment/cable identification and staging;
- documentation/label support;
- physical routing/protection under assigned plan;
- shop prep/QC support appropriate to role.

No energized connection/testing authority is implied.

## E2 — Entertainment Electrician / Production-Power Technician Role Family

Professional technical lane may include:

- portable distribution implementation;
- system connection/testing/verification;
- power/data/equipment coordination;
- troubleshooting;
- documentation;
- code/employer/venue/AHJ requirements.

Exact authority depends on jurisdiction, employer, venue and qualified-person/licensing requirements.

## E2b — Portable Power Distribution Specialist

ETCP Portable Power Distribution Technician is a useful external reference for experienced professional competency.

## E3 — Head Electrician / Power Lead

Leadership/work-package path:

- advance/plan/assign/verify;
- coordinate department loads/interfaces;
- manage documentation/change control;
- coordinate source/generator/venue utility interfaces;
- enforce qualified-person and site boundaries;
- communicate with production/lighting/audio/video/rigging/venue teams.

## E4 — Electrical Designer / Engineer / Licensed-AHJ Interface

Advanced authority can include system design, engineering, code interpretation, venue/AHJ coordination and licensed work where required.

Crew Blueprint does not issue this authority.

## External references

ETCP offers experienced-technician credentials for Entertainment Electrician and Portable Power Distribution Technician roles.

Source:  
https://etcp.esta.org/certify/certify_electrical.html

OSHA Subpart S remains a key federal general-industry electrical-safety reference.

Source:  
https://www.osha.gov/etools/subpart-s

**Electrics architecture conclusion:** general interface awareness and systems education can be deep, but energized/qualified work remains externally controlled.

# 9. Implications for future learning architecture

The research strongly argues against one universal department template such as:

> Course 1 → Course 2 → Course 3 → Lead

because role structures differ.

A later evidence-supported learning system may need to represent:

- transferable support competencies;
- systems understanding;
- operator/specialist branches;
- shop/prep branches;
- leadership branches;
- design/engineering branches;
- external credentials/appointments/licenses.

The final UX remains deferred until the full content and learner/customer evidence are synthesized.
