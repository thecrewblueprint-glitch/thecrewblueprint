# SFS-U03 — Cable Deployment and Gathering Under Direction

**Prepared:** 2026-08-31  
**Status:** Source packet complete enough for bounded course drafting; department/practitioner review remains required  
**Domain:** `D-FLD` Stagehand Field Skills  
**Recommended tier:** `T2` Field Skill  
**Authority:** released cable handling and route support only; no independent patching, energization, testing, repair, configuration, or system ownership

## 1. Research question

What can The Crew Blueprint responsibly teach a general stagehand about laying, gathering, staging, and protecting released production cable while preserving department ownership, cable-specific handling limits, public/work-route safety, and electrical/technical boundaries?

## 2. Executive finding

This belongs in the universal Stagehand Core catalog, but only as **released-cable material handling under direction**.

The transferable learner outcome is:

> **Confirm ownership and release state before touching a cable, confirm the approved origin/destination/route, handle the cable without obvious crushing, sharp bending, connector abuse, or trip-path creation, stop when the cable or route needs a technical decision, and hand the line back to the responsible department in the directed state.**

The course must not imply that a stagehand who can physically lay or gather cable is authorized to disconnect, patch, energize, test, repair, troubleshoot, address, configure, or approve the system.

## 3. Evidence base

### OSHA — clear passageways and cord hazards

OSHA requires walking/working locations and passageways to be kept clear of cords where they create hazards and more generally requires clear, unobstructed aisles/passageways in material-handling environments.

Sources:

- OSHA 1926.416 — General Requirements  
  https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.416
- OSHA 1910.176 — Handling Materials, General  
  https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.176

### OSHA — flexible cord damage and handling

OSHA flexible-cord guidance identifies crushing, pinching, abrasion, sharp edges, and improper strain as damage risks. OSHA also requires portable cord-connected equipment and flexible cords to be handled in ways that do not cause damage and provides visual-inspection requirements in applicable contexts.

Sources:

- OSHA — Flexible Cords  
  https://www.osha.gov/electrical/hazards/flexible-cords
- OSHA 1910.334 — Use of Equipment  
  https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.334
- OSHA 1926.405 — Wiring Methods  
  https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.405

### Public/audience-route protection

OSHA requirements for temporary/amusement-like installations include protection of cable/connectors from public traffic and arrangement of cable protection so it does not itself create a trip hazard. Crew Blueprint should apply this as an authority principle: the approved public-route/cable-protection plan controls; a general hand does not improvise the route.

Source: OSHA 1910.306  
https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.306

### Manufacturer-specific cable limits

Cable families are not interchangeable. Belden publishes cable-specific minimum bend-radius and maximum-load constraints, and Neutrik specifically warns that opticalCON fiber assemblies must respect minimum coiling diameter because excessive bending can reduce cable life/performance.

Sources:

- Belden — Fiber Cable Installation Guide  
  https://catalog.belden.com/prodimgs/AllAssets-approved/std.lang.all/20/09/Fiber_Cable_Installation_Guide_202009.pdf
- Neutrik — opticalCON FAQ  
  https://www.neutrik.com/en/neutrik/faq/opticalcon

### Existing Crew Blueprint evidence

Packages 15/18 establish cable-running support as real stagehand work under direction. Package 46 establishes over-under coiling as a common but cable-dependent method, with technician/manufacturer exceptions. Department Support courses already preserve the boundary between physical cable support and technical system ownership.

## 4. Curriculum boundary

### May teach

- assignment/release confirmation;
- cable-family awareness at a broad level;
- origin/destination/route confirmation;
- controlled physical handling of released cable;
- protecting cable from obvious wheels, doors, sharp edges, pinch/crush points and unsuitable path conditions;
- maintaining clear work/public paths under the approved plan;
- staged/gathered handoff;
- damage/condition reporting;
- stop-and-escalate decisions.

### Do not teach as general Stagehand Core authority

- disconnecting a line because it appears unused;
- energized-circuit work;
- feeder/power distribution procedure;
- continuity/electrical testing;
- fiber cleaning/termination/repair;
- connector repair;
- audio/video/network patch ownership;
- DMX/network addressing/configuration;
- signal troubleshooting;
- deciding an approved public route;
- overriding manufacturer bend/load/handling limits.

## 5. Before touching the line

The course should teach the learner to confirm:

1. **Ownership** — which department/person owns the line.
2. **Release state** — the responsible technician/lead has released it for the assigned movement or strike task.
3. **Task** — lay, gather, stage, coil, move, protect, or hand off.
4. **Origin/destination** — where the assigned line starts and where the lead wants it to end/go.
5. **Route** — the route has been selected/approved by the responsible authority.
6. **Cable-specific exception** — whether this cable uses a reel, basket, figure-eight, minimum bend radius, special connector protection, or another handling method.

If any of those are unclear, the correct action is to ask before moving the line.

## 6. Transferable handling principles

A bounded learner course may teach:

- Keep the cable controlled rather than dragging or throwing connectors/assemblies into place.
- Protect connector faces and strain-relief areas from unnecessary impact or load.
- Avoid obvious crushing by cases/carts/doors and avoid sharp edges or pinch points.
- Do not force a cable into a tighter turn/coil when it resists or when a cable-specific bend limit may apply.
- Keep loose cable from creating uncontrolled loops or trip hazards in active work paths.
- Gather the line in the sequence/direction requested by the cable owner so strike does not pull against still-connected/show-critical equipment.
- Stop if the cable appears connected, energized/show-critical, damaged, unusually stiff/resistant, trapped, or routed through an area whose control is unclear.

These are general handling principles. The department/manufacturer method controls the actual cable.

## 7. Route and public-area boundaries

A stagehand may be assigned to place or support cable on an approved route, but the course should distinguish three roles:

- **Route decision owner** — venue/production/department/public-safety or other responsible authority determines where the line may run.
- **Stagehand support** — physically lays/gathers/stages the released line as directed.
- **Protection/acceptance owner** — designated authority determines cable protection, accessibility/traffic treatment, and whether the route is acceptable.

This keeps SFS-U03 separate from the existing Cable Ramps & Protectors skill, which begins only after the route/product/traffic/access plan is controlled.

## 8. Damage and stop conditions

The learner should stop and report when seeing:

- cut, crushed, flattened, severely kinked, abraded, wet/contaminated, or otherwise visibly damaged cable/connector conditions;
- deformed/missing connector parts;
- a cable trapped under equipment or pinched by a door/edge;
- a line that remains connected or whose release status is unclear;
- a cable requiring a tighter bend than seems natural or known cable-specific limits allow;
- unexpected resistance while gathering;
- public/crew traffic entering the route without the expected protection/control;
- a route change that has not been approved;
- a request to test, repair, reterminate, clean, energize, configure, repatch, or troubleshoot beyond the learner's assignment.

## 9. What good observed practice looks like

A controlled practice can record whether the learner:

- identifies the line owner and assignment;
- confirms the line is released before movement;
- identifies origin/destination/approved route;
- keeps connectors/line physically controlled;
- avoids obvious crush/pinch/sharp-bend hazards;
- keeps the work path organized while laying/gathering;
- stops correctly at a changed condition;
- hands the cable off/stages/coils it according to department direction;
- reports visible damage instead of attempting repair.

## 10. Practice record

Record:

- `course_id`: `C-FLD-CABLE-DEPLOY-GATHER`
- cable family/type if known;
- department owner;
- route/environment;
- assigned task;
- observer and role;
- any manufacturer/employer handling exception;
- result: pass / rework / stop;
- comments;
- date.

Observed practice does not authorize connection, testing, repair, troubleshooting, or configuration.

## 11. Visual requirements

A learner-friendly build should include reviewed visuals for:

1. assignment/release decision flow;
2. broad cable-family examples with an explicit “methods vary” label;
3. connector/strain-relief protection concepts;
4. obvious crush/pinch/sharp-edge hazards;
5. clear versus obstructed/trip-path route examples;
6. approved route → cable deployment → protection/acceptance handoff;
7. stop-condition decision diagram;
8. good staged handoff versus tangled/uncontrolled cable.

Do not use one generic cable illustration to imply that fiber, feeder, multicore, audio, video, data and control lines share identical handling limits.

## 12. Matrix handoff

Suggested high-priority claims:

- `CL-FLD-CABLE-DEPLOY-0001` — release/ownership is confirmed before the line is moved.
- `CL-FLD-CABLE-DEPLOY-0002` — cords/cables should not create walking/working hazards.
- `CL-FLD-CABLE-DEPLOY-0003` — flexible cable/cord should be protected from crushing, pinching, abrasion and other obvious physical damage.
- `CL-FLD-CABLE-DEPLOY-0004` — cable-family/manufacturer bend and handling limits override generic technique.
- `CL-FLD-CABLE-DEPLOY-0005` — public-route/cable protection decisions belong to the responsible plan/authority.
- `CL-FLD-CABLE-DEPLOY-0006` — physical cable handling does not grant technical/electrical system authority.

## 13. Curriculum recommendation

**Build as a Core Field Skill.**

Place it after cable-coiling basics and before Cable Ramps & Protectors.

The course should emphasize **ownership + release + route + physical protection + handoff + stop conditions** rather than teaching technical connection or system procedure.
