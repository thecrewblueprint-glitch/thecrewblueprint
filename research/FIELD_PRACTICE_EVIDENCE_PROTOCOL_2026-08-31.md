# Field-Practice Evidence Protocol

**Prepared:** 2026-08-31  
**Status:** Active research-method extension  
**Purpose:** Add real-world stagehand, warehouse, touring, venue and production-company practice to the Crew Blueprint evidence program without confusing common practice with law, manufacturer procedure, certification or employer authority.

## Why this layer exists

Regulations, standards, manufacturer manuals and formal training sources are strongest for safety boundaries, legal duties, technical facts and model-specific procedure. They are often weak at describing the ordinary rhythm of a call: how new hands are assigned, what leads expect after a task is complete, where standby happens, how cases are staged, how shops prep/deprep gear, how departments protect presets, and what behaviors actually build or damage trust.

Crew Blueprint will therefore maintain a separate **field-practice evidence layer**.

Field-practice evidence may shape learner-facing descriptions of common work patterns. It never overrides a controlling regulation, manufacturer instruction, employer/venue procedure, collective-bargaining requirement, qualified-role boundary or specialist authority gate.

## Evidence classes

### FP-1 — Identified practitioner primary

Examples:
- public writing/interviews by identifiable touring technicians, stagehands, production managers, department heads or rental-house staff;
- practitioner-authored training articles or books;
- public talks, podcasts or videos where the speaker's role/experience can be reasonably verified.

Matrix treatment: normally `practitioner`, authority `supporting` or `contextual`.

### FP-2 — Employer / union operational material

Examples:
- public crew handbooks;
- union-local primers and first-day guidance;
- job descriptions that describe actual call duties;
- production-company onboarding or warehouse expectations.

Matrix treatment: `employer` or `union_local`; applicable to the named context unless corroborated more broadly.

### FP-3 — Community pattern

Examples:
- Reddit discussions in stagehand/live-production communities;
- public technical-theatre/live-sound forums;
- other openly accessible industry discussion boards.

Matrix treatment: `public_unverified` unless an author can be independently identified as a practitioner. One post is anecdote. Several independent discussions can establish a **cross-source pattern**, not a universal rule.

### FP-4 — Public practitioner education / trade secondary

Examples:
- practitioner-oriented training sites;
- trade publications;
- career/operations articles written for working production crews.

Matrix treatment: `trade_secondary` or `practitioner` depending authorship and evidence quality.

### FP-5 — Owner / collaborator field record

Examples:
- owner-confirmed work observations;
- supplied call notes;
- uploaded work-chat excerpts;
- practitioner feedback collected during review.

These must retain provenance, date/context and privacy boundaries. Private chats are used only when legitimately supplied or available through an authorized connector. Crew Blueprint does not attempt to enter private groups or obtain restricted conversations.

## Triangulation rule

A field-practice claim should normally reach **corroborated operational norm** only when:

1. at least three independent practitioner/community observations support the same behavior-level proposition;
2. evidence comes from at least two distinct communities, organizations, employers, practitioner sources or time periods when feasible;
3. the claim is expressed at the common behavioral level rather than copying one crew's exact wording;
4. no controlling legal/manufacturer/employer source contradicts it; and
5. any known venue, department, union, employer or sector variation remains visible.

If only one or two sources support it, retain `practitioner_convention`, `public_unverified`, `context_only`, or `needs_practitioner` rather than upgrading it to a broad norm.

## What this layer is especially good for

- arrival/check-in and finding the responsible lead;
- assignment ownership and reporting completion;
- staying available without obstructing active work;
- legitimate standby/downtime during a call;
- communication habits and correction etiquette;
- department boundaries and preset/ownership sensitivity;
- case labels, destinations, parking, boneyards and workboxes;
- warehouse prep/deprep, inventory, testing, organization and restock patterns;
- shop-to-show career progression;
- call rhythm, changeovers and load-out coordination;
- expectations that distinguish useful new hands from disruptive self-assignment;
- common terminology and sector differences.

## What this layer cannot establish by itself

Community or practitioner evidence cannot independently establish:

- a legal duty or regulatory threshold;
- a universal PPE rule;
- a universal safe load/weight/crew-size rule;
- structural capacity or acceptance;
- electrical qualification or live-power authority;
- rigging qualification or overhead-work authority;
- powered-equipment qualification;
- manufacturer-specific assembly/configuration procedure;
- accessibility, egress, fire-code or AHJ acceptance;
- certification, licensure or employer authorization.

For those topics, field evidence may reveal what needs verification, but the final authority edge must come from the appropriate controlling source and/or qualified reviewer.

## Conflict handling

When field practice conflicts with formal guidance:

- preserve both sources in the matrix;
- mark the conflict explicitly;
- identify whether the disagreement is cultural, sector-specific, outdated, model-specific or unsafe;
- do not teach the community practice as transferable procedure until resolved.

When practitioners disagree with each other, record the variation rather than forcing consensus.

## Language rule

Prefer wording such as:

- "A common pattern across crews is..."
- "Many production crews expect..."
- "In several stagehand and touring-practitioner discussions..."
- "Local practice varies; confirm the current crew's method."

Avoid converting field convention into language such as "always," "never," "industry standard," or "OSHA requires" unless the stronger evidence actually supports that statement.

## Public-community research workflow

For each unresolved field-practice question:

1. search public stagehand, live-sound, technical-theatre, touring and rental-shop communities;
2. search practitioner-authored articles/interviews/training material;
3. search union/employer/job-posting material for the same work pattern;
4. extract the shared behavior-level proposition;
5. record contradictions and local variants;
6. verify safety/authority implications against official or manufacturer sources;
7. create matrix SOURCE + SUPPORT_EDGE records;
8. route unresolved items to practitioner review instead of owner-only correction.

## Research target order

Priority field-practice backfill:

1. Stagehand Fundamentals operational behaviors;
2. Field Skills call-cycle and warehouse/shop practices;
3. Department Support C1 expectations;
4. Department Systems C2 workflow conventions;
5. Lead/supervisor coordination behavior;
6. sector-specific variation: arena, theatre, festival, corporate, convention, warehouse/rental shop, touring;
7. career progression and how workers actually gain responsibility.

## Safety boundary

General learner routes may describe hazards, role boundaries, recognition, stop/escalate behavior and how specialist work interfaces with the broader crew. They must not turn public forum anecdotes into operational instruction for specialist or hazardous work.

## Outcome

The desired evidence stack for a mature learner claim is:

**field reality → cross-practitioner pattern → union/employer context → official/manufacturer verification where applicable → claim-level matrix edge → practitioner review when needed.**

This should reduce the amount of owner correction required later while still preserving the owner's field experience as valuable practitioner evidence rather than the sole source of industry reality.
