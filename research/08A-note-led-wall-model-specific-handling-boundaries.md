# Companion Note 08A — LED-Wall Model-Specific Handling Boundaries

**Date:** 2026-08-15  
**References:** `08-ground-hand-video-wall-support.md`

## Purpose

This note preserves the most important curriculum correction from Research Package 08:

> **Handles, locks, connectors, and storage orientation are variable implementation details; protecting the equipment, following the current system's procedure, and staying inside the worker's authorization are the transferable competencies.**

## Why this matters

A beginner course can accidentally create false confidence if it turns one manufacturer's procedure into a universal LED-wall rule.

Research Package 08 found concrete examples of variation:

- ROE documents designated handles for safe handling rather than a generic instruction to grab any cabinet edge.
- Absen documentation warns against touching the LED surface and uses product-specific transport/storage procedures.
- ROE and Absen product families use different locking mechanisms and verification features.
- Some systems use etherCON-style data connections; others may use different connector families.
- ROE Ruby transport documentation specifies one case orientation, while Absen NT documentation specifies a different panel orientation inside its own flight case.

These are not contradictions. They demonstrate that **the local LED lead and the current product procedure govern the implementation details**.

## Recommended teaching structure

Teach LED support in two layers.

### Layer 1 — Transferable ground-hand competencies

The worker should be able to:

- identify and use intended handles/grip points;
- avoid contact or pressure on the LED face;
- keep control of cabinet weight;
- approach alignment points deliberately;
- avoid forcing locks or connectors;
- support a cabinet before releasing locks;
- protect connector bodies and provide reasonable strain relief under direction;
- place cabinets into the correct purpose-built case, dolly, rack, slot, or protective cover;
- report visible damage or abnormal resistance;
- stop and ask when the current system does not match what they have seen before.

### Layer 2 — Product-specific procedure

The LED/video lead should demonstrate or identify:

- where that cabinet is meant to be carried;
- how its locks work;
- whether it is flat or curved and how that setting is established;
- what connector family is being used;
- the correct power/data routing for that system;
- how panels are removed during strike;
- which flight case/dolly/rack receives which cabinet;
- the intended storage orientation;
- whether local/general hands are permitted to perform the mechanical connection step at all.

## What not to teach as universal rules

Avoid statements such as:

- “Always carry LED panels by the edges.”
- “All LED tiles lock with the same top/bottom sequence.”
- “Green means locked on every wall.”
- “All panels should be stored flat.”
- “All panels should be stored upright.”
- “If the connector fits, it is the right one.”
- “A stagehand who can assemble cabinets is qualified to troubleshoot the wall.”

## Competency boundary

A worker can become competent at **ground-hand LED support** without becoming an LED/video technician.

The hand-level competency is:

**safe handling + repeatable mechanical support + equipment protection + disciplined escalation**

The technician-level competency adds:

**system design/configuration + processor/control operation + signal/power troubleshooting + calibration + repair + advanced fault finding**

This distinction should be preserved when NotebookLM and Claude synthesize the broader department curriculum.

## Curriculum shorthand

**Do not memorize the wall. Learn how to approach any wall safely, then follow the procedure for the wall in front of you.**
