**Created 2026-08-16.** Companion brief to the 5 Deep Research prompts
below. Not itself a research package — a handoff note explaining what

## Why this exists

Stagehand Fundamentals (the live 10-module course) was built from
`14-stagehand-fundamentals-original-source.md` and
`14A-stagehand-fundamentals-module-1-source.json.md` — AI-drafted
curriculum content with **no external citation trail** (confirmed:
zero URLs, zero source list in either document). The owner's direct
statement: *"stagehands fundamentals was not written on my knowledge
alone. it was researched and created by ai and there ARE sources for
the writing, I just don't have access to those sources, so you or gpt
are going to have to create new research packets with source materials
to add into the repository. IF those packets surface new things to add
into the stagehands fundamentals then that is fine, we will deal with
it then. but we should straighten out the research and sources before
we move further away."*

This matches the exact pattern already used successfully for the five
Course 1 pathway pages and both Course 2 courses: real ChatGPT Deep
Research, saved into this repo, with real citations, that a course's
"Sources" section can point to.

**Note:** `04-safety-training-timing-crowd-weather-electrical-risk-
assessment.md` already has 40 real citations, but it's scoped narrowly
to *when* safety topics enter a worker's development (crowd/weather/
electrical/predictive-hazard sequencing) — it doesn't cover PPE
specifics, load-in/out mechanics, tools/gear handling, or crew-role
structure. It's useful supporting context for Prompt 2 below, not a
substitute for it.

## Prompt 1 — The stagehand role, crew structure, and work environments

```
Research the entry-level stagehand role in U.S. live event production —
concerts, corporate events, theater, convention centers, and outdoor
festivals/stadiums.

Find real, citable sources (job postings, union local pages, industry
associations, employer career pages) that support:

1. What an entry-level/general stagehand actually does day to day —
   unloading trucks, pushing road cases, setting staging, running cable
   under direction, moving barricade, sorting gear by department,
   resetting venues after events.
2. The difference between load-in, show call, and load-out as distinct
   phases of a live event, and why new stagehands are more commonly
   assigned to load-in/load-out than show call.
3. How work differs across common environments: arenas, theaters,
   convention centers, and outdoor shows/festivals — what makes each
   one's pace, hazards, and crew expectations different.
4. Crew structure and chain of command on a live event call — who
   riggers, audio/lighting/video crews, carpenters/staging crews, truck
   loaders, and department leads are, and why a new stagehand should
   take direction only from their assigned lead.
5. What employers and crew leads actually say makes a new stagehand
   valuable — reliability, punctuality, communication, safety
   commitment, coachability — ideally from real job postings or
   industry commentary, not generic career advice.
6. Real workplace communication/etiquette norms for stagehands on a
   call — how to ask questions, report problems, take correction, and
   check in with a lead.

For each finding, cite the actual source (job posting URL, union local
page, association page, or similar). Give me a full source list with
working links at the end.
```

## Prompt 2 — Safety mindset, worker rights, and PPE standards

```
Research entry-level workplace safety training and PPE standards
relevant to U.S. stagehands / live event laborers.

Find real, citable sources (OSHA regulations, ANSI/ISEA standards,
industry safety guidance) that support:

1. OSHA's general framework for worker rights and employer
   responsibilities around workplace safety, hazard communication, and
   the right to a safe workplace — with the actual regulation numbers
   and URLs.
2. The distinction OSHA draws between a hazard, a risk, and unsafe
   behavior, and how "stop work" / speaking up about hazards is
   protected.
3. Required and commonly-used PPE for stagehand/live-event labor work:
   hard hats (ANSI Z89.1), high-visibility clothing (ANSI/ISEA 107),
   work gloves, steel-toe or safety-toe boots, eye protection, and
   hearing protection — the actual standards and OSHA PPE requirements
   (29 CFR 1910.132 and related), not just general advice.
4. What "show blacks" (all-black stagehand work attire) actually is and
   why it's standard in the industry, if there's a real citable source
   for this beyond general knowledge.
5. Real guidance on personal readiness for physical labor calls —
   hydration, food, sleep, and preparation — from OSHA heat/hydration
   guidance or similar official sources where it exists.

For each finding, cite the actual source with a working URL. Give me a
full source list at the end.
```
