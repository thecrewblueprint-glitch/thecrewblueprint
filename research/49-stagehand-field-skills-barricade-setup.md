# Research Package 49 — Stagehand Field Skills: Barricade Setup

**RQ item:** RQ-SFS-04  
**Research status:** Source packet complete for model-specific teaching; barricade/venue-lead review required  
**Prepared:** 2026-08-30  
**Use:** Evidence package for supervised physical assistance. It does not authorize crowd-layout, capacity, emergency-access, engineering, or final inspection decisions.

## Decision question

How should Crew Blueprint teach a stagehand to help deploy barricade while preventing the learner from generalizing one product's connector, weight, bracing, gate, corner, or setup sequence to every barricade system?

## Bottom-line finding

“Barricade” is not one interchangeable object. Event work may use folding pedestrian/bicycle barricades, interlocking steel crowd-control panels, heavy concert barricade with audience-side floor plates, corner/wedge pieces, gates, step extenders, temporary fence, or other systems. Each family can have different lift points, connectors, feet, braces, pins, carts, orientations, and load assumptions.

The StageRight CC-500 manual provides a strong model-specific example. It identifies pinch-point and fastener hazards, requires setup on a level surface, lists the barrier at 215 lb, and gives a four-step deployment/connection sequence. That procedure can support an authentic lesson only if it is labeled **CC-500-specific**. Another product requires its own manual and review.

The designated barricade or venue lead retains control of line, orientation, gates, corners, wedges, bracing, emergency/public routes, gaps, final placement, and acceptance.

## Equipment-family distinction

Before teaching a physical sequence, the lesson should require the learner to identify which family is present.

| Family | Recognition only | Why the procedure cannot be generalized |
|---|---|---|
| Folding pedestrian/bicycle barricade | Light/medium panel with folding feet or hooks | Feet, hook direction, stability, and approved use vary |
| Interlocking steel crowd-control panel | Rigid panel with panel-to-panel hook/loop or pin system | Connector direction and ground support vary |
| Concert/front-of-stage barricade | Heavy unit with crowd face, working/security side, and floor plate | Orientation, lock bars, pins, corners, gates, and step systems are product-specific |
| Temporary fence/panel | Taller fence panel, bases, clamps, braces, privacy/wind loading concerns | Base, brace, wind, gate, and site rules are separate |
| Vehicle/security barrier | Rated system intended to resist vehicles or define security perimeter | Requires specialized design/installation; outside beginner course |

The label “bike rack” or “concert barricade” is not enough to select a procedure.

## StageRight CC-500 source findings

### 1. Safety rules

The CC-500 product information packet instructs users to:

- read and understand all instructions;
- stay clear of pinch points;
- make sure fasteners are properly engaged, including faspins, button stops, and jam nuts;
- set up barricades on a level surface;
- understand warning decals and avoid covering them.

Source: https://performance.stageright.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2015/09/pip-cc500-crowd-control-barricade-20161209.pdf

The manual lists the CC-500 barrier at 215 lb. It does not justify a one-person lift. Crew Blueprint should require the employer/lead's assessed team or mechanical handling method and should never invent a universal number of people from the weight alone.

### 2. Model-specific deployment and connection

The CC-500 manual's sequence is:

1. lift the barrier;
2. slide the upright barrier forward until it stops;
3. lower the step until it rests horizontally and locks;
4. to join barriers, pull the faspins, slide units together, push lock bars through the holes, and reinsert the faspins.

The same packet has separate instructions for an optional step extension and directs users to check engaged fasteners and replace damaged equipment with genuine parts.

Source: https://performance.stageright.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2015/09/pip-cc500-crowd-control-barricade-20161209.pdf

This sequence must not be reused for a Sentinel, Mojo, bicycle barricade, temporary fence, or any other model without its own documentation.

### 3. Product role does not establish event layout

StageRight markets the CC-500 as a crowd-control/stage barricade product, but a manufacturer product page is not an event-specific crowd plan.

Source: https://performance.stageright.com/products/accessories/crowd-control-barricades/steel-barricade/

The event's responsible personnel must establish the line, crowd-facing side, security/egress spaces, gates, accessible route, emergency access, corners, and any response to slope or ground conditions.

## Appropriate learner outcome

After model-specific instruction and supervised practice, the learner should be able to:

- identify the barricade family and exact model when marked;
- find and review the applicable manufacturer procedure;
- inspect panels, feet/steps, pins, locks, connectors, fasteners, welds, warning labels, and transport equipment for visible problems;
- use assigned team-lift or mechanical-handling methods and approved hand positions;
- orient and deploy a unit exactly as directed;
- connect units using the correct product-specific hardware;
- confirm visible locks/pins/fasteners are engaged;
- maintain the lead's line and stop rather than improvising around an obstacle;
- protect public, crew, and emergency routes during setup/strike;
- recognize that layout and final acceptance belong to the designated lead.

## Recommended supervised sequence

### 1. Briefing before unload

The lead identifies:

- barricade model and applicable manual;
- unloading method and crew positions;
- designated crowd/public side;
- start point, direction, line, corners, gates, and special pieces;
- exclusion area while heavy units open or lower;
- stop command and final inspector.

### 2. Inspect the transport and first unit

Before moving a panel, check:

- cart/transport restraint;
- obvious bent frame, cracked weld, heavy corrosion, missing piece, damaged foot/step, or unreadable warning;
- missing/bent pin, lock bar, clamp, connector, button stop, jam nut, or other model-specific fastener;
- contaminated, wet, icy, soft, sloped, or uneven setup surface;
- people or equipment in the opening/lowering path.

Report rather than repairing or substituting hardware.

### 3. Move with assigned handling method

Use the number of people or device assigned by the lead. Confirm lift points and commands. Keep hands away from hinges, closing gaps, floor-plate edges, connector interfaces, and places another unit can trap them. Maintain a clear path and set the unit down on command.

### 4. Orient before deployment

Confirm the public/crowd face and working side. Do not open a heavy unit backward and then rotate it through people or cables. Align to the lead's line before connection.

### 5. Deploy the exact model

For a CC-500 training example, follow the four manufacturer steps and show the faspin/lock-bar engagement close up. For another model, replace the sequence with that model's reviewed instructions.

### 6. Connect without improvising

Bring units together on the lead's command. Use only the specified connectors and genuine/approved parts. Do not substitute bolts, rope, cable ties, tape, clamps, or mismatched pins. Keep fingers out of the joint as units close.

### 7. Maintain the designed line

Stop when the line reaches a slope, curb, drain, doorway, cable route, accessible path, fire/emergency route, structure, or gap not covered in the plan. The lead changes the design; the stagehand does not bend the line around the conflict.

### 8. Final check and controlled strike

The lead checks orientation, engaged hardware, line, gates/corners, surface, gaps, and routes. During strike, reverse the model-specific procedure under one person's command and control heavy parts as locks release.

## Common errors

| Error | Consequence | Correct response |
|---|---|---|
| Calling every system “bike rack” and using one connection method | Mismatched or unsecured system | Identify model/manual before setup |
| Hands remain in a hinge or panel joint | Pinch/crush injury | Use reviewed hand zones and coordinated commands |
| Unit is placed on an unapproved slope/uneven surface | Instability or misalignment | Stop and return decision to lead |
| Lock bar or pin looks close enough but is not fully engaged | Connection can separate | Confirm visible engagement and request inspection |
| Missing hardware is replaced with whatever fits | Unknown strength/function | Quarantine unit or obtain approved part |
| Crew improvises a gate or gap | Can compromise crowd and emergency plan | Designated lead controls change |
| Heavy unit is moved without a handling plan | Strain, crush, or dropped-equipment risk | Use assigned crew/device and commands |

## Stop-and-ask conditions

- Model or manual is unknown.
- A unit, fastener, connector, foot, step, weld, or transport cart is damaged.
- Required hardware or special corner/gate/wedge piece is missing.
- Ground is not level/stable enough for the specified model.
- The planned line conflicts with a cable route, door, exit, accessible route, fire lane, structure, or public flow.
- Crowd/public access begins before final inspection.
- Wind loading, temporary fencing, vehicle barriers, or structural anchoring enters the task.
- The lead is not present for a layout change.
- Crew cannot move the unit with the assigned handling method.

## Assessment and practical verification

Knowledge scenarios should test:

- wrong model/manual;
- missing fastener;
- uneven ground;
- a route conflict;
- a request to substitute hardware;
- a gate or corner not in the plan;
- public entry before inspection.

Practical observation must identify the exact model and include handling, orientation, deployment, connection, visible fastener check, and stop/ask behavior. One model signoff is not signoff for every barricade family.

## Authority boundary

The designated barricade, crowd, security, event-operations, or venue lead controls layout, crowd-facing orientation, gates, corners, wedges, bracing, public and emergency access, surface acceptance, sequence, and final inspection. The learner performs assigned model-specific physical work and reports conditions.

## Review required before curriculum handoff

- Barricade lead experienced with the exact system.
- Venue/event-operations review for routes and public-interface language.
- Manufacturer/manual match for every model shown.
- Manual-handling review for the actual unit and transport method.
- Owner and novice-learner review.
