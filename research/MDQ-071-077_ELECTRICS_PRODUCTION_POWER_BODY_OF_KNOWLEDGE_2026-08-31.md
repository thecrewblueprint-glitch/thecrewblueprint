# MDQ-071–077 — Electrics / Production Power Body of Knowledge and Authority Boundaries

**Prepared:** 2026-08-31  
**Status:** Source-grounded role/body-of-knowledge packet; conceptual, recognition, documentation and qualification architecture only  
**Domain:** `D-ELC` Electrics / Production Power  
**Depends on:** MDQ-000–003, MDQ-070 role architecture, current Production Power Awareness and Lead/Advanced evidence, ETCP/ESTA/OSHA source families  
**Purpose:** Map the knowledge universe for entertainment electrical work without creating a self-study path to energized electrical work, temporary-power connection/testing, generator operation, fault work or other qualified-person responsibilities.

---

## 1. Controlling boundary

Production power is a safety-critical field.

Crew Blueprint may teach:

- role architecture;
- electrical terminology and conceptual theory;
- system-block / one-line literacy;
- connector and distribution-family recognition;
- hazard recognition;
- qualified vs unqualified-person distinctions;
- documentation, labels and change control;
- regulatory/standards/AHJ relationships;
- career and external-certification pathways;
- stop/escalate behavior.

Crew Blueprint should **not** provide general learners with operational instructions for connecting, energizing, testing, troubleshooting or modifying production-power systems, using electrical test instruments on circuits, configuring generators, or performing work reserved for qualified/authorized personnel.

Current OSHA rules distinguish qualified from unqualified persons for work around exposed energized parts. ETCP's electrical certifications explicitly target experienced entertainment technicians rather than beginners.

# MDQ-071 — Electrical fundamentals by learner authority

## Concept families

A curriculum map may include conceptual understanding of:

- voltage;
- current;
- resistance;
- power;
- AC vs DC;
- single-phase vs three-phase systems;
- grounding and bonding;
- overcurrent protection;
- neutral/conductor concepts;
- power factor / harmonics;
- voltage-drop concept;
- inrush / transient-load awareness;
- leakage-current concept;
- equipment ratings and listings.

## Authority layering

### General stagehand / non-electrical support

Appropriate learning:

- identify electrical hazards and controlled areas;
- distinguish ordinary equipment/cable handling from qualified electrical work;
- protect assigned cable routes without altering electrical systems;
- recognize obvious damage, heat, water exposure, missing labels or abnormal conditions as stop/report signals;
- follow the responsible electrician/power lead.

### Lighting/audio/video/staging technician interface

Appropriate conceptual literacy:

- read equipment power requirements;
- understand why load, connector, protection and source compatibility matter;
- communicate requirements to the responsible power person;
- interpret system documentation at an interface level.

### Qualified electrical / PPDT / Entertainment Electrician pathway

Crew Blueprint may map the deeper body of knowledge, but qualification and practical authority remain external.

## OSHA qualification baseline

OSHA 1910.332 requires job-relevant electrical-safety training for exposed workers and distinguishes qualified persons permitted to work on or near exposed energized parts from unqualified persons.

OSHA 1910.333 states that only qualified persons may work on energized circuit parts/equipment under the covered conditions and that deenergized systems that have not been properly controlled must be treated accordingly.

**State:** `draftable` for conceptual/authority education; operational electrical work remains external.

# MDQ-072 — Temporary distribution system architecture

## Scope

Teach how a production-power system is **organized on paper and across departments**, not how to connect it.

## System-block categories

A conceptual one-line may include categories such as:

- source / venue service / generator / shore-power interface;
- main distribution;
- feeder/distribution segments;
- downstream distribution equipment;
- branch distribution;
- department loads;
- protection devices;
- grounding/bonding architecture;
- monitoring/metering points;
- cable-route/public/weather interfaces.

## Connector/cable taxonomy

Learners may recognize broad families used in entertainment power systems, but exact ratings, compatibility, connection sequence and inspection requirements are product/code/context specific.

The curriculum should teach:

- identification is not authorization;
- labels/rating information matter;
- damaged, modified, wet/contaminated, overheated or unidentified equipment requires escalation;
- adapters and connector families are not assumed interchangeable;
- temporary electrical systems interact with venue/AHJ/manufacturer requirements.

## Current ESTA outdoor standard

**ANSI E1.58-2017 (R2022)** addresses portable stage/studio electrical equipment used outdoors and explicitly frames energized outdoor portable equipment as work supervised by qualified personnel and separated from the public.

This supports a strong public/weather/qualified-person boundary in Crew Blueprint.

**State:** `researched` for architecture/recognition; connection/energization remains external.

# MDQ-073 — Electrical test instruments and verification boundaries

## Scope

The curriculum should explain **why testing exists, what categories of instruments professionals use, and who is authorized**, without teaching a minor or general learner how to probe or test electrical circuits.

## Instrument-category awareness

Professional electrical work may involve categories such as:

- voltage/current/resistance meters;
- clamp-style measurement tools;
- receptacle/system verification devices;
- continuity/insulation test equipment in appropriate professional contexts;
- power-quality monitoring;
- specialized production-power test equipment.

## Controlling boundary

OSHA interpretations state that testing work on electrical circuits/equipment is for qualified persons under the covered rules.

Learner-facing content should therefore focus on:

- recognizing instruments;
- understanding what question a qualified technician is trying to answer;
- respecting controlled work areas;
- not assuming a simple indicator or tester proves an entire system safe;
- documentation/verification as part of qualified-person workflow.

No operational meter-use steps belong in the general course.

**State:** `external_boundary` for practical use; `draftable` for awareness/role literacy.

# MDQ-074 — Generator and mobile-power interface

## Scope

Map the generator/mobile-power ecosystem at role, hazard and documentation level only.

## Concept families

- source capacity and load planning as professional responsibilities;
- generator vs utility/venue-source distinction;
- grounding/bonding configuration as a qualified-person/code issue;
- environmental/public separation;
- fuel/exhaust/noise/heat hazards;
- weather exposure;
- distribution-system integration;
- monitoring and abnormal-condition escalation;
- permits/AHJ/venue/employer responsibilities;
- shutdown/emergency ownership.

## General learner outcome

A stagehand or department technician should understand that a portable generator is not simply a large wall outlet. Source configuration, grounding/bonding, protection, loading and distribution depend on the exact system and controlling authority.

Do not teach generator setup, connection, grounding configuration, starting/operation, or live troubleshooting.

**State:** `draftable` for interface/hazard/role awareness; operation remains external.

# MDQ-075 — Codes, standards, listings, jurisdiction and AHJ matrix

## Core lesson

There is **not one universal national rulebook that resolves every production-power decision**.

Authority can include:

- OSHA workplace rules;
- NFPA 70 / National Electrical Code as adopted by jurisdiction;
- NFPA 70E / employer electrical-safety program where applicable;
- state/local licensing and code adoption;
- fire/building/event requirements;
- Authority Having Jurisdiction (AHJ);
- NRTL/listing/labeling requirements;
- ESTA entertainment standards;
- manufacturer instructions;
- venue/employer policy;
- union/CBA/work-jurisdiction rules;
- ETCP or other credential requirements where adopted by employer/project.

## Learner competency

Teach learners to ask:

1. Which jurisdiction/site/employer controls this work?
2. Who is the responsible qualified person?
3. Which current code/standard/manufacturer document applies?
4. Is this equipment/configuration listed/rated/approved for the intended context?
5. Who has authority to approve a change?

This is authority literacy, not legal advice or electrical authorization.

**State:** `draftable`; exact jurisdictional claims must be separately matrixed and refreshed.

# MDQ-076 — Power quality and modern electronic loads

## Scope

Modern entertainment systems include large numbers of electronic lighting, video, audio, networking and control loads. Curriculum should explain the **problem categories** that qualified power professionals consider without teaching diagnosis on energized systems.

## Concept families

- nonlinear-load concept;
- power-factor concept;
- harmonic-distortion concept;
- neutral-current concern;
- inrush / startup behavior;
- voltage sag / instability;
- leakage-current concept;
- nuisance-tripping symptom;
- UPS/power-conditioning/isolation/surge-protection categories;
- generator/load interaction;
- monitoring and escalation.

## Cross-department value

Lighting/audio/video technicians benefit from knowing that an apparent device fault may originate upstream in power quality or distribution and that the correct response may be to document symptoms and involve the responsible qualified person rather than improvising electrical fixes.

**State:** `draftable` as systems literacy; live diagnosis remains external.

# MDQ-077 — Electrical planning, documentation and leadership

## Documentation families

Electrical/power departments may use:

- one-line diagrams;
- load schedules;
- equipment schedules;
- distribution/cable schedules;
- labels/identification records;
- inspection/checklist records;
- venue/source documentation;
- generator/vendor documentation;
- change/revision records;
- incident/near-miss reports;
- signoff/acceptance records according to employer/AHJ practice.

## Leadership architecture

Head electrician / power lead / EE / PPDT / venue electrician roles may include, depending on context:

- interpreting production requirements;
- coordinating with venue/source/vendor/AHJ;
- assigning appropriately qualified personnel;
- maintaining documentation/change control;
- coordinating lighting/audio/video/rigging/staging load interfaces;
- maintaining public/weather protection requirements;
- determining when work must stop/escalate;
- ensuring inspection/testing/signoff requirements are owned by appropriate qualified personnel;
- incident reporting and closeout.

Crew Blueprint may teach this responsibility architecture without appointing a learner to the role.

**State:** `draftable` for planning/documentation/leadership architecture.

# 9. External certification / career architecture

ETCP currently maintains two related electrical certifications:

## Entertainment Electrician

- broader entertainment-electrical scope;
- eligibility: **30 points**;
- intended for experienced technicians and leadership/supervisory as well as technical roles.

## Portable Power Distribution Technician (PPDT)

- focused specifically on portable power distribution;
- eligibility: **20 points**;
- intended for experienced power-distribution workers age 21+;
- ETCP describes the PPDT body of knowledge as a **subset** of the Entertainment Electrician body of knowledge;
- certification is intended to help employers assess qualified portable-power-technician status.

This confirms that Crew Blueprint should treat EE/PPDT as external professional pathways, not as equivalents of an online course completion state.

A responsible progression map is:

**General Stagehand / Power Awareness**  
→ **Department technician interface literacy**  
→ **Employer/union/venue supervised experience and training**  
→ **Portable-power/electrical technical roles according to local authority**  
→ **External ETCP / licensure / employer qualification pathways where applicable**  
→ **Power lead / head electrician / advanced systems responsibilities**
