# Research Package 15 — Stagehand Fundamentals: Entry-Level Role Validation

**Research date:** 2026-08-16  
**Purpose:** Source-validation packet for the existing *Stagehand Fundamentals* course.  
**Scope:** Entry-level/general stagehand duties, load-in/show-call/load-out phases, work environments, crew structure, employer-valued behaviors, and communication/etiquette norms.

> **Use note for Claude:** This packet exists to backfill real citations for claims already present in the Stagehand Fundamentals course. Treat employer/union terminology as evidence of real practice, but do not convert employer-specific or local-union practices into national standards unless the evidence supports that generalization.

---

## Executive summary

Current U.S. employer, venue, municipal, and IATSE sources strongly support the course's basic description of an entry-level/general stagehand: stagehands unload and reload trucks, move road cases and production equipment, assemble or place staging and risers, assist technical departments with gear and cabling under direction, clean/reset work areas, and participate in load-in and load-out.

The distinction among **load-in, show call, and load-out** is also well-supported. Employers and union materials treat those as distinct phases or call types.

The weakest existing course claim is the blanket statement that **brand-new stagehands are generally more likely to get load-in/load-out than show call**. Available U.S. sources support a more careful formulation: load-in/load-out are common general-labor entry points, while show calls may involve timing-sensitive or production-specific assignments and can be allocated according to production need, local rules, or demonstrated ability. This should be taught as a practical tendency, not a national rule.

The course's emphasis on **reliability, punctuality, communication, safety, coachability, checking in, following the assigned chain of command, and asking rather than guessing** is strongly aligned with current employer and IATSE expectations.

# 2. Load-in, show call, and load-out

## Load-in

Load-in is the pre-event phase in which production equipment is unloaded, moved into position, and assembled. Current stagehand postings repeatedly use `load-in` or equivalent setup language.

**Evidence:**

## Show call

Show call is assigned work during the active performance or event and may include changeovers, backstage movement, set changes, spotlight work when assigned/qualified, or other timing-sensitive production tasks.

Legends Global listings and Live Nation postings distinguish show/changeover work from load-in and load-out.

**Sources:**

## Load-out

## Are beginners more likely to start on load-in/load-out?

### Evidence status: partially supported — do not present as a universal national rule

IATSE Local 101's referral/hiring-hall rules show that show-call assignments can be controlled according to production requirements and worker abilities rather than being automatic.

**Source:**
- IATSE Local 101 — Hiring Hall Referral Work Rules  
  https://iatse101.net/hiring-hall-referral-work-rules/

IATSE Local 193 also treats show calls as direct performance assignments and expects workers to remain with the assigned department until released.

**Source:**
- IATSE Local 193 — Stagehand Expectations  
  https://www.iatselocal193.com/expectations

### Recommended course wording

> **Load-in and load-out are common places for new stagehands to gain experience because they involve substantial general crew labor. Show calls may involve timing-sensitive or production-specific duties, so assignments depend on the venue, production, union/local rules, and the worker's demonstrated abilities.**

This is better supported than stating that all new hands necessarily start on load-in/load-out.

# 3. Differences among common work environments

## Arenas / amphitheaters

Current Live Nation and arena postings show a large concentration of touring equipment, multiple technical departments, road cases, staging, barricade, lighting/audio/video systems, and substantial load-in/load-out labor.

**Sources:**
- Live Nation — Union Stagehand, Virginia Beach Amphitheater  
  https://livenation.wd503.myworkdayjobs.com/en-US/LNExternalSite/job/Union-Stagehand--VB-Amphitheater-_JR-87529
- PPG Paints Arena / Legends Global — Stagehand listing via arena/ASM channels  
  https://www.teamworkonline.com/arenas-facilities-jobs/asm-global-jobs/asm-global-jobs

### Defensible course framing

- high equipment volume;
- many departments operating simultaneously;
- docks, road cases, staging, barricade, and production traffic;
- irregular hours and large crew coordination;
- heightened awareness around moving and overhead production activity.

Do **not** teach "arena = always fast" as a standard. Pace depends on the event and schedule.

## Theaters

Theater stagehand classifications show more frequent interaction with scenery, flats, props, risers, microphones, lighting, fly/stage systems, and permanent venue equipment.

**Sources:**

- St. Bonaventure University — Stage Hand/Manager, Quick Center for the Arts

- more fixed stage systems and scenic/stagecraft interaction;
- tighter backstage spaces are common in many venues;

# 4. Crew structure and chain of command

## IATSE touring structure

IATSE's touring guidance identifies production and local structures including:

- Head Carpenter;
- lighting department heads;
- sound department heads;
- props;
- wardrobe;
- hair/makeup;
- local house heads;
- local crew;
- steward;
- business agent.

**Source:**
- IATSE International — An Introduction to Pink Contract Touring  
  https://iatse.net/about/stagecraft/touring-2/

A critical point for the course: the touring crew is **not automatically the direct supervisor of local labor**. Local house heads/stewards and the established call hierarchy may control the local crew. This supports teaching beginners to follow the chain of command established for the specific call rather than taking instructions from any random person on site.

## IATSE Local 101

Local 101's hiring-hall rules state that the Job Steward/Leadman makes work assignments according to production requirements and worker abilities.

**Source:**
- IATSE Local 101 — Hiring Hall Referral Work Rules  
  https://iatse101.net/hiring-hall-referral-work-rules/

**Riggers:** overhead rigging systems, hoists, truss, hang points, and related work.

**Source:**
- Encore — Rigger / Lead Rigger career listings  
  https://jobs.encoreglobal.com/

**Audio / Lighting / Video:** distinct technical departments with their own leads/heads and responsibilities.

**Source:**
- IATSE International — Touring structure  
  https://iatse.net/about/stagecraft/touring-2/

**Carpentry / staging:** often coordinated through a Head Carpenter, house carpenter, or equivalent stagecraft lead.

**Source:**

## Recommended course wording

Instead of:

> "Only take direction from leads and department heads."

Use:

> **Follow the chain of command established for that call. Depending on the venue, employer, production, or union local, that may include a steward, crew chief/leadman, house head, department head, Stage Manager, Production Manager, or another specifically assigned supervisor. If you're unsure who owns your assignment, ask your lead or steward before acting.**

# Glossary

**Call** — A scheduled work assignment/shift for production labor. IATSE locals commonly use this terminology.

**Load-in** — Pre-event unloading, movement, and assembly/setup of production equipment.

**Show call** — Assigned work during the active performance/event, often involving operational or timing-dependent duties.

**Load-out** — Post-event strike, packing, movement, and reloading/removal of production equipment.

**Local crew** — Venue/market labor assisting a touring production with load-in, show-running, and load-out.

**Department head** — Person responsible for a craft/technical department such as lighting, sound, props, wardrobe, or another show department.

**Steward / leadman** — Union/local labor authority who may coordinate assignments, work rules, and crew issues depending on the agreement/local.

**Changeover** — Transition between performers, acts, or production configurations; commonly a timing-sensitive stage/technical assignment.

# Course claims to revise or qualify

## 1. "New stagehands are very likely to start with load-in/load-out."

**Status:** Practical tendency, not a documented national rule.

**Recommended:**

> Load-in and load-out are common places for new stagehands to gain experience because they involve substantial general crew labor. Show-call assignments can depend on production-specific timing, trust, ability, venue practice, employer rules, and union/local procedures.

## 2. "Theaters are quieter/slower; arenas are fast."

**Status:** Overgeneralized.

**Recommended:**

> Theaters often involve more fixed stage systems, scenic elements, backstage choreography, and production-specific stagecraft, while arenas often involve larger touring systems, higher equipment volume, and larger multi-department crews. Pace varies by production.

## 3. "Only take direction from leads and department heads."

**Status:** Too narrow.

**Recommended:**

> Follow the chain of command established for the call. Depending on the employer, venue, production, or union local, that may involve a steward, crew chief/leadman, house head, department head, Stage Manager, Production Manager, or other assigned supervisor.

## 4. "Copy / Heard / Standing By" as standards

**Status:** Common-language examples, not verified national standards.

**Recommended:** Teach them as examples of concise production communication.

# Bottom line for Stagehand Fundamentals

The existing Module 1 framing is **substantially supported by real U.S. employer and union sources**. The strongest validated claims are that entry-level stagehands provide general production labor; work inside an organized departmental hierarchy; participate in distinct load-in, show, and load-out phases; encounter different workflows and hazards by venue type; and are expected to be reliable, punctual, communicative, safe, coachable, and responsive to the established chain of command.

The largest correction is to avoid presenting **"load-in/load-out first, show call later"** as a universal U.S. rule. It is better taught as a common practical pattern whose actual assignments depend on the production, employer, venue, union local, and worker capability.
