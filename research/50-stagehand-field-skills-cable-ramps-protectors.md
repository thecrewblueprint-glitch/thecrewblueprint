# Research Package 50 — Stagehand Field Skills: Cable Ramps and Protectors

**RQ item:** RQ-SFS-05  
**Research status:** Source packet complete; department/venue/accessibility review required  
**Prepared:** 2026-08-30  
**Use:** Evidence package for supervised setup of cable protectors after the responsible lead has approved the route and product. It does not authorize independent routing, service separation, traffic engineering, accessibility approval, or electrical decisions.

## Decision question

What should a stagehand understand and demonstrate when assigned to deploy cable ramps/protectors, while preserving the department and venue lead's control over cable route, protector selection, capacity, accessibility, and final acceptance?

## Bottom-line finding

A cable protector is part of a planned route and must be selected for the actual cables/hoses, connector system, surface, pedestrians, accessibility needs, and vehicle/equipment traffic. Checkers' manufacturer guide shows that channel size, outside diameter, height/slope, load capacity, connector style, and model family all affect selection. OSHA requires walking-working surfaces to be maintained free of hazards and flexible cords to be protected from accidental damage. If the crossing is part of a required accessible route, applicable accessibility standards and site decisions also matter; a yellow lid or a marketing label alone does not establish that the installed route complies.

The stagehand may inspect, carry, connect, load channels, close lids, align edges, and recheck the installed line under direction. The responsible department/venue lead chooses the route, product, channel assignment, service separation, traffic suitability, and accessibility treatment.

## Product-family distinction

| Family | Typical recognition | Important limit |
|---|---|---|
| Drop-over cord cover | One or more cables are covered from above; may lack a hinged lid | Capacity, retention, profile, and traffic use vary |
| Hinged modular protector | Base/channels with a hinged lid and interlocking ends | Channel size, connector style, axle rating, lid closure, and model compatibility matter |
| Low-profile pedestrian protector | Lower rise for foot/light-cart areas | Low profile does not automatically establish accessible-route compliance |
| Protector with accessibility side ramps | Core protector plus gradual side ramps/rails or an integrated wider system | Entire installed route, transitions, width, slope, surface, and site context require review |
| Heavy-duty vehicle protector | Higher-capacity modular system | Vehicle type, speed, tire contact, clearance, axle/load rating, surface, and installation rules control |
| Hose bridge/tunnel | Vehicle path passes over a protected tunnel/bridge | Distinct system; may require additional ramps and setup procedure |

Do not mix families or connector generations because pieces appear close enough to fit.

## Source findings

### 1. Selection begins with actual line size and traffic

Checkers directs the selector to confirm:

- cable or hose outside diameter fits the channel;
- protector height and slope suit required clearance;
- load capacity matches the vehicle and pedestrian traffic;
- connector style is compatible with existing units;
- low-profile and accessibility accessories are selected where needed.

Its reference guide shows large differences among model channel sizes, connector types, and per-axle load capacities.

Sources:

- https://checkers.justrite.com/buying-guide/cable-protectors
- https://checkers.justrite.com/media/mageplaza/product_attachments/attachment_file/c/h/checkers_cable-protectors-overview__ck414-web_1.pdf

Curriculum implication: never teach “five-channel yellow ramp” as a capacity or compatibility specification. The exact model label/data controls.

### 2. Protecting both the route and the cable is the purpose

Checkers describes modular systems as providing passage for pedestrians, vehicles, and equipment while protecting cables, cords, and hoses. OSHA's electrical standard requires flexible cords and cables to be protected from accidental damage such as sharp corners, projections, doorways, and pinch points.

Sources:

- https://checkers.justrite.com/media/mageplaza/product_attachments/attachment_file/c/h/checkers_cable-protectors-overview__ck414-web_1.pdf
- https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.305
- https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.405

OSHA's general walking-working-surface rule requires surfaces to be maintained free of hazards. A protector that shifts, leaves an exposed edge, creates an unplanned obstruction, or does not suit the crossing may trade one hazard for another.

Source: https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.22

### 3. Accessibility is an installed-route question

The 2010 ADA Standards address floor/ground surfaces, changes in level, accessible routes, and ramps. Among other provisions, changes above specified small thresholds require ramp treatment, ramp runs generally may not be steeper than 1:12, and ramp clear width is generally 36 inches minimum, subject to scope and exceptions.

Source: https://www.ada.gov/law-and-regs/design-standards/2010-stds/

Checkers sells low-profile and ramp accessory systems represented for accessibility use, but manufacturer product labeling does not replace a venue/accessibility determination for the complete installed route.

Sources:

- https://checkers.justrite.com/cable-protectors/ada-cable-protectors
- https://checkers.justrite.com/news/how-to-choose-an-ada-dda-compliant-cable-protector/

The learner should recognize an accessibility concern and call the designated lead, not calculate legal compliance or declare a route “ADA approved.”

## Appropriate learner outcome

After model-specific instruction and supervised practice, the learner should be able to:

- identify the protector family and exact model/compatible pieces;
- confirm the route and orientation with the department/venue lead;
- inspect units, lids, hinges, connectors, channels, ramps, traction surfaces, and warnings;
- compare cable/hose outside diameter with the approved model/channel plan;
- connect units without forcing incompatible connectors;
- place assigned cables/hoses into assigned channels without crushing, sharp bends, crossed connectors, or unauthorized mixing;
- close and check lids or seat a drop-over unit as its instructions require;
- align transitions and remove visible trip/catch points within the approved plan;
- keep the line stable and recheck it after traffic or conditions change;
- stop when the route, capacity, surface, accessibility, cable separation, or model suitability is uncertain.

## Recommended supervised sequence

### 1. Confirm the route plan

Before moving protectors, the lead identifies:

- start/end points and crossing angle;
- protector model and connector generation;
- pedestrian, cart, equipment, and vehicle traffic expected;
- required accessible-route treatment or alternate route;
- channel assignment for each cable/hose/service;
- cable bend, connector, separation, heat, and fill limits;
- surface preparation and any anti-slip/anchoring accessories;
- inspection frequency and who accepts the installation.

The stagehand does not independently reroute a department cable to make the protector line easier.

### 2. Inspect and sort pieces

Check for:

- cracked, cut, crushed, warped, burned, chemically damaged, or heavily worn body;
- broken hinge, lid, latch, connector, ramp, rail, or traction surface;
- debris, liquid, ice, mud, tape buildup, or sharp objects in channels;
- incompatible male/female ends or different connector families;
- unreadable model/capacity information where selection depends on it;
- surface holes, slopes, soft ground, standing water, curbs, door swings, or other conflicts.

Segregate questionable pieces and notify the lead. Do not drill, cut, tape, shim, or modify units unless the manufacturer and responsible authority provide a specific method.

### 3. Stage the line

Carry with the reviewed grip/team method. Place units in the approved orientation without trapping fingers under heavy edges. Keep the work zone controlled so the public or vehicles do not enter before the line is complete.

### 4. Connect units

Join only compatible connectors, working from the lead's start point. Keep the line straight or shaped only with approved pieces. If connectors require excessive force, do not hammer or jump on them; inspect alignment/model and ask.

### 5. Load the assigned channels

The responsible technician identifies which line goes in which channel. Lay cable/hose without forcing it below the channel's maximum outside diameter, violating the specified bend radius, placing a connector where the lid will press it, or changing service separation. Protect connector ends during the work.

### 6. Close or seat the system

For a hinged model, close the lid completely according to its instructions and confirm no cable is pinched. For a drop-over model, confirm the line is fully captured and the protector sits as designed. Install approved side ramps, rails, end pieces, or anti-slip accessories where the plan requires them.

### 7. Inspect the complete crossing

Walk both approaches and look from pedestrian and vehicle directions. Check:

- connected joints;
- closed lids;
- stable base contact;
- exposed cable;
- raised corners or transitions;
- surface movement;
- visibility/warnings;
- door, exit, aisle, and equipment conflicts;
- the designated accessible route or alternate route.

The lead performs final acceptance.

### 8. Recheck and strike

Traffic, temperature, rain, mud, cable movement, and repeated loads can change an installation. Recheck on the interval assigned by the lead and after a vehicle impact or visible movement. During strike, control public/vehicle access, open or remove units model-correctly, protect cables, and return pieces to the designated storage/inspection area.

## Common errors

| Error | Consequence | Correct response |
|---|---|---|
| Selecting by color or channel count alone | Wrong capacity, size, connector, or slope | Verify exact model and plan |
| Mixing connector styles/generations | Gaps or unstable joints | Use only compatible pieces |
| Overfilling a channel or forcing the lid | Cable damage or lid not seated | Stop; lead selects another channel/system |
| Putting connectors under a closing lid | Crush/damage and incomplete closure | Reposition only with technician approval |
| Treating “ADA” marketing as installed-route approval | Route may still fail scope, width, slope, transition, or surface requirements | Venue/accessibility lead reviews complete route |
| Placing ramps on mud, ice, soft, or sharply uneven ground | Movement and trip/vehicle hazard | Stop; surface/control decision belongs to lead |
| Letting traffic enter before final check | Unverified system is loaded | Control crossing until accepted |
| Rerouting cable without department approval | Technical, separation, signal, or safety conflict | Return decision to responsible technician |

## Stop-and-ask conditions

- Route or responsible lead is unclear.
- Exact model, load capacity, channel size, or connector compatibility is unknown.
- Expected traffic changes from pedestrian to cart, forklift, truck, lift, or other vehicle.
- Required accessible route is affected or no approved alternate exists.
- Surface is wet, icy, muddy, soft, sloped, broken, or incompatible with the system.
- Protector or accessory is damaged or will not connect/close normally.
- Cable/hose does not fit freely or would violate a bend/coil limit.
- Cable separation, voltage/service type, heat, or connector placement is uncertain.
- Route blocks an exit, door, aisle, emergency path, drainage, or another department.
- A vehicle strike or line movement occurs after installation.

## Assessment and practical verification

Knowledge scenarios should test:

- model mismatch;
- a cable too large for the channel;
- a connector beneath the lid;
- changed vehicle traffic;
- an affected accessible route;
- wet/uneven ground;
- a protector line that moves after use;
- pressure to reroute a department cable without the technician.

Practical observation should require inspection, compatible connection, assigned channel loading, correct closure/seating, edge/transition check, and escalation of one planted defect.

Record knowledge completion separately from practical observation and any employer/venue authorization.

## Authority boundary

The responsible department or venue lead chooses the route, protector system, traffic rating, channel assignment, cable/service separation, accessibility treatment, surface controls, inspection interval, and final acceptance. The stagehand assembles and checks the assigned system under that plan. Course completion does not establish electrical qualification, traffic-control authority, accessibility compliance authority, or approval for every protector model.
