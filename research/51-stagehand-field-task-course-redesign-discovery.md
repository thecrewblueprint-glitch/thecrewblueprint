# Research Package 51 — Stagehand Field-Task Course Redesign Discovery

**Research date:** 2026-08-30  
**Status:** Owner/practitioner discovery; not approved learner instruction  
**Purpose:** Identify short, visual, task-specific training that should follow Stagehand Fundamentals.

## Owner direction

The five current field-skill builds are too long and devote too much space to compliance framing for single physical tasks. Rebuild them one task at a time. The learner experience should show **how the task is done**, while preserving only the safety and authority boundaries needed to prevent a dangerous misunderstanding.

**Owner clarification — 2026-08-31:** Use a research-first process. Search authoritative, industry-primary, industry-secondary, and public sources before asking the owner to reconstruct ordinary task knowledge. Label public/unverified claims, cross-source patterns, and inference at claim level so the owner can review only the uncertain field-practice points. The task-first redesign will reshape all Field Skills. These physical-task lessons use a concise explain-and-show format with in-field reinforcement rather than the retention model used for the broader awareness curriculum.

Package 52 applies this method to **Move a Road Case With a Partner** and supersedes the interview-first dependency below. Preserve the original questions as a focused owner-review checklist only where research remains variable or unresolved.

## Evidence summary

- IATSE Local 15's entry guide uses pictures, diagrams, equipment recognition, procedures, and practical vocabulary as entry-level preparation. Its procedures include bucket-brigade handoffs, carrying truss and flats, handling soft goods, truss assembly, basic knots, cable coiling, and repeated warning calls.
- IATSE Local 122's stagehand-skills primer centers three concrete demonstrations: over-under coiling, tabbing/taping cable, and truss assembly.
- IATSE Local 193 says the department head or road person controls the coiling method, while over-under is a common default for many flexible production cables.
- UNLV's stagehand duty descriptions include truck loading/unloading, installing and dismantling department equipment and cable, pipe-and-drape installation, general staging, and setup/tear-down work.
- O*NET describes live-event A/V work around setting up, maintaining, and dismantling equipment, wires, and cables. This supports task instruction built around equipment movement and physical setup rather than abstract career-role theory.

## Finding: the immediate post-Fundamentals sequence should change

The current five-course set mixes one broadly transferable core skill with three specialized assignments and one route-protection task:

| Current course | Recommended disposition | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Over-Under Cable Coiling | Keep, but compress | Common, demonstrable, and visually teachable. |
| Ratchet Straps Under Direction | Move to optional/special assignment | Useful on some calls, but not a universal first assignment. |
| Flatbed Cargo-Securement Support | Move to optional/special assignment | Driver/load-lead context is too specialized for the immediate beginner path. |
| Barricade Setup Under Direction | Move to optional/special assignment | Equipment and event context vary; teach when the assignment is likely. |
| Cable Ramps & Protectors | Fold into cable-path handling | A learner needs the complete physical sequence: lay, dress, cross, protect, inspect. |

Do not delete the specialized material. Preserve it as an optional assignment library until practitioner review determines whether each course should be shortened, split into a visual skill card, or replaced.

## Recommended five-course core field sequence

1. **Move a Road Case With a Partner**
   - Inspect wheels, brakes, handles, latches, balance, and route.
   - Establish the pusher/spotter roles and stop command.
   - Show doors, corners, ramps, thresholds, slopes, and parking.
   - Visual requirement: one continuous real-world demonstration plus close-ups of hands, casters, and sightlines.

2. **Work a Truck Line and Sort the Load**
   - Receive, repeat the destination, pass or move the item, and keep the line flowing.
   - Show case labels, department zones, stack order, loose parts, and what happens when the line stops.
   - Visual requirement: overhead route diagram plus a filmed bucket-brigade/case-line sequence.

3. **Coil a Cable Over-Under**
   - Start with a released cable and directed coil size.
   - Show the alternating wrist/hand motion in normal speed and slow motion.
   - Finish with the tie and correct destination.
   - Keep exceptions to one compact "ask first" panel.

4. **Lay, Dress, Tab, and Protect a Cable Path**
   - Show how cable leaves a source, follows the assigned route, stays relaxed, turns corners, crosses traffic, and reaches its destination.
   - Include tabbing/taping and the physical installation of an assigned cable protector.
   - Visual requirement: before/after floor views and a complete route walkthrough.

5. **Carry and Turn a Long Object as a Team**
   - Cover pipes, truss, flats, and similar long loads without claiming one grip works for every object.
   - Demonstrate high/low ends, shared commands, corners, doors, flip/tip/doe-see-doe language, and set-down.
   - Visual requirement: performer-eye and overhead views showing hand positions and travel path.

## Second-wave task candidates

These are common enough to research after the core five, but depend more heavily on venue or department context:

- pipe-and-drape setup and strike;
- folding and storing drops, curtains, and other soft goods;
- modular stage deck/riser handling and assembly under direction;
- fast-fold projection screen setup and strike;
- basic truss connection under a qualified lead using the exact product family;
- bowline and clove-hitch recognition and supervised practice;
- setting and collapsing microphone stands without changing show-critical placement;
- sorting, counting, and returning loose hardware during strike.

## Task-course production standard

Each task course should target **3–6 minutes before practice** and use the same compact sequence:

1. **Watch it once** — a complete, uninterrupted demonstration.
2. **See the hand positions** — 3–6 annotated stills or short close-ups.
3. **Do the task** — no more than 5–7 numbered actions.
4. **Avoid the three common mistakes** — visual wrong/right comparisons.
5. **Stop and ask** — only the few conditions that change the immediate action.
6. **Practice check** — one observable checklist for a lead or peer coach.
7. **Three-question check** — recognition and sequence, not legal trivia.

Legal and compliance context should remain in the course-entry acknowledgment, source drawer, and one short task-specific boundary. It should not interrupt every physical step.

## First owner interview: road-case movement

Before rebuilding the first course, capture the owner's normal jobsite method:

1. When do you require two people instead of one?
2. Who normally pushes, who spots, and what words stop or restart movement?
3. How do you handle doors, dock plates, ramps, thresholds, slopes, and blind corners?
4. When do you push from the swivel-caster end versus the fixed-caster end, if that distinction is used on your calls?
5. How should a case be parked, braked, chocked, staged, or handed off?
6. Which mistakes do you see from first-call stagehands most often?

## Interpretation limits

Local union training materials document real practice in their jurisdictions; they do not create one national procedure. Product instructions, employer rules, venue conditions, department ownership, and the responsible lead continue to control the actual task. The priority sequence and micro-course format above are Crew Blueprint design recommendations derived from the cited evidence and the owner's stated goals.
