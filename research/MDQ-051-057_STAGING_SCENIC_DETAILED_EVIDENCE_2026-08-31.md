## 1. Controlling safety/authority principle

Staging/scenic training must separate **recognition and assigned support** from **structural acceptance, powered machinery operation, rigging, powered-tool authorization and engineering responsibility**.

Crew Blueprint may teach:

- component/system recognition;
- role architecture;
- documentation literacy;
- handling/movement boundaries;
- inspection/reporting of visible problems;
- material/workflow concepts;
- stop/escalate conditions;
- career progression.

It does not authorize a learner to engineer, approve, modify, rig, operate machinery, or sign off temporary structures or stage systems.

# MDQ-051 — Staging/scenic systems and component literacy

## System families

Learners should distinguish:

- portable platforms/decks/risers;
- stairs/rails/ramps/accessories;
- scenery/flats/wagons;
- soft goods/drapery;
- scenic walls/units;
- carts/dollies/storage systems;
- permanent venue stage systems;
- temporary outdoor structures;
- automation/machinery interfaces.

## Portable-stage evidence

Current ESTA evidence includes **ANSI E1.62-2021 (R2026)** for mass-produced portable platforms, ramps, stairs and choral risers.

This is important because the standard is a **product specification**, not a substitute for manufacturer instructions, venue acceptance or engineering of custom stage systems.

## Temporary structures

**ANSI E1.21-2024** establishes requirements for temporary ground-supported structures used in outdoor entertainment production and is referenced by the 2024 International Fire Code / International Building Code context.

General curriculum should cover recognition, boundaries and documentation interfaces only. Structural design/acceptance remains external.

## Curriculum disposition

- Foundation/Field Skill: component recognition and assigned handling.
- Department Support: portable platform/scenic system literacy.
- Department Systems: documentation, system architecture and inspection/reporting concepts.
- External boundary: structural acceptance/engineering and controlled machinery.

**State:** `draftable` for bounded recognition/support content.

# MDQ-052 — Scenic construction materials, documentation and shop literacy

## Scope

Teach what scenic departments make, how information is communicated, and where a stagehand/carpenter/technician/TD role changes.

## Current USITT evidence

USITT BACKstage resources identify scenic-construction knowledge families including:

- terminology;
- drafting/blueprint literacy;
- measurement;
- construction materials;
- hand/power-tool awareness;
- joinery;
- safe practice.

USITT scenic-design guidance also frames scenic work as a collaborative design/technology discipline requiring drawings/models, material knowledge and coordination with lighting, sound and other production areas.

## Learner competency families

### Support

- scenic terminology;
- drawing/label recognition;
- material identification at a broad level;
- assigned movement/staging;
- visible-damage reporting;
- protecting finished surfaces;
- work-area organization.

### Carpenter / scenic technician

Curriculum may map:

- blueprint/drawing interpretation;
- measurement/layout concepts;
- material properties;
- hardware/fastener/tool selection concepts;
- scenic finish/surface awareness;
- quality-control concepts;
- shop/show documentation.

Exact powered-tool or construction procedures require employer/tool-specific training and supervision.

### Technical direction/design

- structural/design documentation;
- material/production planning;
- interdepartment coordination;
- build/transport/install strategy;
- schedule/budget tradeoffs;
- acceptance/signoff responsibilities according to organization.

## Curriculum disposition

- Department Support: terminology/drawings/material handling.
- Department Systems: documentation/material/system reasoning.
- Advanced/TD/Design: planning and integrated responsibility.

**State:** `draftable` for conceptual/support content.

# MDQ-053 — Platform, deck, riser, stair, rail and ramp interfaces

## Scope

Map the learner’s interaction with portable stage systems without giving generic assembly instructions for unknown products.

## Transferable learning

- identify deck/platform/leg/frame/rail/stair/ramp/accessory families;
- read system/manufacturer labels and configuration documents;
- recognize that component compatibility and support conditions are system-specific;
- understand that guardrail, stair, ramp and access requirements can be safety-critical;
- report visible damage, missing hardware or unstable/unknown conditions;
- preserve egress/accessibility routes;
- distinguish assigned component handling from final structural acceptance.

## Current manufacturer evidence

Wenger StageTek and other portable-platform product families illustrate that systems have exact manuals, bracing/accessory requirements, load-related specifications, proprietary components and approved configurations.

These manufacturer sources support **model-specific evidence**, not a universal assembly method.

## Current ESTA evidence

- ANSI E1.62-2021 (R2026): mass-produced portable platform/ramp/stair/riser product specification;
- ANSI E1.46-2018 (R2023): prevention of falls from theatrical stages/raised performance platforms;
- ANSI E1.60-2024: raked-stage safety guidance.

## Boundary

No generic Crew Blueprint practical should imply that a learner may independently configure, brace, level, modify or approve a stage/platform system.

**State:** `draftable` for recognition/support/inspection-reporting concepts.

# MDQ-054 — Scenic movement, wagons, flats and show-change interfaces

## Scope

Extend the Stagehand Field Skills movement evidence into scenic-specific workflow.

## Competency families

- distinguish scenery/flat/wagon/prop/set-piece categories;
- confirm owner/destination/route before movement;
- protect finished surfaces and show-critical parts;
- identify pinch/crush/clearance hazards;
- maintain sightline/communication with assigned team;
- understand storage/staging/marking concepts;
- recognize locking/braking/guide/track concepts without treating them as universal mechanisms;
- hand off to carpenter/scenic/automation owner for final positioning/acceptance.

## Show-change / theatre context

Scenic movement can become cue- and timing-sensitive. Learners need conceptual understanding of:

- cue ownership;
- preset/strike/changeover states;
- quiet/clear backstage behavior where required;
- avoiding unassigned changes to show-critical scenery;
- reporting interference, damage or misalignment.

## Boundary

Powered scenery, counterweight/flying systems, automation and other machinery remain controlled specialist interfaces.

**State:** `draftable`.

# MDQ-055 — Staging/scenic shop-to-show lifecycle

## Canonical lifecycle

**design/advance → drafting/engineering as applicable → materials/pull → fabrication/shop prep → finish/paint → QC → case/cart/transport prep → load-in → assigned placement/install support → rehearsal/notes → show/changeovers → maintenance/repair triage → strike → return/storage/disposition**

This is a workflow map, not a construction recipe.

## Support-level learning

- recognize phases and ownership;
- understand why labels/drawings/finish protection matter;
- support movement/organization;
- preserve hardware/parts by system;
- report missing/damaged components;
- avoid unauthorized modification.

## Technician/carpenter learning

- drawing/document use;
- material/hardware/tool-system literacy;
- QC and fit/finish concepts;
- repair triage;
- change notes and handoff.

## Lead/TD learning

- labor/work-package planning;
- vendor/material coordination;
- drawing/revision control;
- schedule and transport planning;
- safety/engineering/rigging/electrical handoffs;
- QC/acceptance responsibilities.

**State:** `draftable`.

# MDQ-056 — Stage automation and machinery interface

## Scope

Automation is a specialist branch and controlled-work boundary.

General staging/scenic curriculum should teach:

- recognition of motorized/automated scenery and machinery zones;
- operator/technician/automation-programmer role distinction;
- cue/show-file and control-system concepts at awareness level;
- interlocks/safety systems as concepts;
- communication and exclusion-zone discipline;
- stop/escalate behavior;
- no assumption that ordinary scenic familiarity creates machinery authority.

## Current ESTA evidence

**ANSI E1.44-2014 (R2024)** defines common show-file requirements for entertainment stage-machinery control systems.

That standard helps establish stage automation as a distinct control-system discipline with transferable data/workflow concepts.

## Curriculum disposition

- Stagehand/Department Support: awareness and interface only.
- Department Systems: conceptual machinery/control architecture.
- TX/external specialist: actual automation operation/programming/maintenance according to employer/manufacturer/qualification requirements.

**State:** `researched` for role/interface; practical operation remains external.
