# 41 — Does Mobile Operations / IATSE Outreach Generate Sustainable Work?

**Research Queue ID:** SI-T1-002  
**Status:** Complete research packet — validation framework  
**Research date:** 2026-08-17  
**Purpose:** Determine whether a mobile, multi-market stagehand strategy can generate sustainable work rather than simply more calls with excessive travel cost and volatility.

---

## Decision question

**Can deliberate outreach to IATSE locals and other live-event labor channels across multiple markets support a sustainable mobile-work model?**

## Bottom-line finding

**Potentially yes, but sustainability is a net-opportunity problem, not a raw-call-count problem.**

The evidence supports three important facts:

1. Stagehand work is geographically distributed and organized through local/referral systems, venues, labor providers, and production employers rather than one national dispatch system.
2. Workers can and do work outside a home local or home market, but jurisdiction, referral rules, onboarding, employer paperwork, skills recognition, and local demand vary.
3. A mobile strategy only becomes economically sustainable when clusters of paid work exceed the combined cost of travel, lodging/camping, unpaid transit days, taxes, administrative friction, and the risk of cancelled or short calls.

The correct decision model is therefore:

**market access × expected paid hours × effective rate × repeatability − mobility cost − dead time − risk = net mobile opportunity**

Crew Blueprint/Deadhang should not use “number of cities contacted” or “number of locals joined” as the success metric.

---

# 1. Evidence that multi-market access is real

IATSE Local 26 provides a concrete example of a mixed local with a referral list covering a multi-city West Michigan jurisdiction. Its current public materials say its jurisdiction includes Grand Rapids, Kalamazoo, Battle Creek, Benton Harbor, Muskegon, St. Joseph, and the surrounding area. Local 26 also states that members may work with sister locals in Lansing, Flint, Ann Arbor, and Detroit and may work beyond its own jurisdiction.

Local 26 explicitly provides an **Application for Referral List** and uses CallSteward for dispatch. Its public CallSteward guidance confirms that workers receive job offers and must respond within defined time windows. The Local also acknowledges circumstances in which a stagehand travels outside the jurisdiction to work for another Local.

This proves the basic operating concept: a worker can build access to multiple regional labor channels, but the access mechanism remains local and relationship-specific.

---

# 2. Why mobile outreach can increase opportunity

Live-event demand is episodic.

A single city may have:

- arena runs;
- festivals;
- convention spikes;
- Broadway/touring weeks;
- sports changeovers;
- seasonal outdoor work;
- quiet periods.

Mobility lets the worker arbitrage **calendar differences between markets**.

The strongest version of the strategy is not random national travel. It is a **regional market network** where driving distances are short enough that work can be combined into clusters.

Examples of useful market geometry include:

- several cities along an interstate corridor;
- adjacent IATSE jurisdictions;
- a major festival market connected to nearby arena/convention markets;
- seasonal north/south movement aligned with demand.

This is consistent with prior Crew Blueprint research on geographic market lanes and the worker's ability to move between venue, festival, and field work.

---

# 3. Sustainability equation

For each market, calculate:

**Expected Net Opportunity per Calendar Day (ENOD)**

A practical formulation:

`ENOD = (expected paid gross − travel cost − lodging cost − local transport − incremental food cost − taxes/insurance reserve − expected cancellation loss) / total calendar days committed`

This is more useful than hourly rate alone.

### Example logic

A $30/hour eight-hour call may be economically weak if it requires:

- a 300-mile relocation;
- a hotel night;
- an unpaid travel day;
- parking/tolls;
- uncertain follow-on work.

A lower-rate market can outperform it if the worker receives several consecutive calls while staying cheaply nearby.

---

# 4. What to measure during outreach

For each Local/labor provider/venue market, log:

- date of first contact;
- entry method;
- referral-list/onboarding requirements;
- skills/certifications requested;
- pay structure;
- minimum call/pay guarantees if known;
- typical lead time on offers;
- acceptance/cancellation rules;
- number of offers received;
- number accepted;
- paid hours;
- overtime;
- travel days;
- travel/lodging cost;
- net earnings;
- repeat requests;
- named-request/client preference if applicable;
- seasonality.

After 3–6 months, the sustainable markets will be visible from actual data.

---

# 5. Market qualification tiers

## Tier A — Anchor market

Characteristics:

- repeat dispatch access;
- predictable seasonal or venue demand;
- multiple employers/venues;
- acceptable net opportunity after housing/travel;
- enough work to justify staying nearby.

## Tier B — Opportunistic market

Useful when:

- a festival or tour surge occurs;
- an anchor market is quiet;
- several calls can be clustered;
- travel cost is low.

## Tier C — speculative market

Low-confidence contact, isolated call, poor lodging economics, or high deadhead travel.

Do not reposition long-distance for Tier C work without a confirmed reason.

---

# 6. Relationship capital matters

Prior Crew Blueprint research showed that live-event advancement and dispatch are not purely credential-based. Reliability, availability, capability, recent experience, client request, and reputation can affect referral standing.

That means mobile outreach should be understood as a **relationship-building process**:

**contact → onboarding → first call → reliable execution → repeat dispatch → preferred/requested status**

A worker who repeatedly abandons one market whenever another offers slightly more money can damage the very trust needed for sustainable dispatch.

The mobile model must therefore optimize **long-term market reputation**, not only immediate rate.

---

# 7. Regional network beats constant roaming

The strongest operational recommendation is a hub-and-spoke pattern.

Maintain:

- one or two anchor regions;
- several adjacent secondary markets;
- a small number of high-value seasonal destinations.

This lowers:

- onboarding burden;
- travel mileage;
- administrative complexity;
- uncertainty;
- time spent learning new venues/contacts.

It also creates enough repetition for local leads and coordinators to recognize the worker.

---

# 8. Decision gates for a van/mobile strategy

The queue says SI-T1-002 informs the “van trigger decision.” A vehicle purchase or full mobile commitment should therefore depend on observed economics rather than optimism.

Recommended trigger evidence:

1. at least two markets produce repeat paid calls;
2. multiple-market work produces higher net monthly earnings than staying in one market;
3. lodging/transport savings from mobility materially improve net income;
4. vehicle fixed costs can be covered during slow months;
5. the worker maintains an emergency reserve;
6. administrative systems can handle multi-state taxes, insurance, invoices/payroll records, and receipts;
7. mobility does not materially reduce reliability or arrival readiness.

These are Crew Blueprint/Deadhang planning criteria, not industry standards.

---

# 9. What current labor-market data can and cannot tell us

BLS reports roughly **597,600 employees in Performing Arts, Spectator Sports, and Related Industries in June 2026**, but that NAICS sector includes many occupations beyond stagehands. BLS occupational categories also do not map cleanly onto the live-event stagehand craft.

Therefore national employment totals are useful for macro context but **cannot estimate the number of available stagehand calls in a particular jurisdiction**.

Local referral data, venue calendars, production schedules, employer relationships, and the worker's own call history are more decision-useful.

---

# 10. Current answer to SI-T1-002

The model is **plausible and structurally supported**, but sustainability must be validated using posted/paid work and true mobility costs.

The strongest immediate hypothesis is:

> A regional multi-market stagehand network is more sustainable than either dependence on one city or continuous long-distance roaming.

The next operational step is to maintain a market ledger and calculate net opportunity per calendar day after each run.

## Systems decision

Continue multi-market outreach, but evaluate it as a **regional portfolio of labor markets**. The strategy passes only if repeat dispatch and net earnings after mobility costs beat a single-market baseline. Use actual call history to determine anchor, opportunistic, and speculative markets before making major vehicle or relocation decisions.
