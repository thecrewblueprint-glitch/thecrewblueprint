# Frontier Evidence — Warehouse, Rental Shop, Receiving, QC, and Material Flow

**Prepared:** 2026-08-31  
**Status:** Draftable evidence baseline  
**Scope:** General education for live-event rental shops, production warehouses, receiving/return areas, prep lanes, case staging, and dock interfaces. This packet does not authorize powered industrial truck operation, equipment service, electrical testing, structural acceptance, chemical handling beyond employer-defined training, or truck-load authority.

## Why this domain needs expansion

The current Crew Blueprint shop domain is materially thinner than the field and technical departments even though warehouse/rental work is a major entry and development environment. Shop competence is not just "put gear away": it includes custody, identification, pull accuracy, prep state, discrepancy handling, traffic awareness, returns, damage quarantine, and clean handoff into production.

## Current authoritative baseline

### OSHA Warehousing and Distribution Center Operations

OSHA's Warehousing and Distribution Center National Emphasis Program, CPL 03-00-026, began inspections under the updated program on July 31, 2026. OSHA identifies powered industrial trucks, ergonomics/material handling, hazardous chemicals, slips/trips/falls, heat, egress/fire protection, and related material-flow hazards as important warehouse concerns.

Sources:
- https://www.osha.gov/warehousing/
- https://www.osha.gov/warehousing/standards-enforcement
- https://www.osha.gov/enforcement/directives/cpl-03-00-026-0

### Powered industrial trucks

Powered industrial truck operation is employer-controlled work. OSHA 29 CFR 1910.178(l) places operator training/evaluation duties on the employer. Crew Blueprint may teach pedestrian awareness, traffic-lane recognition, exclusion behavior, and escalation, but does not teach a learner to operate a forklift, powered pallet truck, or similar machine.

Sources:
- https://www.osha.gov/etools/powered-industrial-trucks/
- https://www.osha.gov/training/library/powered-industrial-trucks/general-industry

### Hazard communication

OSHA's Hazard Communication framework uses labels, safety data sheets, written programs, and worker information/training to communicate chemical hazards. A Crew Blueprint learner can be taught to recognize labels/SDS workflow and to route unknown chemicals or damaged containers to the employer's responsible process; product-specific use, PPE selection, spill response, and chemical handling remain controlled by the actual employer/program/product information.

Sources:
- https://www.osha.gov/hazcom/
- https://www.osha.gov/hazcom/HCS-Final-RegText

### Battery and charging awareness

Rechargeable batteries can create fire, explosion, and chemical hazards when damaged, defective, misused, or involved in a workplace incident. Crew Blueprint should teach condition recognition, stop/report behavior, employer-designated charging/storage workflow, and manufacturer-information ownership—not repair, opening packs, improvised charging, or emergency response.

Sources:
- https://www.osha.gov/quicktakes/013026
- https://www.osha.gov/news/newsreleases/osha-trade-release/20260209
- https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/standardinterpretations/2021-06-23

## Draftable competency families

1. Receiving and return-state recognition.
2. Chain of custody: item/case identity, location, owner, status, unresolved discrepancy.
3. Pull-list and prep-list literacy.
4. Case labeling and destination/state marking without overriding controlled labels.
5. Visible-condition QC and discrepancy handoff.
6. Separation of ready / incomplete / damaged / quarantine / repair-needed states.
7. Warehouse pedestrian and powered-equipment interface awareness.
8. Walking routes, egress, staging lanes, and obstruction reporting.
9. Hazard Communication recognition: label → SDS → employer program → responsible person.
10. Battery/charging condition awareness and employer/manufacturer handoff.
11. Dock/truck staging and load-lead ownership boundaries.
12. Return reconciliation and unresolved-loss/damage closeout.

## Authority ceiling

A general learner does **not** gain authority to:
- operate powered industrial trucks or powered access equipment;
- repair, service, electrically test, calibrate, or open equipment;
- decide structural suitability or load distribution;
- establish chemical handling/PPE/spill procedures;
- alter controlled safety/manufacturer labels;
- approve truck packs, securement, weights, or vehicle operation;
- decide that damaged battery equipment is safe to charge or reuse.

## Recommended route split

- Receiving, Returns & Chain of Custody
- Inventory, Pull Lists & Case Identification
- Equipment Prep, QC & Discrepancy Handoff
- Warehouse Traffic & Powered-Equipment Awareness
- Labels, SDS & Hazard Communication Awareness
- Battery & Charging Awareness
- Truck Staging, Dock Interface & Load-Lead Handoff
- Return Reconciliation, Damage Quarantine & Closeout

## Publication note

Claims tied to OSHA apply only where the cited federal framework applies; state-plan, local, employer, site, product, and sector rules may be more protective or different. Course language must preserve that qualifier.
