## Why this correction exists

The original MDQ-010 map made two structural mistakes:

1. it substituted **boundary burden** for the master queue's required **risk** dimension; and
2. it did not explicitly preserve two required task families: **truck-pack support / case orientation** and **basic non-energized connector recognition**.

This addendum is controlling for MDQ-010 priority methodology and required-family completeness. It does not discard the original task research.

## Corrected priority method

Each task family receives four independent 1–5 ratings:

- **Frequency** — how often a general stagehand is likely to encounter it.
- **Transferability** — how broadly the competency transfers across departments/sectors.
- **Risk importance** — consequence of misunderstanding the task or its boundary. Higher means the topic deserves earlier/clearer risk literacy; it does **not** mean Crew Blueprint may teach restricted operation.
- **Teachability** — how well Crew Blueprint can teach the bounded learner outcome without implying employer, manufacturer, specialist, legal or AHJ authorization.

`priority_score = frequency + transferability + risk_importance + teachability`

**Authority / qualification burden is tracked separately as a gate, not folded into the risk score.** A high-risk controlled topic can therefore rank as important for awareness while still terminating at an external gate.

Ties are intentional. They represent equivalent research priority, not learner seniority.

## Missing family 1 — Truck-pack support / case orientation

### Bounded learner outcome

A general hand can learn to:

- recognize that a truck/load-out may have a deliberate pack order or load plan;
- identify case labels, truck/destination identifiers and orientation cues;
- stage or move cases where the responsible loader/lead directs;
- preserve sequencing instead of improvising a new pack order;
- report a mismatch, missing case, blocked route or unclear destination;
- hand control back to the loader/lead for final placement/acceptance.

### What it does not teach

Crew Blueprint does not teach a minor/general learner to design or approve public-road cargo securement, calculate axle/load distribution, drive regulated vehicles, operate powered loading equipment, or accept a road-going load.

### Field-practice corroboration

Public stagehand/live-production discussions independently show that touring/production crews may use truck-pack diagrams, numbered cases, case destinations and deliberate order, while local hands commonly support movement under the responsible touring/production crew. These observations support the **workflow pattern**, not one universal packing scheme.

## Missing family 2 — Basic non-energized connector recognition

### Bounded learner outcome

A general hand can learn to:

- recognize common connector families visually at a basic vocabulary level;
- distinguish that visually similar connectors may have different functions or compatibility;
- read labels and follow the department's current cable/connector assignment;
- stop rather than force a connector that does not seat normally;
- ask the responsible technician when connector identity, signal, power state or destination is unclear.

### What it does not teach

Recognition does not authorize electrical connection, temporary-power work, live/energized handling, patch changes, system configuration, troubleshooting or specialty testing. Exact connector use remains department, equipment, manufacturer and employer controlled.

### Field-practice corroboration

Public live-production discussions show that connector-family literacy is normal vocabulary, while the same discussions also expose why visual resemblance is unsafe as a decision rule: connector families and signal/power uses can differ even when shells appear similar. Community evidence is therefore used to justify **recognition + stop/ask behavior**, not operational patching instruction.
