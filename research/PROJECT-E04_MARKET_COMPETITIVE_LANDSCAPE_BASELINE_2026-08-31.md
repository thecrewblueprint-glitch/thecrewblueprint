# PROJECT-E04 — Market / Competitive Landscape Baseline

**Prepared:** 2026-08-31  
**Status:** Current-market baseline; direct customer/adoption research still required  
**Purpose:** Identify the main live-event/stagehand/AV training substitutes and adjacent credential ecosystems so Crew Blueprint positioning is based on what actually exists rather than on an assumption of an empty market.

---

# 1. Executive finding

The market is **fragmented, not empty**.

Current offerings fall into several different models:

1. short online stagehand orientation courses;
2. hands-on stagehand / AV bootcamps;
3. union/member training and local stagecraft education;
4. safety-specific online credentials/training;
5. college/workforce certificate programs;
6. professional AV certification/training ecosystems;
7. experienced entertainment-rigging/electrical certification ecosystems;
8. manufacturer/product training;
9. books/free web/video/community learning.

No source in this baseline proves Crew Blueprint has product-market fit. The evidence does show that **training, career progression, workforce development, certification, organizational licensing, and portable learning records already exist as separate industry needs**.

The competitive question is therefore not “does anybody teach stagehands?” It is:

> **Can Crew Blueprint combine deep stagecraft content, accurate authority boundaries, visual task instruction, source traceability, career progression, living updates, and eventually credible learner/workforce evidence in a way existing fragmented options do not?**

That remains a hypothesis to test.

# 2. Direct / near-direct stagehand training

## Stagehand 101 — Introduction to Production

Current public offer:

- online course;
- $60;
- 29 lessons;
- 30-day access;
- marketed as roughly 45–60 minutes;
- covers stagehand role, load-in/load-out, terminology, safety and protocol;
- quiz + digital certificate;
- claims collaboration with venues/labor contractors/stagehands.

Source:  
https://www.stagehand101.com/

### Competitive significance

This is the clearest direct comparison for entry-level online stagehand orientation.

Potential strengths:

- simple value proposition;
- inexpensive;
- fast completion;
- clear first-gig framing;
- certificate signal;
- partnership/testimonial positioning.

Questions Crew Blueprint should test rather than assume:

- Do learners actually want much deeper material before first calls?
- Does a 45–60 minute orientation solve the dominant problem well enough?
- Do employers value this certificate or mainly value that someone completed basic orientation?
- Is 30-day access beneficial for focus or harmful for long-term reference value?

Crew Blueprint should not imitate the competitor's certificate language unless the evidence supports exactly what the CB record means.

## CoiL Learning Center — Stagehand Bootcamp / AV Tech Bootcamp

Current public positioning:

- hands-on beginner Stagehand Bootcamp;
- live-event safety/setup, cable wrapping, loading/organization and basic rigging awareness;
- equipment practice;
- further tracks in Audio, Video, Lighting and LED;
- AV Tech Bootcamp explicitly positions itself as a transition from stagehand basics to more technical roles.

Sources:  
https://www.coillearningcenter.com/av-technical-training/stagehand-bootcamp  
https://www.coillearningcenter.com/av-technical-training/av-tech-bootcamp  
https://www.coillearningcenter.com/av-technical-training

### Competitive significance

CoiL demonstrates a strong **hands-on-first + specialization** model.

Crew Blueprint should not pretend online content can fully substitute for this. A stronger potential position is:

- prepare learners before hands-on work;
- improve mental models between calls/classes;
- offer durable reference/career navigation;
- track bounded observed practice separately when real practice occurs;
- complement rather than counterfeit hands-on training.

# 3. Union / labor training ecosystem

## IATSE Training Trust Fund

The IATSE Training Trust Fund provides eligible IATSE workers with safety awareness, craft skills and training resources across areas including stagecraft, lighting, networking, rigging/automation, video/camera and wardrobe.

Source:  
https://www.iatsetrainingtrust.org/additional-training/iatse-ttf-training

### Significance

For eligible union workers, this can be a powerful free/subsidized substitute.

Crew Blueprint should not position itself as replacing local/TTF training. Better hypotheses include:

- preparation before union access;
- cross-market reference material;
- learner-controlled career maps;
- complementary source-transparent content;
- support for nonunion, freelance, venue, corporate and mixed-market workers;
- organization-specific pathways only where partnerships permit.

# 4. Safety-specific training market

## Event Safety Alliance — ESAT

Event Safety Alliance offers Event Safety Access Training (ESAT), positioned as an online entry-level safety awareness program/competency credential for event professionals. Topics include safety concepts, roles/responsibilities, OSHA, PPE, event hazards, crowds and severe weather.

Source:  
https://eventsafetyalliance.org/online-learning/event-safety-access-training

ESA also offers event-focused OSHA Outreach courses.

Source:  
https://eventsafetyalliance.org/osha-training

### Competitive / partnership significance

Crew Blueprint should not duplicate an established safety credential merely to own every layer of the learner journey.

Research should evaluate whether the better architecture is to:

- teach contextual safety awareness;
- link/recognize legitimate external safety training;
- avoid implying equivalent credential authority;
- potentially partner/interoperate where appropriate.

# 6. Professional AV training / certification ecosystem

## AVIXA CTS

Current CTS certification:

- general AV professional certification;
- no mandatory course prerequisite;
- AVIXA recommends experience (currently at least about six months for CTS applicants);
- exam delivered through secure Pearson testing centers;
- current public U.S. exam price: $490 nonmember / $390 Premium or Elite member.

Source:  
https://www.avixa.org/training-certification/certification/cts-certification

## AVIXA training

Current CTS Prep Pathway:

- on-demand structured learning path;
- current public price $449;
- pathway + exam bundle $929;
- broader online, virtual and instructor-led training ecosystem;
- organization/contract/licensing options.

Sources:  
https://www.avixa.org/training-certification/certification-prep/overview  
https://www.avixa.org/training-certification

AVIXA also publishes deeper technical courses and CTS-I/CTS-D pathways.

### Significance

AVIXA demonstrates several mature product patterns worth studying:

- free/introductory vs paid deeper learning;
- certification separate from required training purchase;
- career pathways;
- on-demand + instructor-led formats;
- organization membership/licensing;
- renewal/continuing education;
- employer-recognized professional signals.

Crew Blueprint should learn from these patterns without pretending to be CTS or duplicating AVIXA's credential authority.

# 7. Entertainment specialist credential ecosystem

## ETCP

Current ETCP credentials target experienced entertainment technicians in disciplines such as rigging and entertainment electrical/portable power.

Current public U.S./Canada first-exam fees shown by ETCP:

- $650 nonmember;
- $550 member;
- practice exam $40.

Source:  
https://etcp.esta.org/certify/certify.html

### Significance

ETCP is especially important for Crew Blueprint's liability/authority architecture:

- specialist credentialing is experience-based and externally governed;
- education, observed practice, employer authorization and certification are different evidence states;
- a beginner platform should navigate learners toward legitimate specialist pathways rather than issue lookalike credentials.

# 10. Likely differentiation hypotheses to test

These are **hypotheses**, not marketing claims yet.

## H1 — Evidence transparency

Crew Blueprint may differentiate by exposing:

- source lineage;
- current-vs-local-vs-manufacturer scope;
- review state;
- claim qualifications;
- update history.

Test whether learners/buyers actually value this enough to affect trust or purchase/adoption.

## H2 — Breadth + career continuity

Potential differentiation:

> first-call stagehand reality → broad general skills → department systems → advanced paths → external credentials/careers

Test whether users prefer one durable career platform or focused short courses.

## H3 — Visual field-task library

A high-quality visual/interactive task library may be more useful on mobile and between calls than conventional chapter-based courses.

Test against linear course, search/reference and hybrid formats.

## H4 — Honest evidence states

Separating:

- learned;
- knowledge checked;
- practiced;
- observed;
- employer authorized;
- externally credentialed

may be a meaningful trust advantage for employers and workers.

Test whether buyers actually want/understand these states.

## H5 — Living content / release system

Versioned, continuously researched content with scheduled improvements may outperform one-time static course access.

Test retention/value and willingness to pay.

## H6 — Workforce tooling

Organization assignment/review/progress/version workflows could create B2B value beyond education.

This is currently one of the largest hypotheses and requires buyer discovery before backend implementation.

# 11. Competitive risks

1. **Free training is abundant for some segments.** Union members, manufacturer customers and online learners may already have strong free options.
2. **Hands-on competitors own physical practice.** Online-only claims must stay modest.
3. **Credential ecosystems already have authority.** Competing directly with ETCP/AVIXA/OSHA-like credentials would create unnecessary credibility and liability problems.
4. **Content can be copied/commoditized.** The durable moat is unlikely to be prose alone.
5. **The market is fragmented by sector.** Concert, theatre, corporate AV, broadcast, festivals, venues, locals and warehouses may value different content.
6. **A huge curriculum can overwhelm beginners.** Depth must not become poor information architecture.
7. **B2B software expands compliance/security burden.** Workforce records create obligations absent from a static educational site.

---

# 12. Current market conclusion

The evidence supports continuing toward a **deep workforce-development platform**, but not locking one commercial model.

The clearest strategic opening to investigate is not “another beginner stagehand certificate.” It is a system that potentially combines:

- rigorous living content;
- visual field preparation;
- broad-to-specialist career pathways;
- external credential navigation;
- explicit authority boundaries;
- source/version transparency;
- learner evidence/practice records;
- organization workflows when validated.

That combination appears less directly represented in this baseline than its individual components.
