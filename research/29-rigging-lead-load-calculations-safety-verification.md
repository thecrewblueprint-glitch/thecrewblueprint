# 29 — Rigging Lead: Load Calculations & Safety Verification

**Research Queue ID:** CB-006  
**Status:** Complete research packet  
**Research date:** 2026-08-17  
**Depends on:** CB-001 / Package 24  
**Purpose:** Define the knowledge, decision responsibilities, verification duties, and hard training boundaries for a rigging lead tier without converting Crew Blueprint into unsupervised rigging instruction.

---

## Decision question

**What distinguishes rigging-lead authority from ordinary rigging participation, and what must a lead be able to verify before overhead work proceeds?**

## Bottom-line finding

ETCP’s current Arena Rigger framework provides a strong external benchmark. It treats professional arena-rigging competence as an integrated combination of:

- planning and engineering concepts;
- formulas/forces and load distribution;
- facility/show drawing interpretation;
- hardware/system knowledge;
- inspection;
- task assignment;
- communications and controlled zones;
- pre-movement system verification;
- operations and risk management.

Source: ETCP — Arena Rigger Examination Content  
https://etcp.esta.org/certify/examination_rigger_arena.html

The curriculum implication is:

> **A rigging lead is not simply the most experienced person handling hardware. The lead is accountable for whether the planned rigging system, crew assignment, verification process, communication structure, and movement authorization are appropriate within their qualified scope.**

Because this is safety-critical overhead work, Crew Blueprint should not attempt to teach a novice how to calculate, select, attach, or operate a rigging system through online instruction alone. CB-006 should instead define **lead readiness, professional reasoning, verification architecture, and certification/practical-training boundaries**.

---

# 1. ETCP establishes the professional scope

ETCP’s Arena Rigger certification is explicitly intended for experienced riggers and evaluates the knowledge base of the upper tier of the profession. ETCP describes Arena Rigging as chain-hoist/truss-based temporary suspension work used in arenas, convention/trade-show spaces, theatrical venues, and other entertainment settings.

Source: ETCP — Get Certified: Rigger — Arena / Theatre  
https://etcp.esta.org/certify/certify_rigger.html

The current Arena examination is based on a formal job analysis and covers three broad areas:

1. **Planning and Engineering**
2. **Installation**
3. **Materials and Equipment**

The published outline includes load concepts, working-load limits, strength/material factors, center of gravity, load distribution, facility/show drawings, inspections, risk management, task assignment, communications, pre-movement inspection, load monitoring, system controls, manufacturer recommendations, and system properties.

That is strong evidence that a legitimate rigging-lead curriculum must integrate technical reasoning with supervision and verification.

---

# 2. Recommended lead competency domains

## 2.1 Plan comprehension and constraint recognition

A rigging lead should be able to interpret the approved rigging package sufficiently to understand:

- what is being suspended;
- the intended support/load path;
- venue limitations and obstructions;
- relevant engineered or manufacturer documentation;
- work sequence and department dependencies;
- which questions require engineering, venue, head-rigger, or other specialist escalation.

Crew Blueprint should teach **interpretation and authority boundaries**, not unsupervised design.

## 2.2 Load-calculation literacy

ETCP’s professional benchmark clearly requires quantitative understanding of forces, load distribution, design factors, working-load limits, material/system properties, and center of gravity.

For Crew Blueprint, this should become a readiness rule:

> A person cannot be represented as ready for rigging-lead responsibility merely because they can direct a crew; they must already possess the underlying mathematical and rigging competence required by the real work.

CB-006 itself should not substitute for hands-on rigging education, supervised experience, employer qualification, engineering review, or ETCP certification.

## 2.3 Inspection and verification

The ETCP outline includes inspection of assembled hardware, verification of lifted objects against engineering/load documentation, pre-movement inspection of the entire assembly, and confirmation of system integrity.

This supports a core lead behavior:

**no movement authorization without verification appropriate to the system and responsible role.**

The course can safely teach the verification framework:

- confirm the approved plan/reference exists;
- confirm designated qualified personnel have performed required checks;
- confirm discrepancies are resolved/documented;
- confirm communication and exclusion/control measures are in place;
- confirm the responsible authority has released the system for movement.

It should not teach inexperienced learners how to perform technical rigging inspection by visual examples alone.

## 2.4 Crew assignment

ETCP explicitly includes assigning tasks to riggers and defining safety/communication procedures.

A rigging lead must therefore understand capability matching:

- qualified/experienced riggers to safety-critical tasks;
- trainees or ground support only within assigned supervised scope;
- no assumption that general stagehand experience equals overhead-rigging qualification;
- work stopped when required expertise is unavailable.

## 2.5 Communication and controlled operations

Lead-level responsibility includes ensuring there is one understood communication structure for movement and that affected personnel know the status of the operation.

The curriculum should focus on command clarity, confirmation, conflict resolution, and stop/escalation decisions—not operational movement commands or step-by-step lift procedures.

# 3. Standards and inspection context

ESTA’s Rigging Working Group publishes a broad standards portfolio relevant to professional rigging, including:

- ANSI E1.47 — Recommended Guidelines for Entertainment Rigging Inspections;
- ANSI E1.56 — Rigging Support Points;
- ANSI E1.6 series — entertainment chain-hoist systems and controls;
- ANSI E1.67 — hand/lever-operated chain hoists;
- ANSI E1.2 — aluminum trusses and towers;
- ANSI E1.39 — personal fall-arrest systems on portable entertainment structures.

Source: ESTA Rigging Working Group  
https://tsp.esta.org/tsp/working_groups/RIG/rig.html

This reinforces that rigging-lead competence sits inside a real standards ecosystem and should not be reduced to informal rules of thumb.

# 4. Recommended CB-006 curriculum architecture

## Module A — Authority, qualification, and responsibility

- lead versus technician versus engineer;
- employer authorization;
- ETCP role and limitations;
- venue/production/engineering interfaces;
- when the lead must defer.

## Module B — Planning and load-literacy framework

- why load calculations matter;
- types of information a professional rigging plan contains;
- identifying missing or conflicting information;
- recognizing when an engineering answer is required;
- no field improvisation outside authorization.

## Module C — Crew and communication control

- capability matching;
- task assignment by qualification;
- communication structure;
- coordination with other departments;
- scheduling without trading safety for speed.

## Module D — Verification architecture

- documentation checks;
- inspection responsibilities;
- discrepancy control;
- pre-movement readiness concept;
- accurate release/handoff status.

## Module E — Change, fault, and escalation scenarios

- changed weight/configuration;
- venue obstruction/conflict;
- damaged or questionable equipment;
- missing documentation;
- pressure to proceed without required verification;
- weather/structural/environmental changes where relevant.

---

# 5. Assessment model

Assess decisions, not physical rigging technique.

Useful scenarios:

1. Show documentation changes after the rigging package was approved.
2. A required qualified person is unavailable but production is behind schedule.
3. Two documents disagree about a load or location.
4. Equipment condition is questioned during setup.
5. Another department requests a change that alters the planned suspended system.
6. A crew member reports a concern shortly before movement.

The learner should identify:

- whether work continues or pauses;
- who has decision authority;
- what information must be verified;
- what must be documented/escalated;
- why schedule pressure does not override missing safety-critical verification.

---

# 6. Hard boundaries

Crew Blueprint completion must never be represented as:

- ETCP Arena Rigger certification;
- employer qualification to rig;
- engineering authority;
- authorization to select or configure rigging hardware;
- authorization to perform overhead work;
- proof of competent-person status under every applicable rule;
- substitute for supervised practical training and demonstrated field competence.

ETCP eligibility itself is experience-based and specifically targets experienced riggers. That makes it an inappropriate model for suggesting that online completion alone can produce a rigging lead.

## Curriculum decision

**Treat rigging lead as a high-competence, safety-critical professional tier whose online curriculum focuses on leadership reasoning, verification architecture, scope control, and escalation—not on replacing formal rigging training or qualification.**
