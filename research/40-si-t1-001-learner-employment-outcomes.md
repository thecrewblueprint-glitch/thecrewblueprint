# 40 — Do Crew Blueprint Learners Get Hired After Completing Courses?

**Research Queue ID:** SI-T1-001  
**Status:** Complete research packet — measurement decision  
**Research date:** 2026-08-17  
**Purpose:** Determine what evidence would be required to claim that Crew Blueprint improves hiring outcomes, and establish the measurement system before pricing or Course 3 scope is based on unsupported assumptions.

---

## Decision question

**Do Crew Blueprint learners get hired after completing courses?**

## Bottom-line finding

**This cannot currently be answered empirically from the available evidence.** Crew Blueprint does not yet have a sufficiently mature learner cohort with verified pre-course baseline data and post-course employment follow-up to establish a hiring effect.

The correct research result is therefore not a guessed hiring percentage. It is a measurement standard.

Crew Blueprint should adopt a workforce-outcomes model based on established U.S. training-program accountability concepts:

1. completion;
2. measurable skill gain;
3. job search / application activity;
4. employment after exit;
5. employment in a related field;
6. earnings / paid-call activity;
7. retention and repeat employment;
8. employer satisfaction or repeat request.

The U.S. Department of Labor's WIOA system is the strongest public benchmark for outcome architecture. WIOA tracks employment in the second and fourth quarters after exit, median earnings in the second quarter, credential attainment, measurable skill gains, and employer-serving effectiveness. Crew Blueprint does not need to copy WIOA reporting mechanically, but its structure provides a defensible model for distinguishing **course completion from employment outcome**.

---

# 1. Why completion rate is not enough

A training product can have high completion and still fail to improve employment.

Crew Blueprint should maintain separate metrics for:

- enrollment;
- course completion;
- assessment performance;
- demonstrated learning;
- job applications;
- interviews/referral-list acceptance;
- first paid call;
- continued paid work;
- earnings;
- employer/crew-lead feedback.

A learner who passes a quiz but never applies for work has a different outcome from a learner who applies but cannot get hired, and both differ from a learner who receives a first call but is not rebooked.

---

# 2. Recommended primary outcome

For an entry-level career-readiness course, the most useful initial outcome is:

> **Percentage of employment-seeking completers who obtain at least one verified paid live-event production call within 90 days of course completion.**

This should be accompanied by a broader 180-day measure because stagehand work is seasonal and call-based.

### Why employment-seeking completers?

Not every learner intends to seek immediate paid employment. Including hobbyists, students, venue volunteers, or learners taking the course only for orientation would distort the hiring metric.

At enrollment or completion, ask whether the learner intends to seek paid live-event work within the next six months.

---

# 3. Secondary outcomes

## 3.1 Time to first paid call

Measure days between completion and first verified paid stagehand/event-production work.

## 3.2 Paid-call count

At 90 and 180 days, record the number of paid calls or workdays. A single first call is useful, but repeat work is a stronger labor-market signal.

## 3.3 Employer / labor-provider retention

Track whether the learner:

- receives a second call;
- remains on a referral/dispatch list;
- is requested by name;
- receives positive documented feedback;
- advances to additional responsibility.

## 3.4 Earnings

Where learners voluntarily report it, capture total live-event earnings during defined windows.

Do not require intrusive financial documentation for ordinary outcome tracking. Self-report can be used with optional verification categories.

## 3.5 Related-field employment

Separate:

- stagehand/general production work;
- department-support work;
- venue AV/event technology;
- theatre production;
- production warehouse/shop work;
- other live-event roles.

A learner taking a stagehand course and becoming a venue event technician may still represent a successful related outcome.

---

# 4. Measurement schedule

Recommended checkpoints:

### At enrollment

Capture:

- prior paid live-event experience;
- current employment status;
- previous number of stagehand calls, if any;
- location/market;
- intended work pathway;
- whether the learner plans to seek work.

### At course completion

Capture:

- assessment result;
- self-efficacy/readiness measures;
- job-search intention;
- target labor providers/locals/venues.

### 30 days

Capture:

- applications/referral-list submissions;
- interviews/orientations;
- barriers encountered;
- first-call status.

### 90 days

Primary employment outcome.

### 180 days

Repeat work, earnings, retention, pathway movement.

### 12 months

Longer-term retention and advancement for learners who consent to follow-up.

---

# 5. Verification model

Use evidence tiers rather than requiring sensitive records from every learner.

### Tier 1 — self report

Learner reports employer, role, and date range.

### Tier 2 — lightweight corroboration

Optional supporting evidence such as a call sheet, non-sensitive schedule entry, dispatch confirmation, or employer email with personal information minimized.

### Tier 3 — partner confirmation

For participating employers/labor providers, confirm aggregate placement or retention directly.

Public reporting should use aggregate numbers and minimum-cell rules to avoid exposing individual learners.

---

# 6. Attribution problem

Even if graduates get hired, Crew Blueprint should not automatically claim the course caused the hiring.

Important confounders include:

- prior experience;
- local labor demand;
- seasonality;
- personal network/referrals;
- union/referral-list access;
- transportation;
- schedule availability;
- geography;
- existing certifications;
- prior employer relationships.

A stronger evaluation design compares outcomes across learner baselines.

Possible later designs include:

- pre/post cohort comparisons;
- matched comparison groups;
- staggered course rollout;
- employer-partner cohorts;
- regression controlling for prior experience and market.

Do not claim causal impact until the study design supports it.

---

# 7. WIOA as an external benchmark

The U.S. Department of Labor uses six primary WIOA performance indicators, including:

- employment rate in the second quarter after exit;
- employment rate in the fourth quarter after exit;
- median earnings in the second quarter;
- credential attainment;
- measurable skill gains;
- effectiveness in serving employers.

This is useful because it demonstrates that serious workforce systems measure **employment, earnings, skill progress, and employer outcomes separately**.

Crew Blueprint can use a lighter-weight adaptation suited to a small vocational platform.

---

# 8. Minimum viable dashboard

Before making employment claims, the platform should be able to report:

- number enrolled;
- number completed;
- number intending to seek work;
- response rate at 90 days;
- percentage of respondents with first paid call;
- percentage with repeat paid work;
- median time to first paid call;
- percentage entering a live-event-related role;
- top reported barriers;
- geographic distribution;
- employer/labor-provider partners where applicable.

Every percentage should display the denominator.

Example:

**18 of 27 employment-seeking completers who responded to the 90-day survey reported at least one paid live-event call.**

That is more trustworthy than "67% of graduates get hired" when response rates or sample size are hidden.

---

# 9. Decision thresholds

Do not set a universal success percentage before baseline data exists.

Instead use staged gates:

### Pilot gate

Can the system obtain follow-up data from at least 60–70% of a small pilot cohort?

### Signal gate

Do employment-seeking completers show credible first-call and repeat-call outcomes?

### Comparison gate

Are outcomes meaningfully stronger for learners who demonstrate course mastery than for otherwise similar learners without the course or before training?

### Marketing-claim gate

Only publish outcome claims after sample size, response rate, definition, timeframe, and methodology are documented.

---

# 10. Current answer to SI-T1-001

**Unknown today. Measure it before claiming it.**

The absence of data is itself a critical finding because SI-T1-001 blocks Course 3 scope and pricing in the queue. Advanced course investment should not be justified by an assumed hiring effect from foundational courses.

## Systems decision

Build employment-outcome instrumentation **before** using learner hiring as evidence for pricing, marketing, or expansion. Crew Blueprint's first defensible employment KPI should be verified or clearly labeled self-reported first paid live-event work among employment-seeking completers, with 90-day and 180-day follow-up and transparent denominators.
