The Call Sheet

The call sheet is the one document every new stagehand actually needs to read closely. It's usually sent out before the job and tells you everything you need to know to show up ready.

What It Tells You

  • Date and venue — where you're going, usually with a full address if it's somewhere new.
  • Crew call time — when you need to be there, which is not the same as show time or doors.
  • Sign-in location and PC — who's running sign-in (often called the PC, or Production Coordinator) and where to find them — a parking lot, a specific door, a dock.
  • Parking instructions — where to park so you don't get towed or block a truck route.
  • Attire — black clothes, closed-toe shoes, or whatever the call requires.
  • Emergency contact — usually separate from the PC, for anything urgent that can't wait.

Read it before you leave the house, not in the parking lot. If anything on it is unclear, that's exactly what the PC is for. On many calls, this is also the sheet you sign at sign-in to confirm your hours — and on bigger crews, your name can show up more than once with different call times, so check the whole thing carefully, not just the first mention of your name.

Where You Fit In

As a new stagehand, you won't own or manage this paperwork — but you'll see pieces of it on most calls. Knowing what it is, and who it belongs to, helps you stay useful without needing to manage any of it yourself.

What You'll Actually See

  • You'll usually get a Call Sheet before the job — read it closely, it has everything you need to show up ready. It's often the same sheet you sign at sign-in with the PC, which is how your hours and headcount get tracked.
  • Everything else — production reports, run sheets, spike plots — belongs to stage management and leads. You don't need to manage it, just follow direction when it affects you.
