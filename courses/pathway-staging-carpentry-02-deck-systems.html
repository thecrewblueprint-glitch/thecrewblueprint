Owner Review · Staging Course 2

Modular Deck Systems Literacy

Course 2 explains how staging teams think about modular systems: component identity, drawings, interfaces, tolerances, inspection handoffs, and documentation. It intentionally stops short of teaching one generic deck-assembly or fabrication procedure.

System Literacy Begins With Exact Identity

A deck, platform, riser, stair, rail, support, or accessory belongs to a specific system with its own design limits and instructions.

Learn to distinguish a system family, manufacturer or fabricator documentation, component identification, approved hardware, current drawing set, and the people responsible for final acceptance. Similar appearance does not make two systems interchangeable.

Read the Build as a Set of Relationships

The useful systems question is “what must agree?” rather than “what do I assemble first?”

RelationshipSystems-literacy question
Drawing ↔ field conditionDoes the installed environment match the current plan?
Component ↔ system familyIs this the identified component the plan expects?
Interface ↔ approved hardwareDoes the responsible system documentation identify the intended interface?
Completed work ↔ acceptanceWho is authorized to inspect and accept this portion of the build?

Why Generic Assembly Procedure Is Blocked

The current Crew Blueprint matrix does not have one exact deck system, current manufacturer procedure, and qualified practical review that could justify a universal learner-facing assembly sequence.

That is a release boundary, not a missing paragraph. The course therefore teaches system recognition, communication, documentation, and escalation. Practical procedure must come from the exact current system and the qualified workplace process responsible for it.

Fabrication Literacy Without Fabrication Instruction

Course 2 can explain why specifications, drawings, tolerances, materials, finish requirements, and inspection records matter without teaching shop fabrication steps.

A learner should be able to recognize that a fabrication detail carries design intent and that substitutions or field changes can affect fit, strength, finish, or approval. The responsible fabricator, engineer where applicable, supervisor, or other authorized process owns the method and acceptance.

Discrepancy Handling

  • Identify what record or component does not match the expected system.
  • Keep the discrepancy visible to the responsible lead.
  • Do not substitute hardware, change geometry, modify structure, or invent a field repair.
  • Wait for the authorized decision and updated direction before the affected work continues.

Knowledge Check

Why doesn't Course 2 teach one modular-deck assembly sequence?

Because systems differ, and the matrix does not have an exact current system plus qualified practical review that would justify a universal procedure.

What can a learner do with drawings at this level?

Use them to understand relationships, identity, destination, and discrepancies while leaving structural decisions and acceptance to the responsible authority.

Does fabrication literacy authorize fabrication?

No. It teaches how specifications and records communicate design intent; the actual process remains with trained and authorized personnel.

Publication Boundary

This owner-review course does not authorize structural assembly, modification, fabrication, hardware substitution, load-bearing decisions, leveling acceptance, stairs/rail acceptance, inspection signoff, or exact-system installation procedure.
