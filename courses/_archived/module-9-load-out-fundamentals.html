Module 9

Load-Out Fundamentals

Load-out feels faster than load-in, and it usually is. Everyone's more tired, it's later, and the room is darker. That combination is exactly why it still needs structure.

Load-Out vs. Load-In Staying Alert Case & Cable Strike Truck Pack & Walkthrough Finishing Strong

What Makes Load-Out Different From Load-In

Same gear, opposite direction, and a different set of pressures entirely.

Load-in is about building the show. Load-out is about striking it and getting the gear out — often late at night, after a long day, with people tired, hungry, and mentally checked out. The room is usually darker, louder, and more crowded than it was that morning.

None of that changes the sequence. Strike means taking down, packing, or clearing show equipment, and it follows a planned order — some gear can't move until another department is finished, some cable may still be active or in use, and some equipment may be supporting a system that hasn't been cleared yet. Randomly striking gear because it looks done is exactly what strike order exists to prevent. Wait for direction before you move or break anything down.

For the fast-facts version of what to actually do at load-out — when you're allowed to leave, how sign-out works — see the Load-In / Load-Out Reference in the Resource Hub.

Staying Alert When Everyone Is Tired

Fatigue is one of the biggest risks at load-out — bigger than any single piece of gear.

Fatigue reduces focus, reaction time, patience, and judgment. Tired workers rush, stop communicating, or miss hazards they'd normally catch without thinking. Don't try to "prove yourself" by ignoring how tired you actually are — hydration, food, taking an approved break, and just communicating that you're running low are all normal, expected moves, not weakness.

Case Packing and Cable Strike Discipline

Two places where rushing at the end of a long day creates real, avoidable damage.

Road cases are assigned to specific gear, and labels, colors, and department tags exist to keep it that way. Don't force gear into a case, and don't close a case that hasn't been checked by the department it belongs to — incorrect packing can damage gear or delay the next show entirely.

Cable strike means removing, gathering, coiling, or staging cable once it's been cleared — not before. Some cable may still be connected, active, or needed even after the show ends. Never pull cable without checking direction, and never unplug power, feeder, or motor lines yourself. Same rule as load-in, just easier to forget when everyone's tired and moving fast.

Truck Pack Awareness and Final Walkthroughs

The truck has a plan too, and the job isn't done when the big gear is gone.

Truck loading isn't random — gear has a planned order and position, and truck loaders or leads control what goes in and when. The dock, ramp, liftgate, and trailer area are high-risk zones: don't enter trailers, stack gear, or load cases unless you're assigned to, and never stand behind a rolling case on a ramp.

Cleanup is part of professional load-out, not an afterthought. Trash, tape, zip ties, and loose hardware left behind create slip, trip, and fire hazards, and emergency exits, aisles, ramps, and fire lanes have to stay clear right up until everyone's actually gone. A final walkthrough catches forgotten gear and unsafe conditions before they become someone else's problem.

Avoiding "Hurry Up and Get Hurt," and Finishing Strong

The last hour of a call is where reputations get made or lost, in both directions.

Load-out creates real pressure to move fast, but speed without control is what causes injuries, gear damage, and confusion — running with cases, yanking cable, lifting alone, blocking paths, ignoring pinch points, skipping communication. The safest crews move quickly because they're organized, not because they're reckless. Move with purpose, not panic.

Finishing strong means staying useful until you're actually released: don't disappear near the end, check in when tasks are done, help with cleanup and final details, return anything borrowed, and report damage or lost items instead of just leaving. The last impression you leave on a call is often what determines whether you get the next one.

Quick Knowledge Check

Click a question to check your answer.

Why does gear come down in a specific strike order instead of whatever's fastest?

Some gear can't move until another department finishes, and some cable or equipment may still be active or supporting another system — strike order prevents moving the wrong thing at the wrong time.

You're exhausted near the end of a long call. What should you do?

Hydrate, eat, take an approved break if needed, and communicate that you're running low — ignoring fatigue to "prove yourself" is what causes preventable mistakes.

Can you close a road case once the gear looks like it fits?

No — a case shouldn't be closed until it's been checked by the department it belongs to.

Are you allowed to enter a trailer or stack gear on the truck if you see room?

No — truck loading follows a planned order controlled by truck loaders or leads; don't enter trailers or load cases unless you're assigned to.

What does "finishing strong" actually mean at load-out?

Staying useful until released — not disappearing, checking in when done, helping with cleanup, returning borrowed items, and reporting damage or lost gear.

Key Takeaways

  • Load-out feels faster and looser than load-in, but it still follows a planned strike order — wait for direction before moving or breaking anything down.
  • Fatigue is one of the biggest load-out risks — hydrate, eat, and communicate when you're running low instead of pushing through it.
  • Cases and cable both require the same discipline as load-in: don't force packing, don't close a case unchecked, don't pull cable without direction.
  • The dock, ramp, and trailer stay high-risk right to the end — don't enter trucks or load cases unless assigned, and keep emergency paths clear until everyone's gone.
  • "Hurry up and get hurt" mistakes come from speed without control — the safest crews move fast because they're organized, not reckless. Finish strong instead of disappearing.
