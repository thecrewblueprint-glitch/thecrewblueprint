Module 3

PPE, Clothing, and What to Bring

A call sheet can set a dress expectation. The workplace and task set the protective-equipment requirement. Those are two different systems, and a reliable hand knows which one they are looking at.

Work AttirePPE AuthorityWhat to BringCall ReadinessDay One

Work Attire Is a Call Requirement, Not PPE by Default

Many live-event crews use dark or all-black clothing, especially where crew may be visible during a show. That is a production convention unless a separate safety rule makes a garment protective equipment.

Read the call sheet, employer message, venue rules, or department instructions before the call. Some jobs want show blacks. Other calls specify high-visibility clothing, weather gear, a uniform, or something else. Do not treat one employer's dress rule as an industry-wide safety standard.

Crew rule

When the call gives a clothing requirement, follow that requirement. If it is unclear, ask the employer or lead before arrival rather than guessing.

PPE Starts With the Hazard, Not a Generic Packing List

OSHA's general-industry PPE rule puts workplace hazard assessment and PPE selection on the employer. Required equipment changes with the actual hazards and task.

OSHA also has rules about when employers must pay for required PPE, with listed exceptions for some ordinary clothing and non-specialty items. The useful beginner habit is simpler: read the employer's requirements before the call, bring only what you are expected to bring, and do not substitute personal equipment for missing employer training or authorization.

What to Bring

Bring what the employer or call sheet tells you to bring, plus normal personal items that help you stay ready for the day.

  • Identification, check-in information, and any employer-required documents.
  • Clothing that matches the call requirement and the expected weather.
  • Water and food planning appropriate to the venue's rules and the length of the call.
  • Only employer-approved personal tools or equipment that you are trained and authorized to use.
  • As few unsecured valuables as practical; storage conditions vary by venue.

Do not assume a personal tool is allowed because another venue allowed it. Security rules, employer policies, and job assignments change from site to site.

Long-Call Readiness

Live-event schedules can be long or irregular. Plan your transportation, food, water, weather protection, and rest around the actual call information rather than a fixed industry timetable.

Federal wage law does not create a universal lunch-or-coffee-break schedule. State law, a collective bargaining agreement, employer policy, venue practice, or the specific call may add requirements. Learn the rule that applies to your job. When an employer provides a short rest break, federal wage rules generally treat that short break as compensable time; bona fide meal periods follow different rules.

If a schedule or working condition changes and you do not know what is expected, ask the lead or employer. Do not make a safety decision by relying on a remembered rule from a different venue or employer.

What Prepared Looks Like on Day One

Before the call

Read the call details, confirm the location and check-in point, and resolve unclear attire or PPE requirements before arrival.

At check-in

Find the right lead or check-in contact, listen for site rules, and do not treat a previous venue's procedure as authority for this one.

When assigned

Make sure you understand the task, the boundary of your role, and any required protective equipment before starting.

Quick Knowledge Check

Are show blacks automatically PPE?

No. A production may require dark clothing as a dress convention. PPE is selected for actual workplace hazards.

Who determines required PPE for a workplace task?

The employer's hazard assessment and the controlling workplace requirements determine the required PPE; other applicable standards or site rules can also control.

Is there one federal break schedule for every live-event call?

No. Federal law does not require a universal lunch or coffee-break schedule. State law, agreements, and employer policies can add requirements.

What should you do when a call's attire or PPE requirement is unclear?

Ask the employer or lead before the task or call instead of guessing from another workplace.

Key Takeaways

  • Separate production attire from protective equipment.
  • Required PPE follows the hazard assessment and controlling workplace rules.
  • Noise protection depends on exposure and the employer's hearing-conservation controls, not a simple “show versus load-in” rule.
  • Break and meal rules vary; do not teach one local schedule as federal law.
  • Prepared hands read the call, confirm unclear requirements, and stay inside the task they were assigned and trained to perform.
