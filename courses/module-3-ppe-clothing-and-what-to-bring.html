Module 3

PPE, Clothing, and What to Bring

A lot of new hands lose their first call before the work even starts — not because they can't do the job, but because they showed up dressed or packed wrong. This lesson fixes that.

Show Blacks PPE Categories What to Bring Long-Call Readiness Day One

Show Blacks and Why They Matter

All-black clothing isn't a style choice on a live event call — it's the default uniform, and there's a real reason for it.

Crew stays in black so they disappear into the background once the show starts. The audience's eye is supposed to go to the performance, not to the people moving cases or adjusting a mic stand at the edge of the stage. It also puts everyone on a crew who's never met before in the same uniform, which reads as organized and professional before anyone says a word.

This doesn't need to be expensive. A plain black shirt and pants from a thrift store does the same job as anything from a work-wear brand — nobody's checking labels.

What counts as show blacks

A black top, black pants, and closed-toe shoes — no visible logos, graphics, or bright colors. If a call has different expectations, it'll be on the call sheet; black is the safe default when it isn't specified.

PPE: What It Is and When You Need It

Personal protective equipment isn't one thing — it's a category, and which pieces you need depends on the call and the task.

PPEProtects AgainstWhen You'll Need It
Work glovesCuts, blisters, pinch pointsCable, cases, rigging hardware — day one, every call
Hard hatHead impact, overhead hazardsRigging zones, overhead work, some load-ins
High-vis vestBeing seen by vehicle and forklift operatorsRequired at almost every call during load-in and load-out — comes off for show call, when blending in matters more than being seen
Eye protectionFlying debris, dustSpecific power-tool or rigging-hardware tasks, only when directed — not something most beginners need to carry
Hearing protectionLong-term hearing damage from sustained loud exposureShow volume, generators — not load-in or load-out, where you need to hear calls and equipment moving around you more than you need to block noise out

As covered in Module 2, if a task calls for PPE beyond your own basics, providing it is generally on the employer — not something you're required to already own. That said, not every employer actually follows through, and you won't know which kind of call you've got until you're there. The stronger habit, and what a lot of experienced hands actually do: own your own basic PPE and tools before your first call, and just bring all of it every time, even when you don't expect to need it. It costs you a little bag space. Not having it when it turns out you do need it costs you a lot more.

What to Bring, and What to Leave Home

The full packing list — and where to find everything cheap — already lives in the Resource Hub. This is the part that list doesn't cover: what actually costs you on a call if you bring it.

Leave expensive jewelry, watches, and anything you can't afford to lose track of at home — you won't have a safe place to store it, and you'll be moving gear all day. Skip the oversized bag; you need your hands free, not a backpack the size of a suitcase getting in everyone's way. And don't bring a blade-style tool until you know a specific venue allows it — plenty of security screenings turn them away at the door.

For the full bring-list — boots, blacks, gloves, a wrench, where to buy it cheap — see Gear & Tools in the Resource Hub.

Long-Call Readiness: Hydration, Food, Sleep, and Getting There

Calls can run a lot longer than a typical shift. Being ready for that is part of showing up prepared, not an afterthought.

Eat before you go — breaks aren't guaranteed on every call, especially early on. Bring water even if the venue provides some; don't count on it. Sleep before an early call sounds obvious until you're the person who took the call time lightly and is running on no rest by hour twelve.

Line up reliable transportation before you commit to a call, not the morning of. Being late because a ride fell through isn't an excuse a lead remembers kindly — and arrive early enough to park, check in, and find your lead before call time, not exactly at it.

Some calls run long by design — a bundled show call can mean 18–20 hours on the clock in one day. See What a Work Week Can Actually Look Like in the Resource Hub to see what that actually looks like before you're in the middle of one.

How to Avoid Looking Unprepared on Day One

Everything above adds up to one thing: don't be the hand a lead has to solve a problem for before the work even starts.

Good Example

A new hand arrives 15 minutes early, already in show blacks, bag packed with gloves and water, and checks in calmly.

Good Example

A new hand brought their own gloves and a full water bottle, so they're not the one asking to borrow from someone else on their first call.

Bad Example

A new hand shows up in a graphic t-shirt and sneakers, and a lead has to pull them aside or send them home before the day even starts.

Quick Knowledge Check

Click a question to check your answer.

Why does most crew wear all black?

So the crew disappears into the background during the show and the audience's attention stays on the performance — it also reads as organized and professional across a crew that's never met.

Do you need to own a hard hat before your first call?

Not necessarily — ask what a specific task actually requires. Beyond your own basics like gloves, required PPE is generally on the employer to provide.

Name one thing you should leave at home instead of bringing to a call.

Any of: expensive jewelry or valuables you can't secure, an oversized bag, or a blade-style tool at a venue that hasn't cleared it.

Why should you eat before you leave for a call?

Breaks aren't guaranteed on every call, especially early on — you can't count on getting food during the shift.

What's the fastest way to look unprepared on day one?

Showing up in the wrong clothing (not show blacks) or without basics like gloves and water, forcing a lead to solve a problem for you before the work even starts.

Key Takeaways

  • Show blacks are the default uniform because they keep crew out of the audience's eye and put everyone in the same look on sight.
  • PPE isn't one thing — know which category a task calls for, and don't assume you need to own it all before day one.
  • What you leave home matters as much as what you bring: valuables, oversized bags, and unapproved tools are liabilities, not gear.
  • Long calls are common in this work — eat, hydrate, sleep, and arrange transportation like the call could run longer than expected, because it might.
  • Looking prepared on day one is mostly about the basics: right clothing, right bag, arrive early. It's the fastest way to not need a lead's help before you've even started.
