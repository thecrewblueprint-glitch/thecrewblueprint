Video Pathway · Course 1

Supporting an LED Video Wall Build

LED walls are some of the most fragile, expensive gear on a call. This course starts the Video pathway by teaching you to protect that gear while helping build it — not to become a video engineer overnight.

What You Can Help With Don't Memorize the Wall What Stays Off-Limits

What You Can Help With, Under Direction

Real ground-hand support on an LED wall build is physical logistics and repeatable mechanical assistance — genuinely useful, and genuinely safe when done right.

You can move cases and dollies into position, carry cabinets using the product's actual handles, hold a cabinet steady while the lead engages a lock, repeat a locking step once someone's shown you how, and move cable and connectors under direction. During strike, each cabinet goes back to the exact case, slot, and orientation it came from — not an ad-hoc pile.

Don't Memorize the Wall — Learn the Pattern

This is the single most important idea in this course.

Different LED brands lock, connect, and store completely differently

There's no one universal method, and assuming a wall works like the last one you built is exactly how gear gets damaged. Learn the system in front of you: how it locks, what connector it uses, how it's cased. The transferable skill is protecting the equipment and following the current lead's procedure — not memorizing one brand.

This applies to handling, too. "Carry it by the edges" isn't a safe universal rule — edges can hold alignment hardware, curve mechanisms, and locking hardware that are easy to damage. Use the handles the product is actually built with.

What Stays Off-Limits Without Direction

The face of the wall is the one thing you never touch, no matter how confident you are with the rest of the build.

Never touch, press, or lean on the LED face itself — the pixels are delicate and on some panels are static-sensitive. Processor configuration, calibration, receiving-card work, and troubleshooting all stay with the video/LED lead. If a panel looks dark, damaged, or wrong, report it — don't try to diagnose or fix it yourself.

Common ways LED gear gets damaged by ground hands: touching or pressing the LED face, letting magnet-assisted panels snap together uncontrolled instead of guiding them in deliberately, forcing a lock before panels are properly aligned, forcing a connector that doesn't seat normally, and losing control of a cabinet's weight during a carry or strike.

Quick Knowledge Check

Click a question to check your answer.

You've built one brand of LED wall before. Does that mean you know how the next one locks and cases?

No — different LED brands lock, connect, and store completely differently. Learn the system in front of you instead of assuming it matches the last one.

Can you touch the LED face directly, carefully, while positioning a cabinet?

No — never touch, press, or lean on the LED face. Use the product's actual handles instead.

An LED panel looks dark or damaged mid-build. What do you do?

Report it to the video/LED lead. Diagnosing or fixing it yourself is outside a ground hand's role.

Two panels are approaching each other with magnet-assisted alignment. What's the right technique?

Guide them together deliberately — don't let them snap together uncontrolled, which can damage the LEDs on impact.

Where does each cabinet go during strike?

Back into the exact case, slot, and orientation it came from — not an improvised stack.

Key Takeaways

  • Real LED wall support: moving cases, carrying by intended handles, holding cabinets while locks engage, cable/connector discipline, correct strike/casing.
  • Different brands lock, connect, and store completely differently — learn the current system, don't assume it matches the last one.
  • Never touch the LED face. Processor configuration, calibration, and troubleshooting stay with the video/LED lead.
  • Most damage comes from handling mistakes: uncontrolled magnet snaps, forced locks, forced connectors, losing control of cabinet weight.
