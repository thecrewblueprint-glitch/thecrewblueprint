Getting Ready

Gear & Tools

Nobody hands you a packing list on day one. This is what actually gets used on a call, why it matters, and where to find it without overspending before you've even worked your first job.

Bring This on Day One

Show up without these and you're either borrowing from someone else, or standing around unable to help.

Closed-toe boots or shoes

Most venues won't let you on the floor without them — dropped cases and gear happen. Sturdy is more important than expensive at first.

All-black clothing

The default attire for most calls. You'll fade into the background during a show instead of standing out in the wrong way.

Work gloves

Cases, cable, and rigging hardware chew up bare hands fast. A basic pair saves you from blisters and cuts on your first load-in.

A basic multitool

Cutting zip ties, tightening a loose screw, opening a case — small fixes come up constantly, and having your own means you're not always asking to borrow one.

An adjustable (crescent) wrench

Truss bolts, case hardware, road boxes — it comes up constantly once you're near rigging or setup work. Most experienced hands never leave it at home.

Earplugs

Load-in noise and show volume add up over a career. Cheap, reusable earplugs are the easiest habit to start on day one.

A bag or backpack

Somewhere to keep all of this so your hands are free to actually work.

Worth Adding as You Keep Working

Not day-one essentials, but the stuff that makes longer calls and touring life easier.

Reusable water bottle

Outdoor festival calls in summer heat are brutal. Staying hydrated is a safety habit, not an optional one.

Sunscreen and a hat

Long outdoor calls add up over a season. Cheap insurance against a bad sunburn on day two of a multi-day festival.

Portable phone charger

Long calls drain your phone, and your phone is often how the PC reaches you. Don't be the person who goes dark mid-shift.

Cash

For parking, or meals on calls that don't provide a meal card. Not every venue takes card for incidentals.

A tool belt or pouch

Once you're carrying more than a multitool, a belt keeps everything on you instead of buried in a bag across the room.

A flashlight or headlamp

Most calls happen in lit spaces, but an early load-in, a dark truck, or an overnight outdoor call can still catch you without light. Worth having once you know your calls run that way.

A note on knives and blade tools

Bringing one usually isn't the obstacle people expect, but security screening varies by venue and festival and doesn't clear the same thing every time — keep some awareness of it rather than assuming it's always fine.

Where to Find It

You don't need specialty gear to start. Most of this is available cheap, and secondhand is completely fine for your first few calls.

Hardware & Home Stores

Home Depot, Lowe's, Harbor Freight — budget-friendly multitools, adjustable wrenches, work gloves, and flashlights. Harbor Freight in particular is a common starting point for cheap basics.

Work-Wear Retailers

Carhartt, Dickies, Duluth Trading — durable black clothing and boots built to actually hold up to physical work, not just look the part.

Outdoor & Sporting Goods

REI, Academy Sports, or similar — headlamps, reusable water bottles, and sun protection gear built for long days outside.

Thrift & Secondhand Stores

The cheapest way to build an all-black wardrobe fast. Nobody needs to know your black shirt came from Goodwill.
