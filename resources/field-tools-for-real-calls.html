Common Hand Signals

Calls get loud, and distances get long. These are the basics used across crews to communicate without shouting — site-specific direction always overrides what's listed here.

Stop / Hold

A closed fist raised overhead. Means stop what you're doing immediately — used most often around moving loads or rigging.

All Clear / Go Ahead

A raised open palm, or a thumbs-up depending on the crew. Confirms it's safe to proceed with the next step.

Come Here

A waving motion toward yourself, usually paired with eye contact first so the other person knows it's meant for them.

Cut / Kill It

A flat hand drawn across the throat. Used to mean stop or shut off — power, audio, a motor, whatever's running.

Up / Down

A pointed finger raised or lowered, often circled for continuous movement. Common around lifts, trusses, and rigging points.

Radio / Talk to Me

A hand shaped like a phone near the ear, or a point at a radio. Means switch to verbal communication — the signal alone isn't enough right now.

These aren't universal

Every crew and every department can vary slightly. On an unfamiliar call, especially anything involving rigging or moving loads, confirm signals with your lead before you rely on them.
