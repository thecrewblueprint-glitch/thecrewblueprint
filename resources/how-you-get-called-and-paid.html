The Call Sheet

Quick Facts

  • Date/venue — full address if it's new, so you're not pulling up a maps app in the parking lot.
  • Crew call — not the same as show time or doors; show up at show time and you're already late.
  • Sign-in / PC — who marks you present. Can't find them, and as far as the job's concerned, you're not there.
  • Parking — it's on there for a reason; guess wrong and you're towed or blocking a truck route.
  • Attire — usually black, closed-toe shoes; show up wrong and you may get turned away at the door.
  • Emergency contact — separate from the PC, for when it's actually serious.
  • Not the sign-in sheet — that's a separate document you sign on-site. Mix them up and your hours may not get counted right.

The Sign-In Sheet

A different document from the call sheet, filled out once you're actually on-site. This is what determines your hours — get on the call sheet, then sign this to prove you showed up.

How Calls Work

Touring & Festival

  • One call sheet covers the day, or the whole run — check it once, not every morning.
  • Sign in once with the PC; that's your confirmation, nothing else to do.
  • On the sheet = expected. Not on it = don't assume you're working.

Local & Staffing Agency

  • Calls come one at a time, by text or email — easy to miss if you're not checking.
  • You confirm each one yourself, usually in an app. Getting the notice isn't the same as being booked.
  • Hours tracking varies by company — app or paper. Assume wrong, and your hours might not get logged at all.
