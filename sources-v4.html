Sources & evidence

How the learning material is grounded.

Crew Blueprint separates instructional evidence from product policy. Regulations, consensus standards, manufacturer documentation, technical references, and practitioner knowledge do not all carry the same authority, so the curriculum records where a claim comes from and what limits apply to it.

Source model

Different evidence answers different questions.

A source is used only for claims it can reasonably support. A regulation is not treated like a field preference, one manufacturer's procedure is not made universal, and practitioner framing is not presented as law.

Regulatory sources

Applicable OSHA and other government material may support hazard-recognition, employer-duty, worker-rights, or regulatory-context claims. Jurisdiction and applicability still matter.

Consensus standards

Applicable industry standards may inform terminology, design boundaries, and accepted technical practice. Copyrighted standards are referenced rather than reproduced.

Manufacturer documentation

Product-specific instructions remain product-specific. A manual can support information about that product without becoming a universal procedure for unrelated systems.

Technical and educational references

Authoritative technical or educational material may support system concepts, terminology, and transferable mental models when it matches the scope being taught.

Practitioner knowledge

Field-practice guidance may be used when appropriate, but it is labeled and bounded rather than presented as regulation, certification, or manufacturer instruction.

Crew Blueprint policy

Some boundaries are intentionally stricter product rules—for example, separating online education from jobsite authorization. Those rules are identified as Crew Blueprint policy instead of being misrepresented as universal law.

Evidence boundaries

What a source does not do.

It does not authorize work

Reading a source or completing a course does not create employer appointment, qualification, certification, licensing, or permission to perform a controlled task.

It does not erase local procedure

Current employer, venue, manufacturer, engineered-plan, union, credential, or jurisdictional requirements continue to control where applicable.

It does not make every statement universal

Applicability, edition, jurisdiction, equipment model, evidence strength, and review status are considered before a claim is presented broadly.

Controlling rule: evidence can support what the curriculum teaches, but it does not convert online education into jobsite authority. When real conditions, procedures, or responsibilities differ from an example, the controlling work process and responsible authority take precedence.
