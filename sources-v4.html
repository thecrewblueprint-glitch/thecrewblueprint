Research chooses the problem. Evidence teaches the lesson.

How the curriculum is grounded.

Current job-market research helps identify the competencies workers are repeatedly expected to bring to the job. Roadmapdev reduces that evidence into non-identifying competency-demand signals. Crew Blueprint then teaches those competencies using appropriate safety, regulatory, manufacturer, technical, educational, and practitioner sources.

OSHA · Safety-management training

OSHA guidance emphasizes worker training in hazard recognition, controls, safe work practices, reporting, and role-specific responsibilities. That supports Crew Blueprint’s focus on recognizing hazards and escalating instead of improvising.

OSHA · Worker participation

OSHA’s safety-management guidance supports worker reporting of hazards, injuries, illnesses, and near misses and participation in safety processes without retaliation.

OSHA · Walking-working surfaces

29 CFR 1910.22 supports the course emphasis on orderly work areas, safe access and egress, hazard correction, and walking-working-surface conditions.

Open OSHA 1910.22 ↗

OSHA · Personal protective equipment

29 CFR 1910.132 addresses hazard assessment, PPE selection, training, limitations, care, and employer responsibilities. Crew Blueprint treats PPE as protection, never as task authorization.

OSHA · Materials handling and storage

29 CFR 1910.176 supports the emphasis on clear aisles, safe storage, housekeeping, and materials-flow awareness.

ANSI / ESTA consensus standards

ESTA’s Technical Standards Program is the ANSI-accredited standards program dedicated to entertainment technology. Applicable standards may inform research and boundary-setting, but copyrighted standards are referenced rather than reproduced.

Open ESTA Technical Standards Program ↗

Manufacturer documentation

Product-specific instructions stay product-specific. Crew Blueprint does not turn one manufacturer’s procedure into a universal rule, and employer/site procedure remains controlling where applicable.

Practitioner knowledge

Field-practice guidance is used only when appropriate and is labeled as practitioner or instructional framing rather than presented as law, regulation, manufacturer instruction, or consensus standard.

The research bridge

What market evidence is allowed to do.

It may identify recurring competencies

Public employment and industry evidence may show that communication, equipment handling, documentation, troubleshooting, logistics, department workflow, leadership, and safety boundaries recur across many roles. Roadmapdev may normalize those patterns into competency-demand signals.

It may shape curriculum priority

Those signals may influence which competencies receive a course, how much depth they need, and which advanced pathways deserve development. They are planning evidence, not lesson claims.

It may not become raw lesson content

Employer lists, vacancies, pay, market rankings, labor routes, worker reviews, private buyer information, and current opportunity data stay outside the lesson body. Learners go to Production Atlas for current work intelligence.

Hard firewall

What never crosses into learner content.

Deadhang commercial intelligence

Pricing, margins, client/vendor strategy, procurement, insurance strategy, market strategy, sourcing methods, and internal operations do not enter Crew Blueprint curriculum or learner examples.

Raw Atlas employment records

Production Atlas owns current employers, openings, pay evidence, markets, events, labor routes, organizations, and public application paths. Crew Blueprint links to Atlas rather than duplicating those records.

Mixed Roadmapdev packages

Roadmapdev may support curriculum only after educational evidence and normalized competency signals are separated from raw commercial or employment payloads. Provenance stays available privately.

Controlling rule: job-market research can answer what is worth teaching. It cannot by itself answer how a learner should perform a task. Instructional claims must be supported by the appropriate educational or technical authority, and controlled work remains subject to employer, site, regulatory, manufacturer, credential, and qualified-person requirements.
